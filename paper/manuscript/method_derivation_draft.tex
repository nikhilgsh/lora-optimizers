\title{PoLoRA from a per-factor gradient-covariance metric\\[2pt]
\large (derivation draft --- for discussion, not for \texttt{main.tex})}
\author{}
\date{}

\maketitle

This note derives the PoLoRA update direction. The method measures a dense layer
update against \emph{sample pseudo-gradients}---the rank-one matrices $bx^\top$ a
single training sample produces, from one input $x$ and one output sensitivity
$b$. It takes the worst case over these, which reduces to a spectral norm, then
pulls that constraint back to the LoRA factors.

The pseudo-gradients are measured in the fitted gradient metric, the Kronecker
fit $\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$ that KL-Shampoo already maintains. The worst
response of an update to a unit-size sample pseudo-gradient is a spectral norm.
Capping it by a step budget gives the trust-region step solved in
\S\ref{sec:metric}.

The remaining sections are algebra:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \S\ref{sec:setup} sets up the first-order LoRA tangent
    $T=B\Delta A+\Delta B A$.
  \item \S\ref{sec:pullback} pulls the spectral constraint back to the factors
    and splits it into two single-factor blocks.
  \item \S\ref{sec:solve} solves each block by whitening, the polar map, and
    unwhitening.
  \item \S\ref{sec:radius} fixes the scalar step lengths by a product-radius
    rule.
  \item \S\ref{sec:est} estimates the metric factors $P,Q$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[Metric only, no probabilistic model]
The derivation uses the covariance model only as a metric. Adding a Gaussian or
elliptical model for vectorized layer gradients makes the same Mahalanobis
constraint a fixed-radius density contour, but the algebra does not need that
probabilistic assumption. Appendix~\ref{app:frob} gives the dense Frobenius
analogue.
\end{remark}

\section{Setup: the LoRA tangent split}
\label{sec:setup}

A LoRA layer adds the rank-$r$ adapter
$W=BA\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ to a frozen weight, with
$A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and
$B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. One optimizer step increments the factors by
$\Delta A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and
$\Delta B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. The exact displacement is
$B\,\Delta A+\Delta B\,A+\Delta B\,\Delta A$. We keep the first-order tangent
\begin{equation}
  T:=B\,\Delta A+\Delta B\,A,
  \label{eq:tangent}
\end{equation}
dropping the second-order term $\Delta B\,\Delta A$.
Let $G=\nabla_W\mathcal L\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be the
gradient with respect to $W$. The first-order loss change splits across the two
factors,
\begin{equation}
  \langle G,T\rangle
  =\langle B^\top G,\Delta A\rangle
  +\langle G A^\top,\Delta B\rangle ,
  \qquad
  G_A:=B^\top G\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}},\quad
  G_B:=G A^\top\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r} .
  \label{eq:split-linear}
\end{equation}
The derivation uses raw gradients. In the optimizer, $G_A,G_B$ are replaced by
bias-corrected momentum buffers before the polar map.

\section{Covariance-normalized pseudo-gradients}
\label{sec:metric}

Let $U\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be a dense update. A single
sample or token gives a rank-one layer gradient $bx^\top$, with $x$ the layer
input and $b$ the backpropagated output sensitivity. We test $U$ against such
sample pseudo-gradients, with first-order interaction
\begin{equation}
  \langle U,bx^\top\rangle=b^\top Ux .
  \label{eq:rank-one-interaction}
\end{equation}

The pseudo-gradients are measured in the fitted gradient metric. With $\Sigma$
the second moment of vectorized layer gradients, any matrix $R$ has
inverse-covariance size
\begin{equation}
  \norm{R}_{\Sigma^{-1}}^2
  :=\vecop(R)^\top\Sigma^{-1}\vecop(R).
  \label{eq:maha-size}
\end{equation}
The worst interaction of $U$ with a unit-size sample pseudo-gradient is then
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{\Sigma}(U)
  :=\sup_{\substack{b,x\\
      \vecop(bx^\top)^\top\Sigma^{-1}\vecop(bx^\top)\le1}}
      |b^\top Ux| .
  \label{eq:rank-one-bound-def}
\end{equation}

PoLoRA never forms $\Sigma$. It uses the same diagonal--Kronecker fit as the
KL-Shampoo metric estimator,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma\approx Q\otimes P,
  \label{eq:PQ-approx}
\end{equation}
with $P\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ the output-side factor and
$Q\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ the input-side factor. These
factor the full second moment and need not be the separate covariances of $b$ and
$x$. When $P,Q$ are positive definite, the inverse-covariance size of $bx^\top$
factors,
\begin{equation}
  \vecop(bx^\top)^\top (Q^{-1}\otimes P^{-1}) \vecop(bx^\top)
  =(b^\top P^{-1}b)(x^\top Q^{-1}x).
  \label{eq:kron-rank-one}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{P,Q}(U)
  =\sup_{(b^\top P^{-1}b)(x^\top Q^{-1}x)\le1}|b^\top Ux|
  =\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2 .
  \label{eq:kron-spectral}
\end{equation}
The last equality substitutes $b=P^{1/2}a$ and $x=Q^{1/2}z$. Bilinearity makes
the supremum over $\norm{a}_2\norm{z}_2\le1$ equal the supremum over
$\norm{a}_2\le1$ and $\norm{z}_2\le1$.

The objective and the constraint are both linearized loss changes. By
\eqref{eq:rank-one-interaction}, $\langle U,bx^\top\rangle$ is the first-order loss
change $U$ induces under a single pseudo-gradient, so $\Gamma_{P,Q}(U)$ is the
largest such change over unit-size pseudo-gradients. PoLoRA minimizes the loss
change $\langle G,U\rangle$ under the true gradient, subject to this worst-case
per-sample change being at most $\eta$:
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\langle G,U\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2\le\eta .
  \label{eq:kron-trust-region}
\end{equation}

\section{Pullback to the LoRA factors}
\label{sec:pullback}

For LoRA the dense update in \eqref{eq:kron-trust-region} is the tangent
$T=B\Delta A+\Delta B A$ from \eqref{eq:tangent}. Substituting this tangent and
using the split \eqref{eq:split-linear} gives the joint problem
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta A,\Delta B}\;
  \langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle+\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_2\le\eta .
  \label{eq:rank-one-lora}
\end{equation}
The constraint couples $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$. PoLoRA splits it into two
blocks, one per factor:
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2
  =\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2,
  \qquad C_A:=B^\top P B\in\R^{r\times r},
  \label{eq:A-rank-one-fit}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,A\,Q^{1/2}}_2
  =\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_2,
  \qquad C_B:=AQA^\top\in\R^{r\times r},
  \label{eq:B-rank-one-fit}
\end{equation}
These hold when the partner factor has full column rank: writing
$P^{1/2}B=V C_A^{1/2}$ with $V^\top V=I$, the orthogonal $V$ leaves the operator
norm unchanged. The $r\times r$ matrices $C_A,C_B$ are the metrics the fitted
geometry induces on each factor; equivalently $Q\otimes C_A$ and $C_B\otimes P$
fit the per-factor gradient covariances.

\section{Solving by whitening and polar projection}
\label{sec:solve}

The split gives two independent subproblems, one per factor:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta A}\;\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2\le\tau_A,
  \label{eq:A-spectral-block}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta B}\;\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_2\le\tau_B .
  \label{eq:B-spectral-block}
\end{equation}

Here $\polar(M)$ is the orthogonal polar factor of $M$: the nearest orthogonal
matrix, keeping $M$'s singular vectors and setting all singular values to one.

\begin{proposition}[Per-factor spectral subproblems]\label{prop:channels}
Assume $P,Q,C_A,C_B$ positive definite, and set
\[
  H_A:=C_A^{-1/2}G_AQ^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  H_B:=P^{-1/2}G_BC_B^{-1/2}.
\]
Canonical minimizers of \eqref{eq:A-spectral-block}--\eqref{eq:B-spectral-block} are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A(\tau_A)
  =-\tau_A\,C_A^{-1/2}\polar(H_A)Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \Delta B(\tau_B)
  =-\tau_B\,P^{-1/2}\polar(H_B)C_B^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:spectral-solutions}
\end{equation}
If a whitened gradient is zero, any feasible update with zero objective is a
minimizer; the zero update is the canonical choice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $Y_A=C_A^{1/2}\Delta A Q^{1/2}$,
$\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle=\langle H_A,Y_A\rangle$ and the constraint is
$\norm{Y_A}_2\le\tau_A$. Spectral/nuclear duality gives
$\min_{\norm{Y_A}_2\le\tau_A}\langle H_A,Y_A\rangle=-\tau_A\norm{H_A}_*$,
attained at $Y_A=-\tau_A\polar(H_A)$. Undoing the change of variables gives
\eqref{eq:spectral-solutions}; the $\Delta B$ case is identical with
$Y_B=P^{1/2}\Delta B C_B^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

Define the unscaled update directions
\begin{equation}
  W_A:=C_A^{-1/2}\polar(H_A)Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  W_B:=P^{-1/2}\polar(H_B)C_B^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:WA}
\end{equation}

\section{Product-radius calibration}
\label{sec:radius}

The budgets $\tau_A,\tau_B$ set only the lengths of the fixed directions
$W_A,W_B$. A single budget on the tangent does not pin both lengths: with
$\norm{\Delta A}_2=\rho_A$ and $\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho_B$,
$\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2\le\norm{B}_2\rho_A+\norm{A}_2\rho_B$, which many
$(\rho_A,\rho_B)$ satisfy. PoLoRA breaks the tie symmetrically, giving both
factors the same radius,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\Delta A}_2=\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho .
  \label{eq:equal-radius}
\end{equation}
The tangent then obeys
$\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2\le\rho(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$. Setting this bound
to $\eta$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \rho=\frac{\eta}{\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2}.
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Scaling the directions $W_A,W_B$ to this radius gives the PoLoRA step
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A=-\rho\,\frac{W_A}{\norm{W_A}_2},
  \qquad
  \Delta B=-\rho\,\frac{W_B}{\norm{W_B}_2}
  \label{eq:final-step}
\end{equation}
for nonzero $W_A,W_B$.

\section{Estimating the diagonal metric}
\label{sec:est}

It remains to estimate $P,Q$. PoLoRA uses two moment identities of the Kronecker
fit \eqref{eq:PQ-approx}, with expectations over per-example gradients and any
compatible $M,N$:
\begin{equation}
  \E[G M G^\top]=\tr(MQ)P,
  \qquad
  \E[G^\top N G]=\tr(NP)Q .
  \label{eq:moment-identities}
\end{equation}
The factor gradients are linear images of $G$, so their second moments carry the
same $r\times r$ matrices $C_A,C_B$ that appear in the update:
$\E[\vecop(G_A)\vecop(G_A)^\top]\approx Q\otimes C_A$ and
$\E[\vecop(G_B)\vecop(G_B)^\top]\approx C_B\otimes P$.

\begin{lemma}[Moment closure under a Kronecker gradient-covariance model]
\label{lem:estimator}
If $\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$ with $C_A=B^\top P B$ and
$C_B=AQA^\top$, then
\begin{equation}
  \E\!\left[\tfrac1r G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A\right]=Q,
  \qquad
  \E\!\left[\tfrac1r G_B C_B^{-1}G_B^\top\right]=P .
  \label{eq:unbiased}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the second identity of \eqref{eq:moment-identities} with $N=B C_A^{-1}B^\top$:
$G^\top N G=G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A$ and
$\tr(NP)=\tr(C_A^{-1}B^\top P B)=\tr(I_r)=r$, giving the first identity. The
second follows with $M=A^\top C_B^{-1}A$.
\end{proof}

PoLoRA keeps only the diagonals and updates them by EMA:
\begin{equation}
  q_j\leftarrow
  \mathrm{EMA}\!\left[\tfrac1r (G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}\right],
  \qquad
  p_i\leftarrow
  \mathrm{EMA}\!\left[\tfrac1r (G_B)_{i,:}C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top\right].
  \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
Because $C_A$ depends on the current $P$ and $C_B$ on the current $Q$, the two
estimates are coupled. The optimizer takes one EMA alternation per step, then
freezes $P,Q$ while computing \eqref{eq:WA}--\eqref{eq:final-step}. In the
implementation the inverse square roots are damped, while the formulas above are
written for the ideal positive-definite case.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

PoLoRA measures pseudo-gradients by their inverse-covariance size under the
fitted gradient second moment. With the KL-Shampoo fit
$\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$, the worst unit-size pseudo-gradient interaction is
$\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2$. Pulling that constraint back to the LoRA factors gives
$C_A=B^\top PB$ and $C_B=AQA^\top$. The per-factor solutions are the polar
directions \eqref{eq:WA}, and the common product radius \eqref{eq:rho} fixes the
scalar lengths, giving the implemented step \eqref{eq:final-step}.

\section{Dense covariance ellipsoid and the Frobenius alternative}
\label{app:frob}

A sample pseudo-gradient $bx^\top$ is rank one, so the main text pairs $U$ only
with rank-one matrices. Relaxing to an arbitrary dense matrix $R$ (same
inverse-covariance bound) gives the worst-case interaction
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\Gamma_{\Sigma}(U)
  :=\sup_{\vecop(R)^\top\Sigma^{-1}\vecop(R)\le1} |\langle U,R\rangle| .
  \label{eq:dense-probe-interaction}
\end{equation}
Under the Kronecker fit $\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$,
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde\Gamma_{P,Q}(U)
  =\sqrt{\vecop(U)^\top(Q\otimes P)\vecop(U)}
  =\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_F .
  \label{eq:full-ball-frob}
\end{equation}
Thus the dense-matrix analogue of \eqref{eq:kron-trust-region} is
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\langle G,U\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_F\le\eta .
  \label{eq:frob-trust-region}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Dense Frobenius solution]\label{lem:frob}
Let $H:=P^{-1/2}GQ^{-1/2}$. If $H\ne0$, a minimizer of
\eqref{eq:frob-trust-region} is
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{frob}}
  =-\eta\,\frac{P^{-1}GQ^{-1}}{\norm{P^{-1/2}GQ^{-1/2}}_F}.
  \label{eq:frob-dense-solution}
\end{equation}
If $H=0$, the zero update is a minimizer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $Y=P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}$. Then
$\langle G,U\rangle=\langle H,Y\rangle$ and the constraint is
$\norm{Y}_F\le\eta$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives the minimum at
$Y=-\eta H/\norm{H}_F$. Undoing the change of variables gives
\eqref{eq:frob-dense-solution}.
\end{proof}

For LoRA, substitute the tangent $T=B\Delta A+\Delta B A$ into
\eqref{eq:frob-trust-region}. The same block split as \S\ref{sec:pullback}
replaces the spectral constraints by Frobenius constraints,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F
  =\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F,
  \qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,A\,Q^{1/2}}_F
  =\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_F.
  \label{eq:frob-factor-blocks}
\end{equation}
The per-factor Frobenius subproblems are
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta A}\;\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F\le\tau_A,
  \label{eq:A-frob-block}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta B}\;\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_F\le\tau_B .
  \label{eq:B-frob-block}
\end{equation}
With $H_A=C_A^{-1/2}G_AQ^{-1/2}$ and
$H_B=P^{-1/2}G_BC_B^{-1/2}$, their nonzero-gradient solutions are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A_{\mathrm{frob}}
  =-\tau_A\,\frac{C_A^{-1}G_AQ^{-1}}{\norm{H_A}_F},
  \qquad
  \Delta B_{\mathrm{frob}}
  =-\tau_B\,\frac{P^{-1}G_BC_B^{-1}}{\norm{H_B}_F}.
  \label{eq:frob-factor-solutions}
\end{equation}
The difference is now localized: arbitrary dense matrices give the
Frobenius-normalized inverse direction \eqref{eq:frob-factor-solutions}, sample
pseudo-gradients the spectral polar direction \eqref{eq:spectral-solutions}.
