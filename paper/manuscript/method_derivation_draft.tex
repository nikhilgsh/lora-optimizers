\title{PoLoRA from a per-factor gradient-covariance metric\\[2pt]
\large (derivation draft --- for discussion, not for \texttt{main.tex})}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

This note derives the PoLoRA direction from a dense linearized trust-region
problem and then pulls the result back to the LoRA factors. For a dense update
$U$ and the gradient $G_i$ on example $i$, the first-order change in that
example's loss is $\delta_i(U)=\langle G_i,U\rangle$. The trust region bounds the
root-mean-square (RMS) effect $\E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]$---equivalently, it bounds the
response to every unit-RMS weighted average of the observed per-example
gradients. PoLoRA replaces that full dual ball by the rank-one layer tests
$r=\vecop(bx^\top)$, fits the empirical gradient covariance by $Q\otimes P$, and
thereby obtains the spectral constraint $\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2$. The remaining
steps are algebra: apply this constraint to the LoRA tangent
$B\Delta A+\Delta B A$, split the two factor blocks, and solve the resulting
spectral linear subproblems by whitening, applying the polar map, and
unwhitening.

Throughout, the metric is built from observed per-example gradients only.
Appendix~\ref{app:frob} repeats the same path but stops short of the rank-one
restriction, recovering a Frobenius (full-inverse) step in place of the polar
one.

\section{Setup: the LoRA tangent split}
\label{sec:setup}

A LoRA layer adds the rank-$r$ adapter
$W=BA\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ to a frozen weight, with
$A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and
$B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. One optimizer step increments the factors by
$\Delta A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and
$\Delta B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. The exact adapter displacement is
$\Delta W_{\mathrm{exact}}=B\,\Delta A+\Delta B\,A+\Delta B\,\Delta A$; the
derivation works in the first-order tangent model
\begin{equation}
  T:=B\,\Delta A+\Delta B\,A,
  \label{eq:tangent}
\end{equation}
discarding the second-order product term $\Delta B\,\Delta A$.
Let $G=\nabla_W\mathcal L\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be the
gradient with respect to $W$. The first-order loss change splits across the two
factors,
\begin{equation}
  \langle G,T\rangle
  =\langle B^\top G,\Delta A\rangle
  +\langle G A^\top,\Delta B\rangle ,
  \qquad
  G_A:=B^\top G\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}},\quad
  G_B:=G A^\top\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r} .
  \label{eq:split-linear}
\end{equation}
The derivation uses raw gradients; the optimizer replaces $G_A,G_B$ by
bias-corrected momentum buffers before the polar map.

\section{A per-example linearized trust-region step}
\label{sec:metric}

Let $G_i\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be the dense adapter
gradient on example or token $i$, let $g_i:=\vecop(G_i)$, and write
$u:=\vecop(U)$ for a dense update. The first-order change in example $i$'s loss
is
\begin{equation}
  \delta_i(U):=\langle G_i,U\rangle .
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
The per-example gradients average to the batch gradient of \S\ref{sec:setup},
$G=\E_i[G_i]$, so the mean first-order change is
$\E_i[\delta_i(U)]=\langle G,U\rangle$. The starting point is the constrained
linearized step
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\E_i[\delta_i(U)]
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]\le \eta^2 .
  \label{eq:rms-trust-region}
\end{equation}
It asks for a descent step whose per-example linearized effects have bounded RMS
size.

The RMS constraint is the empirical gradient second moment:
\begin{equation}
  \E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]
  =\E_i[(g_i^\top u)^2]
  =u^\top \Sigma u,
  \qquad
  \Sigma:=\E_i[g_i g_i^\top].
  \label{eq:ef-penalty}
\end{equation}
This is the empirical Fisher: the covariance of the observed per-example
gradients, used here because it is exactly the quadratic form that penalizes the
RMS first-order effect of $U$ across examples.

\begin{lemma}[Dual view of the RMS constraint]
\label{lem:rms-dual}
Define the full dual unit ball
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal D_{\Sigma}:=
  \{\,r\in\range(\Sigma): r^\top\Sigma^+r\le1\,\}.
  \label{eq:test-set}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\mathcal D_{\Sigma}$ is the set of gradients
$r=\E_i[\alpha_i g_i]$ that can be synthesized by weights with
$\E_i[\alpha_i^2]\le1$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \sqrt{\E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]}
  =\sup_{r\in\mathcal D_{\Sigma}} |r^\top u| .
  \label{eq:rms-dual}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The identity \eqref{eq:ef-penalty} gives
$\sqrt{\E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]}=\sqrt{u^\top\Sigma u}$. Generalized
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$|r^\top u|\le (r^\top\Sigma^+r)^{1/2}(u^\top\Sigma u)^{1/2}$ for
$r\in\range(\Sigma)$, and equality is attained by $r=\Sigma u$ after
normalizing when $\Sigma u\ne0$. For the weighting interpretation, consider the
linear map $\alpha\mapsto\E_i[\alpha_i g_i]$ from the example-weight Hilbert
space with norm $(\E_i[\alpha_i^2])^{1/2}$ into parameter space. Its range
covariance is $\Sigma$, so the minimum weight norm required to synthesize $r$ is
$(r^\top\Sigma^+r)^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

Thus the RMS constraint in \eqref{eq:rms-trust-region} has a dual reading: every
unit-RMS weighted average of per-example gradients has response at most $\eta$.
The full dual ball $\mathcal D_\Sigma$ is large; it contains arbitrary weighted
combinations of observed example gradients. PoLoRA's spectral step comes from
replacing this full set by a structured subset.

\section{Rank-one layer tests and the spectral norm}
\label{sec:rank-one}

A layerwise first-order increment has a special bilinear probe form. For a
backward vector $b\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and an input vector
$x\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$, the test gradient is $r=\vecop(bx^\top)$ and its
response is
\begin{equation}
  r^\top u=b^\top Ux .
  \label{eq:rank-one-response}
\end{equation}
Define the rank-one slice of the dual ball by restricting to these
layer-structured directions:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal R_{\Sigma}:=
  \{\,\vecop(bx^\top)\in\mathcal D_\Sigma:
        b\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}},\ x\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}\,\}.
  \label{eq:rank-one-slice}
\end{equation}
The associated rank-one response of $U$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{\Sigma}(U)
  :=\sup_{r\in\mathcal R_{\Sigma}} |r^\top u|
  =\sup_{\vecop(bx^\top)\in\mathcal D_\Sigma}|b^\top Ux| .
  \label{eq:rank-one-bound-def}
\end{equation}
Because $\mathcal R_{\Sigma}\subseteq\mathcal D_\Sigma$,
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{\Sigma}(U)
  \le \sqrt{u^\top\Sigma u}.
  \label{eq:rank-one-bound}
\end{equation}
This rank-one restriction is therefore not the full RMS constraint. It keeps only
the largest response generated by one backward direction paired with one forward
direction.

PoLoRA never forms $\Sigma$; it fits the covariance by a diagonal--Kronecker
matrix
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma\approx Q\otimes P,
  \label{eq:PQ-approx}
\end{equation}
with $P\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ the output-side factor and
$Q\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ the input-side factor. In the
ideal SPD case, the rank-one normalization becomes
\begin{equation}
  \vecop(bx^\top)^\top (Q^{-1}\otimes P^{-1}) \vecop(bx^\top)
  =(b^\top P^{-1}b)(x^\top Q^{-1}x).
  \label{eq:kron-rank-one}
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{P,Q}(U)
  =\sup_{(b^\top P^{-1}b)(x^\top Q^{-1}x)\le1}|b^\top Ux|
  =\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2 .
  \label{eq:kron-spectral}
\end{equation}
The last equality follows by substituting $b=P^{1/2}a$ and $x=Q^{1/2}z$; the
product constraint is equivalent to $\norm{a}_2\le1$ and $\norm{z}_2\le1$ because
the response is bilinear. Thus the rank-one response bound is exactly the
spectral norm of the whitened matrix.

Combining the pieces, the dense trust-region problem evolves in three steps. The
original RMS program \eqref{eq:rms-trust-region} is equivalently
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\langle G,U\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \sup_{r\in\mathcal D_\Sigma}|r^\top\vecop(U)|\le\eta .
  \label{eq:dual-trust-region}
\end{equation}
Restricting the tests to the rank-one slice gives
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\langle G,U\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \Gamma_{\Sigma}(U)\le\eta .
  \label{eq:rank-one-trust-region}
\end{equation}
Finally, the Kronecker fit $\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$ turns
\eqref{eq:rank-one-trust-region} into the spectral program
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\langle G,U\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2\le\eta .
  \label{eq:kron-trust-region}
\end{equation}

\section{Pullback to the LoRA factors}
\label{sec:pullback}

For LoRA the dense update in \eqref{eq:kron-trust-region} is the tangent
$T=B\Delta A+\Delta B A$ from \eqref{eq:tangent}. Substituting this tangent and
using the split \eqref{eq:split-linear} gives the joint problem
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta A,\Delta B}\;
  \langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle+\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_2\le\eta .
  \label{eq:rank-one-lora}
\end{equation}
The constraint couples the two factor increments. PoLoRA uses a block split,
treating the two tangent
contributions separately:
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2
  =\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2,
  \qquad C_A:=B^\top P B\in\R^{r\times r},
  \label{eq:A-rank-one-fit}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,A\,Q^{1/2}}_2
  =\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_2,
  \qquad C_B:=AQA^\top\in\R^{r\times r},
  \label{eq:B-rank-one-fit}
\end{equation}
assuming the partner factor has full rank in the corresponding metric. For
example, if $P^{1/2}B=V C_A^{1/2}$ with $V^\top V=I$, left multiplication by $V$
does not change the operator norm. Here $C_A,C_B$ are the sensitivity metrics
induced on the LoRA factor directions by the per-example gradient metric;
equivalently $Q\otimes C_A$ and $C_B\otimes P$ are the Kronecker fits to the
per-factor gradient covariances
$\E_i[\vecop(G_{A,i})\vecop(G_{A,i})^\top]$ and
$\E_i[\vecop(G_{B,i})\vecop(G_{B,i})^\top]$.

\section{Solving by whitening and polar projection}
\label{sec:solve}

The block split makes the two factor linear minimization problems independent:
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta A}\;\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2\le\tau_A,
  \label{eq:A-spectral-block}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta B}\;\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_2\le\tau_B .
  \label{eq:B-spectral-block}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Per-factor spectral subproblems]\label{prop:channels}
Assume $P,Q,C_A,C_B$ positive definite, and set
\[
  H_A:=C_A^{-1/2}G_AQ^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  H_B:=P^{-1/2}G_BC_B^{-1/2}.
\]
Canonical minimizers of \eqref{eq:A-spectral-block}--\eqref{eq:B-spectral-block} are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A(\tau_A)
  =-\tau_A\,C_A^{-1/2}\polar(H_A)Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \Delta B(\tau_B)
  =-\tau_B\,P^{-1/2}\polar(H_B)C_B^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:spectral-solutions}
\end{equation}
If a whitened gradient is zero, any feasible update with zero objective is a
minimizer; the zero update is the canonical choice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $Y_A=C_A^{1/2}\Delta A Q^{1/2}$,
$\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle=\langle H_A,Y_A\rangle$ and the constraint is
$\norm{Y_A}_2\le\tau_A$. Spectral/nuclear duality gives
$\min_{\norm{Y_A}_2\le\tau_A}\langle H_A,Y_A\rangle=-\tau_A\norm{H_A}_*$,
attained at $Y_A=-\tau_A\polar(H_A)$. Undoing the change of variables gives
\eqref{eq:spectral-solutions}; the $\Delta B$ case is identical with
$Y_B=P^{1/2}\Delta B C_B^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

With the Frobenius norm in place of the spectral norm, the same linear problem
gives the full-inverse directions $-C_A^{-1}G_AQ^{-1}$ and
$-P^{-1}G_BC_B^{-1}$ after normalization; the operator norm is the sole source of
the polar map
(Appendix~\ref{app:frob}). Define the unscaled update directions
\begin{equation}
  W_A:=C_A^{-1/2}\polar(H_A)Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  W_B:=P^{-1/2}\polar(H_B)C_B^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:WA}
\end{equation}

\section{Product-radius calibration}
\label{sec:radius}

The spectral budgets $\tau_A,\tau_B$ in \eqref{eq:spectral-solutions} control only
scalar lengths along the polar directions $W_A,W_B$. PoLoRA keeps those directions and
sets the scalar lengths by a separate product-radius rule. The product-radius
budget alone does not fix the two lengths: if
$\norm{\Delta A}_2=\rho_A$ and $\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho_B$, then
$\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2\le\norm{B}_2\rho_A+\norm{A}_2\rho_B$, and many
$(\rho_A,\rho_B)$ meet a given budget. PoLoRA removes this freedom by the
no-preference symmetry between the two factor coordinates---exchanging the blocks
should not change the radius allocation---which forces the common radius
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\Delta A}_2=\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho .
  \label{eq:equal-radius}
\end{equation}
The tangent update then obeys
$\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2\le\rho(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$; setting this equal
to the budget $\eta$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \rho=\frac{\eta}{\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2}.
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Normalizing the polar directions to this radius gives the PoLoRA step
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A=-\rho\,\frac{W_A}{\norm{W_A}_2},
  \qquad
  \Delta B=-\rho\,\frac{W_B}{\norm{W_B}_2}
  \label{eq:final-step}
\end{equation}
for nonzero $W_A,W_B$.

\section{Estimating the diagonal metric}
\label{sec:est}

It remains to estimate $P,Q$ in \eqref{eq:PQ-approx}. PoLoRA does so by a coupled
moment closure, with all expectations over the per-example gradient distribution.
The only algebraic consequences needed from \eqref{eq:PQ-approx} are the moment
identities, for compatible $M,N$,
\begin{equation}
  \E[G M G^\top]=\tr(MQ)P,
  \qquad
  \E[G^\top N G]=\tr(NP)Q .
  \label{eq:moment-identities}
\end{equation}
The factor gradients are linear images of $G$, so their second moments carry the
same $r\times r$ matrices $C_A,C_B$ that appear in the update:
$\E[\vecop(G_A)\vecop(G_A)^\top]\approx Q\otimes C_A$ and
$\E[\vecop(G_B)\vecop(G_B)^\top]\approx C_B\otimes P$.

\begin{lemma}[Moment closure under a Kronecker gradient-covariance model]
\label{lem:estimator}
If $\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$ with $C_A=B^\top P B$ and
$C_B=AQA^\top$, then
\begin{equation}
  \E\!\left[\tfrac1r G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A\right]=Q,
  \qquad
  \E\!\left[\tfrac1r G_B C_B^{-1}G_B^\top\right]=P .
  \label{eq:unbiased}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the second identity of \eqref{eq:moment-identities} with $N=B C_A^{-1}B^\top$:
$G^\top N G=G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A$ and
$\tr(NP)=\tr(C_A^{-1}B^\top P B)=\tr(I_r)=r$, giving the first identity. The
second follows with $M=A^\top C_B^{-1}A$.
\end{proof}

PoLoRA keeps only the diagonals and updates them by EMA:
\begin{equation}
  q_j\leftarrow
  \mathrm{EMA}\!\left[\tfrac1r (G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}\right],
  \qquad
  p_i\leftarrow
  \mathrm{EMA}\!\left[\tfrac1r (G_B)_{i,:}C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top\right].
  \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
Because $C_A$ depends on the current $P$ and $C_B$ on the current $Q$, the two
estimates are coupled; the optimizer takes one EMA alternation per step, then
freezes $P,Q$ while computing \eqref{eq:WA}--\eqref{eq:final-step}. In the
implementation the inverse square roots are damped; the formulas above are
written for the ideal positive-definite case.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

The RMS gradient-covariance constraint is first rewritten as a dual bound over the
full ball $\mathcal D_\Sigma$. PoLoRA keeps only the rank-one slice
$\mathcal R_\Sigma$, fits $\Sigma$ by $Q\otimes P$, and obtains the spectral
constraint $\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2$. Pulling that constraint back to the LoRA
factors gives $C_A=B^\top PB$ and $C_B=AQA^\top$; the per-factor solutions are the
polar sandwiches \eqref{eq:WA}, and the common product radius \eqref{eq:rho}
fixes the scalar lengths, giving the implemented step \eqref{eq:final-step}.

\appendix

\section{Average response without the rank-one restriction}
\label{app:frob}

This appendix repeats the main derivation but stops before the restriction from
the full dual ball $\mathcal D_\Sigma$ to the rank-one slice $\mathcal R_\Sigma$.
Starting from \eqref{eq:dual-trust-region}, the Kronecker fit
$\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \sup_{r\in\mathcal D_\Sigma}|r^\top\vecop(U)|
  =\sqrt{\vecop(U)^\top(Q\otimes P)\vecop(U)}
  =\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_F .
  \label{eq:full-ball-frob}
\end{equation}
Thus the full-ball analogue of \eqref{eq:kron-trust-region} is
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\langle G,U\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_F\le\eta .
  \label{eq:frob-trust-region}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Dense full-ball solution]\label{lem:frob}
Let $H:=P^{-1/2}GQ^{-1/2}$. If $H\ne0$, a minimizer of
\eqref{eq:frob-trust-region} is
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{frob}}
  =-\eta\,\frac{P^{-1}GQ^{-1}}{\norm{P^{-1/2}GQ^{-1/2}}_F}.
  \label{eq:frob-dense-solution}
\end{equation}
If $H=0$, the zero update is a minimizer.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $Y=P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}$. Then
$\langle G,U\rangle=\langle H,Y\rangle$ and the constraint is
$\norm{Y}_F\le\eta$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives the minimum at
$Y=-\eta H/\norm{H}_F$. Undoing the change of variables gives
\eqref{eq:frob-dense-solution}.
\end{proof}

For LoRA, substitute the tangent $T=B\Delta A+\Delta B A$ into
\eqref{eq:frob-trust-region}. The same block split as \S\ref{sec:pullback}
replaces the spectral constraints by Frobenius constraints,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F
  =\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F,
  \qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,A\,Q^{1/2}}_F
  =\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_F.
  \label{eq:frob-factor-blocks}
\end{equation}
The per-factor full-ball subproblems are
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta A}\;\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F\le\tau_A,
  \label{eq:A-frob-block}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta B}\;\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  \qquad\mathrm{s.t.}\qquad
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_F\le\tau_B .
  \label{eq:B-frob-block}
\end{equation}
With $H_A=C_A^{-1/2}G_AQ^{-1/2}$ and
$H_B=P^{-1/2}G_BC_B^{-1/2}$, their nonzero-gradient solutions are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A_{\mathrm{frob}}
  =-\tau_A\,\frac{C_A^{-1}G_AQ^{-1}}{\norm{H_A}_F},
  \qquad
  \Delta B_{\mathrm{frob}}
  =-\tau_B\,\frac{P^{-1}G_BC_B^{-1}}{\norm{H_B}_F}.
  \label{eq:frob-factor-solutions}
\end{equation}
The difference from the main text is now exactly localized: the full dual ball
produces the Frobenius-normalized inverse direction \eqref{eq:frob-factor-solutions},
whereas the rank-one slice produces the spectral polar direction
\eqref{eq:spectral-solutions}.
