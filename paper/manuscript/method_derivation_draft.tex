This note derives the optimiser update from a single principle. The update has a
\emph{magnitude} and a \emph{direction}; the magnitude is forced by symmetry (no free knob),
and the direction is the one place a modelling choice (the curvature metric) enters. Both
reduce to one linear-minimisation oracle --- the curvature-metric cap, with its budget chosen so
the step's operator norm is exactly the radius $\rho$ --- whose closed-form solution is the update
(\S\ref{sec:joint}). Unlike Aurora~\cite{aurora}, whose two constraints conflict and need
iteration, ours is a single cap with a closed form.

\paragraph{Setup.}
A LoRA layer adds $W=BA$ to the frozen weight, with
$A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and $B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. One step
$A\!\to\!A+\Delta A$, $B\!\to\!B+\Delta B$ changes the effective weight by
\begin{equation}
  \DW = B\,\Delta A + \Delta B\,A + \Delta B\,\Delta A
      \;\approx\; B\,\Delta A + \Delta B\,A \qquad\text{(first order).}
  \label{eq:dw}
\end{equation}
Throughout, $G=\nabla_W\mathcal{L}$ is the loss gradient w.r.t.\ the effective weight;
$G_A=B^\top G$ and $G_B=GA^\top$ are the factor gradients; $a:=\norm{A}_2$, $b:=\norm{B}_2$
are the factors' spectral norms; $s_A:=\norm{\Delta A}_2$, $s_B:=\norm{\Delta B}_2$ are the
step sizes we will solve for. $\norm{\cdot}_2$ is the spectral (operator) norm.

\section{The magnitude is forced, not a hyperparameter}

The single assumption is that each step changes the layer's function by a bounded amount. The
worst-case output change of a step is $\max_{\norm{x}=1}\norm{(\DW)x}=\norm{\DW}_2$, so we
require
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\DW}_2 \le \eta .
  \label{eq:trust}
\end{equation}
Everything below either transfers this bound to the factors or pins the resulting step size;
the operator norm and the equal-radius rule are both consequences, not separate choices.

\begin{lemma}[Operator-norm factor budget]\label{lem:budget}
Any step satisfies
$\norm{\DW}_2 \le b\,s_A + a\,s_B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:dw}, the triangle inequality, and submultiplicativity $\norm{MN}_2\le\norm{M}_2\norm{N}_2$,
\[
  \norm{\DW}_2=\norm{B\,\Delta A+\Delta B\,A}_2
  \le\norm{B}_2\norm{\Delta A}_2+\norm{A}_2\norm{\Delta B}_2
  = b\,s_A+a\,s_B. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}
\noindent The factor steps enter \eqref{lem:budget} only through their operator norms
$s_A,s_B$ --- which is why the operator norm is the factor budget. A sufficient condition for
\eqref{eq:trust} is $b\,s_A+a\,s_B\le\eta$; saturating it leaves one equation in two unknowns.
The second comes from symmetry.

\begin{lemma}[Equal radius from symmetry]\label{lem:equal}
Require the update to be equivariant under the transpose relabelling
$\tau:(A,B)\mapsto(B^\top,A^\top)$ (which sends $W\mapsto W^\top$ and $(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)$).
Then a single rule sets both radii, $s_A=\sigma(a,b)$ and $s_B=\sigma(b,a)$, and any
state-independent ratio $s_B/s_A=\lambda$ forces $\lambda=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equivariance means the optimiser cannot use which factor is labelled ``$A$,'' so the radius
of a factor is one fixed function of (its own norm, its partner's norm): $s_A=\sigma(a,b)$,
$s_B=\sigma(b,a)$. A constant ratio gives $\sigma(b,a)=\lambda\,\sigma(a,b)$ for all $a,b$;
swapping $a\leftrightarrow b$ gives $\sigma(a,b)=\lambda\,\sigma(b,a)=\lambda^2\sigma(a,b)$,
hence $\lambda^2=1$ and $\lambda=1$. Equal radii are the unique constant-ratio rule. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Operator-norm radius]\label{prop:rho}
Imposing equal radii $s_A=s_B=:\rho$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:equal}) and saturating the budget
(Lemma~\ref{lem:budget}) gives
\begin{equation}
  \rho \;=\; \frac{\eta}{\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2}.
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\rho\,b+\rho\,a=\eta\Rightarrow\rho=\eta/(a+b)$. \qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent No factor preference is hard-coded: any $A$/$B$ asymmetry enters only through the
state $a,b$ (the per-factor contributions to $\DW$ are $b\rho:a\rho$). The magnitude is thus
not a tunable knob. Empirically the factors self-balance toward $a\!\approx\!b$, so the two
contributions equalise over training; this is an observation, not part of the derivation.

\section{The direction: curvature-adapted steepest descent}

The one modelling input is the geometry in which the step is measured. Model the gradient
second moment as diagonal--Kronecker,
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma=\E[\mathrm{vec}(G)\,\mathrm{vec}(G)^\top]\approx Q\otimes P,\qquad
  P=\diag(p)\ (d_{\mathrm{out}}),\quad Q=\diag(q)\ (d_{\mathrm{in}}),
  \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
the Shampoo Kronecker factorisation under an Adafactor diagonal restriction, and measure the
step in the induced metric $\norm{X}_{P,Q}:=\norm{P^{1/2}XQ^{1/2}}_2$.

\begin{lemma}[Metric cap $=$ gradient whitening]\label{lem:dual}
Capping a step in the $\Sigma$-metric and whitening the gradient by $\Sigma^{-1/2}$ are the
same change of variables: with $Y=\Sigma^{1/2}\mathrm{vec}(\Delta W)$,
\[
  \argmax_{\Delta W}\langle\Delta W,-G\rangle\ \text{s.t.}\ \norm{\Delta W}_\Sigma\le\tau
  \quad=\quad
  \Sigma^{-1/2}\,\argmax_{Y}\langle Y,-\Sigma^{-1/2}\mathrm{vec}(G)\rangle\ \text{s.t.}\ \norm{Y}_2\le\tau .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $\mathrm{vec}(\Delta W)=\Sigma^{-1/2}Y$; the constraint becomes $\norm{Y}_2\le\tau$
and the objective $\langle\Sigma^{-1/2}Y,-G\rangle=\langle Y,-\Sigma^{-1/2}\mathrm{vec}(G)\rangle$.
\qedhere
\end{proof}

\noindent So putting $\Sigma$ in the trust region is one decision viewed two ways. It is the
right decision because raw weight distance is the wrong trust measure --- it ignores that the
loss is anisotropic:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a direction with large $\Sigma$ (steep / high gradient scale) gets a \emph{smaller}
        step (it contributes more to $\norm{\Delta W}_\Sigma$);
  \item a flat direction gets a \emph{larger} step;
  \item so a bounded step is a bounded \emph{predicted loss change}, uniform across directions
        --- the matrix analogue of Adam's $1/\!\sqrt{v}$, natural-gradient logic with a
        spectral cap.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Metric LMO direction]\label{prop:dir}
With $P,Q$ frozen and the $A$-channel's contribution capped in the metric,
$\norm{B\Delta A}_{P,Q}\le\tau$, the maximiser of $\langle\Delta A,-G_A\rangle$ has direction
\begin{equation}
  W_A = C_A^{-1/2}\,\polar\!\big(C_A^{-1/2}\,G_A\,Q^{-1/2}\big)\,Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad C_A=B^\top P B,
  \label{eq:dir}
\end{equation}
with $W_B$ the mirror under $C_B=AQA^\top$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $Y_A=C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}$, so $\norm{B\Delta A}_{P,Q}=\norm{Y_A}_2$ and the
objective is $\langle Y_A,-C_A^{-1/2}G_A Q^{-1/2}\rangle$; the spectral-norm LMO
(Lemma~\ref{lem:dual}) gives $Y_A\propto\polar(C_A^{-1/2}G_A Q^{-1/2})$, and undoing the
substitution gives \eqref{eq:dir}. \qedhere
\end{proof}
\noindent The whitening appears twice: inside the polar (whiten the gradient) and outside
(map the orthogonalised direction back). The polar --- the orthogonalisation at the heart of
Muon --- is therefore taken in the curvature-whitened frame: curvature-aware Muon.

\section{The update is the exact closed-form solution of one capped LMO}
\label{sec:joint}

The direction (Proposition~\ref{prop:dir}) and the radius (Proposition~\ref{prop:rho}) are one
linear-minimisation oracle --- the curvature-metric cap of Proposition~\ref{prop:dir}, with its
budget chosen so the step's operator norm is exactly $\rho$. Writing the unit metric-norm
direction $W_A$ of \eqref{eq:dir} ($\norm{B W_A}_{P,Q}=1$, with $\norm{W_A}_2$ its operator norm),
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\Delta A}\ \langle B\Delta A,-G\rangle \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \norm{B\Delta A}_{P,Q}\le\tau ,
  \qquad \tau:=\rho/\norm{W_A}_2 .
  \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[The update]\label{prop:update}
Assume $H_A:=C_A^{-1/2}G_A Q^{-1/2}$ has full row rank, so $\polar(H_A)$ is unique. The unique
solution of \eqref{eq:joint} is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A=-\rho\,\frac{W_A}{\norm{W_A}_2},\qquad \Delta B=-\rho\,\frac{W_B}{\norm{W_B}_2},
  \qquad \norm{\Delta A}_2=\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho .
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:dir} gives the LMO maximiser $-\tau W_A$ (the sign aligns $B\Delta A$ with
$-G$), whose operator norm is $\tau\norm{W_A}_2=\rho$ by the choice of $\tau$. Full row rank of
$H_A$ makes $\polar(H_A)$, hence the maximiser, unique. The $B$-block is the mirror. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\noindent There is a single knob, the radius $\rho$ (the $B=0$-stable magnitude of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rho}); the metric budget $\tau$ is \emph{not} free --- it is fixed by $\rho$
as the budget for which the solution has operator norm $\rho$. So there is no second
hyperparameter, and no ``solve a direction, then set a magnitude'': \eqref{eq:update} is the
closed-form solution of one LMO.

\begin{remark}[Why this budget --- and why a second cap would be redundant]
One could instead impose an explicit operator-norm cap $\norm{\Delta A}_2\le\rho$ alongside the
metric cap. As $\tau$ grows, the solution $-\tau W_A$ scales linearly, so its operator norm passes
through $\rho$ at exactly $\tau=\rho/\norm{W_A}_2$: below this the metric cap binds and the
operator-norm cap is slack; above it the operator-norm cap binds and the solution degenerates to
the un-whitened polar $-\rho\,\polar(G_A)$. Choosing $\tau=\rho/\norm{W_A}_2$ is the boundary,
where the operator-norm cap is tight but redundant. Stating both caps therefore adds nothing
beyond re-expressing this choice of $\tau$ --- which is why we set $\tau$ from $\rho$ directly.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Contrast with Aurora]
Aurora~\cite{aurora} also poses steepest descent under a joint constraint, but its two constraints
--- orthogonality and uniform row norms --- genuinely \emph{conflict}: a tall matrix cannot satisfy
both (their Claim~1), so the joint problem has no closed form and is solved by an \emph{iterative}
alternating projection. Ours reduces to a single cap with the exact closed form \eqref{eq:update}:
the curvature and magnitude requirements are compatible, met by one whitened-polar step.
Conflicting constraints force iteration; compatible ones admit a closed form.
\end{remark}

\section{Estimating the metric: a coupled flip-flop}

We never form $G$. The factor gradients are linear images of it, so their row second moments
are the partner side conjugated by the factor: $\mathrm{Cov}\,\mathrm{vec}(G_A)=Q\otimes C_A$
with $C_A=B^\top P B$, and the mirror $C_B=AQA^\top$ --- the same $r\times r$ matrices used in
\eqref{eq:dir}.

\begin{lemma}[Unbiased curvature estimator]\label{lem:est}
Under model \eqref{eq:sigma}, $\E\!\big[\tfrac1r\,G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A\big]=Q$ and
$\E\!\big[\tfrac1r\,G_B C_B^{-1}G_B^\top\big]=P$, with no distributional assumption on $G$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Model \eqref{eq:sigma} gives the moment identity $\E[G^\top N G]=\tr(NP)\,Q$. Put
$N=B C_A^{-1}B^\top$; then $G^\top N G=G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A$ and
$\tr(NP)=\tr(C_A^{-1}B^\top P B)=\tr(C_A^{-1}C_A)=r$, so the expectation is $rQ$. The mirror is
identical. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\noindent The diagonals of these estimators, run as EMAs, are the metric:
\begin{equation}
  q_j=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r\,(G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}\Big],\qquad
  p_i=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r\,(G_B)_{i,:}\,C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top\Big].
  \label{eq:est}
\end{equation}
The two sides are coupled, so the estimate is a fixed point solved by alternation:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $C_A$ carries the current $P$ and $C_B$ the current $Q$ --- that shared dependence is
        the coupling;
  \item we estimate $Q$ under the current $P$, then $P$ under the current $Q$, one EMA step per
        iteration (the ``flip-flop'');
  \item this is the coupled diagonal second-moment matching of KL-Shampoo, on the factor
        gradients rather than the dense gradient;
  \item decoupling ($C_A=C_B=I$) degenerates to two independent Adafactor diagonals.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[Matrix-normal lens]
Assuming $G$ is matrix-normal, $\mathrm{vec}(G)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q\otimes P)$, strengthens
Lemma~\ref{lem:est} but is not needed for it:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the flip-flop \eqref{eq:est} becomes the maximum-likelihood estimator (Dutilleul's
        flip-flop algorithm), upgrading ``unbiased'' to ``MLE'';
  \item the whitened gradient is then exactly $\Sigma^{-1/2}\mathrm{vec}(G)\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$,
        upgrading ``white in second moment'' to ``exactly white'';
  \item the trust-region argument (\S2) uses only the second-moment version, so this is an
        interpretive lens, not a load-bearing assumption --- real gradients are not exactly
        matrix-normal.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\noindent Estimation (this section) and the step (\eqref{eq:joint}) are separate problems ---
estimate the curvature, freeze it, take the step --- composed by an outer alternation.

\section{Why this construction: stability at $B=0$}

The operator-norm radius \eqref{eq:rho} is gauge-dependent on purpose, and that is what makes
the standard initialisation $B=0$ safe.
\begin{itemize}
  \item At $B=0$, $\rho=\eta/\norm{A}_2$ by \eqref{eq:rho} --- finite, an ordinary step. ($\Delta A=0$ there, since $G_A=B^\top G=0$, so $B$ moves first.)
  \item The metric LMO's \emph{own} magnitude is scale-free: $\norm{\Delta A}_2\propto1/\sigma_{\min}(B)\to\infty$ as $B\to0$ --- the decoupled / iMuon failure.
  \item A magnitude invariant to the rescaling $(A,B)\mapsto(cA,c^{-1}B)$ could depend only on gauge invariants ($\norm{A}_2\norm{B}_2$ or $\norm{BA}_2$), both zero at $B=0$. Pinning $\rho$ to $\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2$ trades that invariance for $B=0$ stability; the leftover scaling freedom is resolved dynamically by self-balancing.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Summary.}
The update is the exact closed-form solution of one curvature-metric-capped LMO \eqref{eq:joint}.
The direction is the whitened polar in the estimated metric (Proposition~\ref{prop:dir}); the only
knob is the radius $\rho=\eta/(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$, forced by the merged-step budget
(Lemma~\ref{lem:budget}) and transpose symmetry (Lemma~\ref{lem:equal}), and it fixes the metric
budget $\tau=\rho/\norm{W_A}_2$ at which the solution has operator norm $\rho$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:update}). The metric is estimated by a coupled flip-flop
(Lemma~\ref{lem:est}), composed with the step by an outer alternation.
