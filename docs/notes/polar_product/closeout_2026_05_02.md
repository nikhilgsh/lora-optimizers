# Polar-product close-out (2026-05-02)

Empirical close-out of the architecture-search day that followed `proposal.md`.
This doc records what was tested, what was learned, and what's open. Reference
spec is `proposal.md`; reference theory is `theory.md`; reference older empirical
trail is `investigations.md`.

All numbers single-seed, 2k-step horizon, OLMo-2-0425-1B + Magicoder-OSS-Instruct-75K.
Noise floor (5 seeds AdamW lr=3e-4): σ_r=16 = 0.0011, σ_r=64 = 0.0008. Per-rank
leaders: baseline polar k=1 r=16 = 0.7546, baseline polar k=2 r=64 = 0.7382.

## TL;DR

- **No strict same-rule winner identified.** Across all variants tested today,
  no single rule lands within 2σ noise of both per-rank leaders simultaneously.
- **Best same-rule candidate by avg-Δ: picard k=16** (0.7582 / 0.7369). Within
  noise of r=64 leader; ~3σ loss at r=16. Beats AdamW everywhere.
- **Operator axis (polar vs clip) is empirically and theoretically dead** in the
  LoRA regime. Diagnosed below.
- **Gauge axis** changes the trade-off but doesn't yield a strict winner. Gauge
  fixes the k=2-hurts-r=16 problem but absorbs cross-coupling, losing the r=64
  benefit.
- **Mechanism for baseline polar k=2's r=64 win** remains opaque. Per-step
  diagnostics (col(B), descent, frob_J) all rule out the obvious stories.

## What was tested

### Operator × gauge 2×2

| | with gauge (lift, no postscale) | without gauge (RMS-align) |
|---|---|---|
| polar | gauge+polar k=2 lr=3e-3: 0.7540 / 0.7431 | baseline polar k=2-endrms: 0.7615 / 0.7382 |
| clip | clip+gauge corrected lr=3e-3 k=2: 0.7532 / ~0.7445 | clip+RMS k=2-endrms: 0.7604 / ~0.7474 |

Reading: within each operator (row), gauge fixes r=16 by ~0.007–0.008 at the cost
of ~0.005–0.006 at r=64. Within each gauge (column), polar vs clip differ by
≤ 0.002 — within noise. **The gauge constraint is the real lever; the operator
choice is empirically irrelevant.**

### Picard k-sweep

| k | r=16 | r=64 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.7546 | 0.7454 |
| 2 | 0.7615 | 0.7382 |
| 3 | 0.7557 | — |
| 4 | 0.7594 | 0.7374 |
| 16 | 0.7582 | 0.7369 |

k=3 < k=4 because Picard inner iterates oscillate (osc_cos ≈ -0.85 at r=16).
k=16 is the per-step fixed-point; k=4 is partway with ~30% residual. The fixed
point at r=16 (~0.758) is itself worse than k=1 (0.7546).

### Failed directions tested today

- **R2 (local-model variational selector for k):** dead. Per-step variational
  score `⟨u, ΔA⟩ + ⟨u, ΔB⟩ + (1/2η)‖J‖²` strictly prefers k=1 over k=2 at every
  pair, every step, every cell — including at r=64 where k=2 empirically wins.
  Linear-term-only variant: same direction. No per-step Adam-covector inner
  product predicts cross-coupling's long-run benefit.
- **Saturation-conditional interpolation** (gauge ↔ baseline by sat_B): refuted
  by existing α-sweep data showing interior α between k=1 and k=2 is worse than
  endpoints at r=16. Smooth interpolation in this 1D family doesn't escape the
  trade-off.
- **R-equal τ rule for clip:** blew up due to magnitude calibration mismatch.
  Fixed via M = lr·√r (polar-equivalent magnitude) — clip then becomes
  empirically equivalent to polar within noise.
- **R-AdamW-cap (M = lr·‖u_A‖_F):** wrong scale (~50× polar's effective
  magnitude); blew up at lr ≥ 1e-3.

## Mechanistic findings

### What we ruled out for "why baseline polar k=2 wins r=64"

Per-step diagnostics in `mechanism_diag_baseline` (full instrumentation in
`AdamPolarProductLoRA.step`):

- **col(B) refinement at iter 2** — FALSIFIED. iter 2's dB has slightly LESS
  col(B)-projection than iter 1's (0.314 → 0.308 at r=64 step 20; same at all
  steps and ranks).
- **Per-step linear descent gain** — FALSIFIED. descent_iter1 ≈ descent_iter2
  to 4 decimals. Cross-coupling at iter 2 doesn't buy more linear loss decrease.
- **Joint NE convergence** — FALSIFIED. The exact joint-Frobenius solver
  (`adam-lin-lora` = AdamLinLoRA) loses at r=64 (0.7527 vs polar k=1's 0.7454).
  So "approaching the joint NE" can't be the explanation.

### Picard convergence/oscillation

- **Damped oscillation at both ranks.** osc_cos ≈ −0.85 at r=16, −0.79 at r=64
  (cos between successive picard increments δ_0 = dA2−dA1 and δ_1 = dA3−dA2).
  Iterates flip ~150° between steps; magnitude shrinks (contract ratio 0.04
  early → 0.73 late at r=16).
- **k=3 < k=4 < k=16** non-monotone pattern at r=16 IS oscillation — k=2
  overshoots, k=3 partial-recovers, k=4 overshoots smaller, k=16 damps near
  fixed point.
- **Per-step convergence at k=16 is essentially exact** (residual ~1.3% at
  r=16, ~10⁻⁴ at r=64). The per-step fixed point at r=16 is itself worse than
  block-diagonal (k=1) — going to convergence is structurally suboptimal at
  small r.

### Gauge KKT shrinks K → kills cross-coupling

- **Algebraic identity:** under min-Frob gauge, Q_B^T·dB_prev = R_B^{−T}·K_prev,
  so the cross-coupling input to picard iter k is `T_A = −R_B^{−T}·K_prev·A`.
  Cross-coupling magnitude is proportional to ‖K‖.
- **Empirical:** K_frob in gauge variants stays at **0.001–0.002** throughout
  training — about 1–2% of typical norm_dA (~0.05). Gauge structurally
  absorbs cross-coupling content.
- **Confirmed mechanism:** gauge k=1 ≈ gauge k=2 at every rank because the
  cross-coupling pathway is mathematically near-zero. Same eval at full 2k
  (Δ ≤ 0.0003 across all configs tested).

### sr_B trajectory (longitudinal, partial)

At r=64 (step ~500, where data overlaps):
- baseline polar k=1: sr_B/r ≈ 0.39 (most spread)
- baseline polar k=2 / k=16: sr_B/r ≈ 0.37
- gauge polar k=1 / k=2: sr_B/r ≈ 0.37 (same as baseline k=2)

Confirms k=2 concentrates B's column space MORE than k=1, but gauge variants
also concentrate (similarly) and DON'T win like baseline k=2. So
"B-concentration" is a side effect, not the cause of k=2's r=64 win.

### Operator equivalence at LoRA scale (cos diagnostic)

`cos_polar_clip_probe` (200 steps, baseline polar+RMS-align r=16/r=64,
lr=3e-4) measures cos(polar(X), clip(X; τ_R-equal)) per pair:
- r=16: median 0.97–0.98 (range 0.93–1.00)
- r=64: median 0.96 (range 0.89–0.99)

Polar and clip produce step directions ~12–15° apart on average. Trajectory
averages this out across 2k steps → empirical equivalence in eval.

### Mathematical bound on τ-rule

Any τ rule navigates a 1D family with cos ∈ [√p/√r, 1] where p is the
participation ratio:
- τ → ∞: cos = √p/√r (clip becomes identity → matches `adam-scaled-lora`,
  empirically dominated)
- τ → 0: cos = 1 (clip becomes polar, matches baseline polar)

So τ rules interpolate between two already-tested optimizers. R-equal sits
near the polar end (cos ≈ 0.96). No τ choice escapes this segment.

**Practical shipping rule:** picard k=16. Within noise of r=64 leader, ~3σ loss

optimization could reduce to ~1.2×. The first single-rule LoRA optimizer in this
project that's no-worse-than-AdamW at every rank tested.

## Future directions

In rough priority order.

### Already closed (no follow-up needed)

- **Operator (polar vs clip) and τ rule.** Empirically and theoretically
  closed. Mathematical bound shows τ rules interpolate between two tested
  optimizers, neither of which is a same-rule winner.
- **Smooth k-interpolation (constant or saturation-conditional α).** Existing
  `alpha_sweep_2x2` shows interior α is worse than endpoints at r=16.
- **Cross-coupling as r=64 win mechanism via single-step diagnostics.** All
  per-step probes ruled out the obvious stories.

### High priority — likely to pay off

1. **Anderson acceleration on Picard inner loop.** Damped-oscillation regime
   (osc_cos ≈ −0.85) is exactly Anderson's sweet spot. Project to converge in
   3–5 effective iters instead of 16. Combined with Gram-NS implementation
   (Dao 2026, see `docs/papers/gram_newton_schulz_dao_2026.md`), picard k=16
   wall cost could drop from 2.6× to ~1.2× k=1. Concrete: ~50 lines in
   `AdamPolarProductLoRA.step`.

2. **Heavy-ball averaging of picard inner iterates (cheaper Anderson cousin).**
   Instead of applying the last picard iterate, apply `dA_step = β·dA_{k-1}
   + (1−β)·dA_k` with β ≈ 0.5. With osc_cos ≈ −0.85 at r=16, pairs of
   adjacent oscillating iterates average to near the fixed point in one shot.
   Cheap (1 line, no Anderson bookkeeping). Sub-priority of #1; test as
   ablation for whether full Anderson is needed or simple averaging suffices.

3. **SOAP-style preconditioning of u_A, u_B before polar.** Empirical
   evidence: full Adam on raw G beats no-Adam by 0.04 at r=16; matrix-Adam
   (scalar v) is between them at +0.02. So **per-coord conditioning of
   u_A, u_B IS doing major work** — the pipeline benefits from a
   well-conditioned input before polar saturates the spectrum. SOAP's
   "Adam-in-data-derived-basis" is the natural extension: maintain rolling
   eigenbasis Q_A, Q_B (r×r per pair) and apply Adam moments to projected
   gradients. r×r state per side instead of r×d_in / d_out·r — cheaper AND
   captures correlated-direction structure that per-coord Adam misses.

   **Likely impact:** speculation, not asymmetric prediction. SOAP is
   theoretically motivated by the empirical "well-conditioned input
   matters" chain (full Adam → no Adam = +0.04). It could push that
   further, plausibly improving BOTH ranks. Whether it asymmetrically
   helps r=16 more is unclear from data — both ranks have low effective
   rank relative to nominal r. The earlier asymmetric argument here was
   over-confident; reframe as "SOAP is a generally promising direction
   that may or may not close the r=16 gap specifically."

   Distinct from variants already tested:
   - AdaMuon (variance on polar output, coord basis): tested, lost.
   - Per-singular-mode adaptive (track v on spectrum): untested but
     simpler than full SOAP — could be a stepping-stone test.
   - GaLore (project onto top-r subspace + Adam): tested, lost — but
     GaLore's goal was memory not conditioning, different design.

   **Update (2026-05-03): one-sided SOAP implemented and tested at r=16.**
   - Implementation: `AdamSOAPPolarProductLoRA` in `lora_playground/optim.py`.
     Faithful to Vyas et al. (arXiv:2409.11321) Algorithm 3, validated by
     two equivalence tests (`tests/test_polar_product.py::test_soap_matches_*`):
     line-for-line against an inline reference of Alg 3, AND against the
     official `nikhilvyas/SOAP` repo configured one-sided via
     `max_precond_dim`. Operates on the r-side only (Q_R = I for d_in/d_out)
     — paper's huge-dim special case (§"For layers with huge dimensions").
   - Empirical result at step 200/400 (group `adam_soap_polar_product_r16_v2_2k`,
     job 6327855, single-seed, sweep cancelled and replaced with diag-on
     v2_diag_2k — final 2k numbers TBD): SOAP ≈ AdamPolarProductLoRA at
     matched η across {1e-4, 3e-4, 1e-3, 3e-3, 1e-2}, |Δ| ≤ 0.008. The
     one-sided rotation neither helps nor hurts. (A buggy v1 had a
     momentum-frame bug + full-eigh discontinuity + refresh-order bug; v1
     lost by +0.0160 at η=3e-4 step 2000. The full Δ in v1 was the bugs,
     not a SOAP signal.)
   - **Spectrum diagnostic added** (group `adam_soap_polar_product_r16_v2_diag_2k`,
     job 6328045): `optim_soap_step` JSONL events log per-pair condition
     number, top-eigenvalue fraction, and participation ratio for L_A and
     R_B every 20 steps. Probes whether the "SOAP ≈ baseline" result is
     "rotation finds nothing structured" (PR ≈ r) vs "rotation finds the
     right basis but it doesn't help" (PR ≪ r). Smoke-step values at near-init
     (r=4 toy model) showed PR(L_A) median 1.6/4 — strong anisotropy. Need
     full r=16 trajectory before drawing conclusions.

   **Low-rank SOAP on the d-side as the natural extension** (untested):
   if the diagnostic confirms anisotropic L_A AND R_B but one-sided SOAP
   doesn't close the r=16 gap, the missing axis is d-side preconditioning.
   Full d×d SOAP is too expensive (d ∈ {2048, 4096}). A truncated `Q_R ∈
   ℝ^{d×k}` with k ≈ r is the cleaner abstraction than mixing in GaLore:
   maintain top-k eigenpairs of `R = E[g^T g]` via streaming subspace
   iteration, never forming R explicitly. Per-step update of the EMA
   `(R Q_R)_t = β·(R Q_R)_{t-1} + (1-β)·gᵀ(g·Q_R_{t-1})`, refresh
   `Q_R ← QR(R Q_R)` periodically (Algorithm 4 with truncated Q). State:
   r²·Q_L + d·k·Q_R + d·k for streaming `R Q_R` + r·k Adam moments — linear
   in d·k vs d² for full SOAP. Open choice: handle the residual `(I - Q_R
   Q_R^T)g` direction with per-coord Adam (no info dropped, more state) vs
   discard it (matches GaLore's truncation, simpler). This is a v3 candidate
   IF v2 diagnostics motivate it — defer until spectrum data lands.

4. **Cautious-mask post-polar with rescaling.** Apply sign-agreement filter
   (Liang et al. 2024, arXiv:2411.16085): mask out coordinates where the
   polar+RMS step disagrees with raw gradient sign, then rescale the
   surviving coordinates to preserve the original step's Frobenius norm.
   Filters noise where the optimizer's smart direction goes against
   greedy descent.

   **Existing diagnostic data on sign-agreement (from mechanism_diag_baseline):**
   - r=16 iter-1 polar step sign agreement with −grad ≈ 0.62
   - r=64 iter-1 polar step sign agreement with −grad ≈ 0.62
   - **The disagreement rate is nearly IDENTICAL across ranks.** So
     cautious-mask is NOT differentially r=16-helpful. Could still be a
     general noise filter (38% of coords removed uniformly), but doesn't
     target the asymmetric r=16-vs-r=64 problem.

   Demoted to "general regularizer" status; not the same-rule fix.

### NOT a r=16 fix (cost optimizations only): Anderson, heavy-ball

Earlier draft listed Anderson with residual-based stopping and adaptive-β
heavy-ball as "geometric r=16 fixes". On closer look: **these accelerate
picard convergence but converge to the same point.** At r=16 the picard
fixed point itself (~0.758, where k=16 lands) is WORSE than k=1's 0.7546
— no amount of cheaper convergence improves the destination. Picard
convergence diagnostics can't detect "fixed point is bad"; they only
measure speed of approach.

Anderson and heavy-ball are valuable purely as **wall-cost reductions
for k=large variants** (priorities 1, 2 above). They don't fix r=16
quality.

### Medium priority — actual r=16-targeting (speculative)

5. **Mechanism probes — explanatory, NOT actionable.** Per-step
   diagnostics ruled out the obvious mechanisms. Further probes
   (Hessian-vector products on the real loss, toy-quadratic
   simulations, optimizer-operator eigenspectrum) could potentially
   reveal *why* baseline polar k=2 wins r=64 — but understanding the
   mechanism doesn't directly hand us a better optimizer. The
   polar+RMS+picard pipeline already exploits whatever mechanism is at
   play; knowing what it is doesn't tell us how to do better.

   Mechanism probes satisfy curiosity. They don't generate algorithms.
   Deprioritized — focus on action-oriented exploration of the
   optimizer family (Anderson, SOAP) instead. If a probe is run
   eventually, the cheapest is real-loss HVP (no transfer assumption)
   not toy-quadratic.

### Low priority / not on critical path

8. **Memory-saving "drop v, keep m" Adam variant.** True no-v with full
   per-coordinate m has NOT been directly tested in this project (only
   matrix-Adam = scalar v, and pure-momentum Muon = no Adam at all). Cheap
   to add; may match `adam-polar-product-lora` at half the optimizer state.

9. **Anti-concentration regularizer on B.** Add a small penalty on
   `σ_max(B) / ‖B‖_F` to the loss. Keeps B's spectrum spread independent
   of the optimizer's choices. Out-of-band fix; doesn't change the
   optimizer. Risk: regularizer hyperparameter, and may cap useful
   concentration at r=64.

10. **Multi-seed any candidate** — only when shipping a variant.
    Single-seed numbers are accurate to within ~0.001 noise for the
    leading candidates.

11. **Different parametrization (DoRA, periodic merging, rank-adaptive).**
    Out of scope per project goal — optimizing LoRA optimizers, not
    LoRA parametrizations.

### Already explored, not future direction

- **Spectral re-centering of B as post-processor** (initial draft of this
  doc listed this). Equivalent to gauge-invariance reparametrization
  `(A, B) → (S^{−1} A, B S)` already explored as `polar-coupled-core-*-imbalance-*`
  and `state-rebalanced` variants. Per `investigations.md` §3.3: drives
  imbalance residual to 10⁻³ as designed, eval gain ≤ 0.014. Doesn't fix
  r=16. Subset of already-tested state-rebalance space.

- **Matrix-Adam (scalar v):** tested, loses 0.02.

- **Pure-momentum Muon (no Adam):** tested as `polar-product-lora`, loses
  0.04 vs `adam-polar-product-lora`.

- **Smooth k-interpolation (constant α or saturation-conditional):**
  tested via `alpha_sweep_2x2`; interior α worse than endpoints at r=16.

## Where to look for the next breakthrough

The operator and gauge axes are well-explored. The remaining structural
levers that could matter:

- **Magnitude rule** (priorities 2 and 3 above) — different than what we've
  tested (RMS-align with full Adam covectors).
- **Cross-coupling iteration scheme** (priority 1 — Anderson) — same picard
  fixed point, cheaper to reach.
- **Multi-step trajectory effects** that single-step diagnostics can't see —
  the actual r=64 mechanism likely lives here (priority 5).

The gauge axis closed a question (cross-coupling can be neutralized by
constraint geometry) but the answer's the wrong sign for shipping at r=64.
The clip variants closed another question (operator differences are too small
at LoRA scale to matter). What's left is exotic: magnitude policies, smarter
fixed-point methods, or genuine multi-step mechanism understanding.
