LoRA \cite{lora} freezes the pretrained weight $W_0$ and trains a rank-$r$ update
$\DW=(\alpha/r)\,BA$, $A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$,
$B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. The optimizer that trains it is the
under-examined lever; practice defaults to Adam on the factors $A,B$. We introduce
\methodname{}, a spectral-polar LoRA optimizer that reaches Adam's eval loss in
$1.2$--$1.7\times$ fewer steps across nine (model, dataset, rank) settings.

\paragraph{Motivation.} Naive Muon (\Cref{lem:lmo}) and iMuon (\Cref{prop:decoupled}) are the same spectral LMO under the Euclidean inner product, differing only in what they cap. \methodname{} keeps a spectral LMO for the \emph{direction} but measures it in a learned two-sided metric, and sets the \emph{magnitude} by a separate rule. Three stages:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.7em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item[(A)] \emph{Curvature.} Estimate diagonal $P,Q$ online as the Kronecker factors of the gradient second moment (coupled diagonal second-moment matching \cite{klshampoo}; \Cref{eq:ours-klcoupling}).
  \item[(B)] \emph{Direction.} Spectral-norm LMO of the loss in that metric --- a whiten--polar--unwhiten step \eqref{eq:factor-directions}.
  \item[(C)] \emph{Magnitude.} Discard the LMO budget; re-pin to the operator-norm radius (\Cref{eq:ours-prodcap}).
\end{itemize}
Stages (A) and (B) are separate problems --- estimation, then a step under the \emph{frozen} estimate --- composed by alternation, not one objective.

\section{Spectral Geometry with LoRA}
\label{sec:setup}



Consider a single linear layer with frozen pretrained weight
$
W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}.
$
Following LoRA~\cite{lora}, we fine-tune this layer by learning a low-rank update
\begin{equation}
\DW = \frac{\alpha}{r}\,BA, \qquad
B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times r}, \quad
A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\mathrm{in}}},
\label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
of rank \(r\) with scaling \(\alpha\). We absorb the scaling \(\alpha/r\) into \(A\) or \(B\) henceforth and write the update as \(BA\), so that we solve
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{A, B}{\mbox{minimize}}&
\ell(W_0 + BA),
\end{array}
\]
where \(\ell\) is the task loss.


Here we will derive a type of spectral descent method~\cite{carlson2015preconditioned, carlson2015stochastic}, that is, a variant of Muon~\cite{muon} specialized to low rank matrices. 
For clarity, we first write the method without momentum; in practice, \(G_t\) may be replaced by a momentum buffer before applying the polar map.

The key idea behind Muon is that we must control the size of the updates to the weight matrices in the spectral norm:
\[ 
\|W\|_2 := \max_{\|x\|_2 =1} \|W x \|_2,
\]
where $x\in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and $\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}} x_i^2}$ is the standard Euclidean norm.
The spectral norm is a conservative norm over linear operators, since it focuses on the \emph{worst-case} possible input $x$, that will give the largest output. Thus constraining against a large change in the spectral norm, can lead to more stable training and feature learning~\cite{yang2023spectral,bernstein2025modular}.
That is, for a given weight $W_t = W_0 + B_tA_t$ and corresponding  gradient matrix $G \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ for this layer, we minimize the local linearization of our loss, subject to a spectral ball constraint:
\begin{align} \label{eq:muon}
    W_{t+1} &= \argmin_{W\in  \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}} \langle G_t,W-W_t\rangle \quad \mbox{subject to}\quad  \| W-W_t\|_2 \leq \eta \\
    &= W_t - \eta \polar(G), \label{lem:lmo} 
\end{align} 
where  $\polar(G_t) := U V^\top$ and 
$
G_t = U \Sigma V^\top
$
is the reduced or thin singular value decomposition.

This polar factor is the matrix sign used in Muon and can be computed efficiently by Newton--Schulz-type iterations~\cite{muon,polarexpress}.

\paragraph{Naive Muon.}
A naive way to combine Muon with a LoRA is simply to update the factors $A$ and $B$ separately using  
\begin{equation}
   A_{t+1} = A_t-\eta\,\polar(G_A),\qquad  B_{t+1}= B_t-\eta\,\polar(G_B),
  \label{eq:naive}
\end{equation}
where $G_A \in  \R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and $G_B
\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$ are the gradients of $A$ and $B$, respectively. 


This naive update~\eqref{eq:naive} ignores the product structure, and introduces undesired dependencies on otherwise ignored symmetries. Indeed
the product $BA$ is invariant under $(A,B)\mapsto(NA,BN^{-1})$ for invertible $N$. However, the factor gradients transform as
\[
G_{A} \mapsto N^{-\top}G_{A},
\qquad
G_{B} \mapsto G_{B}N^\top.
\]
Since $\polar$ is not equivariant, the update \eqref{eq:naive} depends on the factorization, even though the product $BA$ does not~\cite{imuon}.


\paragraph{Ideal Low Rank Muon.}
The issue with the above naive approach, is that $A$ and $B$ alone do not encode the linear layer, but rather the product $BA$. 
Indeed, the resulting linear layer of our model is now encoded by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:lin-op}
    x \mapsto W_0 \,x + BA \,x.
\end{equation} 
Let $W = W_0 + BA $ for shorthand.
Applying exactly the Muon update~\eqref{eq:muon} and substituting in $W$ and $W_t$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:muon-quad}
    \argmin_{B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times r},
A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}} \langle G_t,BA-B_t A_t\rangle  \quad \mbox{subject to}\quad  \| BA-B_tA_t\|_2 \leq \eta,
\end{equation} 
  This update~\eqref{eq:muon-quad} would be the ideal spectral descent for LoRA, but it is not practical, because of the difficult nonlinear constraint. To make this practical, we will admit one approximation, and one upper bound.

\paragraph{Practical Low Rank Muon.}
Our  approximation will be to linearize the map \((B,A) \mapsto BA\) around the current iterate \((B_t,A_t)\), that is we will use the first-order approximation 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:BA-lin}
BA
\approx
B_tA_t
+
B_t(A-A_t)
+
(B-B_t)A_t.
\end{equation}
Using this linearization we have that the objective in~\eqref{eq:muon-quad} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obj-lin}
    \langle G, BA -B_tA_t\rangle \approx 
\langle G, B_t(A-A_t)
+
(B-B_t)A_t\rangle \; =\;  
\langle G_A,A-A_t \rangle
+
\langle G_B, B-B_t\rangle,
\end{equation} 
where for the equality we used  the chain-rule giving
  \[
  \nabla_B \ell(W_0+B_tA_t) = G_B = G_t A^\top,
  \qquad
  \nabla_A \ell(W_0+B_tA_t) = G_A = B_t^\top G .
  \]
Finally to make the constraint tractable, we will use the linearization~\eqref{eq:BA-lin} and an upper bound as follows
\begin{align} \label{eq:constraint-lin-bnd}
    \|BA - B_tA_t\|_2 & \approx 
    \|B_t(A-A_t)
+
(B-B_t)A_t\|_2 \leq     \|B_t(A-A_t)\|_2
+
\|(B-B_t)A_t\|_2,
\end{align}
where we used subadditivity of the spectral norm. 
Therefore, it is sufficient to impose separate budgets 
\begin{equation} \label{eq:twobudget} 
\|B_t(A-A_t)\|_2 \leq \eta_A,
\qquad
\|(B-B_t)A_t\|_2 \leq \eta_B,
\qquad
\eta_A+\eta_B \leq \eta.
\end{equation}
This bound was also explored in  \cite{tilde_csd}.
Finally using~\eqref{eq:obj-lin},~\eqref{eq:twobudget} and~\eqref{eq:constraint-lin-bnd} in~\eqref{eq:muon-quad} we arrive at the following two tractable programs and their solution.

\begin{restatable}[Decoupled factor step]{proposition}{DecoupledFactorStep}\label{prop:decoupled}
Assume that \(B_t\) has full column rank and \(A_t\) has full row rank. 
Let $S_A=A_tA_t^\top \in \R^{r \times r}$, $S_B=B_t^\top B_t \in \R^{r \times r}$.
Using the linearization~\eqref{eq:BA-lin} and the bound~\eqref{eq:constraint-lin-bnd}
in~\eqref{eq:muon-quad},
we arrive at the two programs
\begin{align}
  A_{t+1}& =   \argmin_{ 
A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}}~ \langle G_A, A-A_t\rangle \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad  \norm{B_t(A-A_t)}_2\le\eta_A \nonumber \\
 B_{t+1} &=   \argmin_{B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times r}}~ \langle G_B,  B -B_t\rangle \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad  \norm{(B-B_t) A_t}_2\le\eta_B,
  \label{eq:imuon-lmo}
\end{align}
to which their solutions are  given by 
\begin{equation*}
 A_{t+1}= A_t-\eta_A S_B^{-1/2} \polar \big(S_B^{-1/2}G_A\big),
 \qquad
B_{t+1}= B_t-\eta_B \polar \big(G_B S_A^{-1/2}\big) S_A^{-1/2}.
\end{equation*}
Furthermore we have that 
\begin{equation} \label{cor:imuon}
    \norm{B_t(A_{t+1}-A_t)}_2 = \eta_A, \quad \mbox{and} \quad   \norm{(B_{t+1}-B_t) A_t}_2 = \eta_B.
\end{equation}
\end{restatable}

For the proof see 
(\Cref{app:proofs}, iMuon \cite{imuon}, or the half-split of compositional Muon \cite{tilde_csd}.


The resulting update~\eqref{eq:imuon-lmo} whitens each factor gradient by the inverse Gram root of the opposite factor. This makes the step depend on the local geometry of the product \(BA\), rather than on the arbitrary scaling of the individual factors.

\section{Method: \methodname}
\label{sec:method}
% NOT A PURE LMO: the metric P,Q is a coupled diagonal KL flip-flop (estimation,
% not an LMO), and the MAGNITUDE is the operator-norm pin rho=eta/(smax A+smax B)
% (a separate rule, not the LMO budget tau). Keep those three ingredients distinct.
% Prose is terse here by design (author expands); the equations are the content.
% Citation keys (verify in references.bib): kang_specgf, imuon, spectron,
% polarexpress, soap, mua, tilde_csd (compositional steepest descent -- no arXiv).
\label{sec:method-ours}
Consider a single linear layer with frozen pretrained weight
$
W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}.
$
Following LoRA~\cite{lora}, we fine-tune this layer by learning a low-rank update
\begin{equation}
\DW = \frac{\alpha}{r}\,BA, \qquad
B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times r}, \quad
A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\mathrm{in}}},
\label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
of rank \(r\) with scaling \(\alpha\). To simplify the derivation, we suppress the scaling \(\alpha/r\) so that the merged weight $W = W_0 + BA$. This simplification matches our experimental setting in \Cref{sec:exp}, where $\alpha = r$. The goal is to learn factors $A, B$ that solve
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{A, B}{\mbox{minimize}}&
\mathcal{L}(W_0 + BA),
\end{array}
\]
where $\mathcal{L}$ is the task loss.

\paragraph{Local model.}
To make the optimizer step tractable, we choose the update direction using a local first-order approximation to the loss. In this local model, we want to decrease the average task loss while bounding how much the loss can change on any single sample. Additive updates $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ change $W$ by the first-order term $U = B \Delta A + \Delta B A$, plus a second-order term $\Delta B \Delta A$. Writing $G = \nabla_W \mathcal{L}$ for the loss gradient and $G_i$ for the gradient on sample $i$, the local model gives the following linear minimization oracle (LMO)
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta A,\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, U \right\rangle \\[2pt]
  \mathrm{subject\ to} &
  \displaystyle \max_i
  \left| \left\langle G_i, U \right\rangle \right|
  \le \gamma ,
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:sample-lmo}
\end{equation}
Since the LMO is homogeneous, $\gamma$ only sets the scale of its solution. We use the solution as the update direction. Later we will choose its magnitude separately so that the merged update has a controlled spectral norm.

\paragraph{Pseudogradient proxy.}
Since enumerating the full set of sample gradients is not tractable, we replace the sample-wise constraint by a maximum over a proxy set $\Gamma$ of pseudogradients. For a linear layer, the per-sample gradient has rank one. By the chain rule, $G_i = b_i x_i^\top$, where $x_i$ is the incoming activation and $b_i$ the backpropagated gradient of the sample loss with respect to the layer output. We therefore choose $\Gamma$ to be the set of rank-one matrices $bx^\top$ whose size is controlled by the second moment of the gradient distribution, $\Sigma = \E_i[ \mathrm{vec}(G_i) \mathrm{vec}(G_i)^\top ]$, namely
\[
\Gamma = \{bx^\top : \mathrm{vec}(bx^\top)^\top \Sigma^{-1} \mathrm{vec}(bx^\top) \le 1\}.
\]
Note that the overall scale of $\Gamma$ is immaterial since it only rescales the constraint and can be directly absorbed into $\gamma$. Replacing the empirical sample gradients by this proxy set gives
\begin{equation}
  \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta A,\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, U \right\rangle \\[2pt]
  \mathrm{subject\ to} &
  \displaystyle
  \sup_{b x^\top \in \Gamma}
  | \ip{bx^\top, U} |
  \le \gamma.
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:pseudogradient-lmo}
  \end{equation}

The matrix $\Sigma$ has size $d_{\mathrm{out}} d_{\mathrm{in}} \times d_{\mathrm{out}} d_{\mathrm{in}}$, so the constraint is not efficiently computable without additional structure. We use a Kronecker ansatz
\begin{equation}
\Sigma = Q \otimes P,
\qquad
P \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{out}}},
\quad
Q \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}},
\label{eq:ours-sigma}
\end{equation}
with $P$ and $Q$ positive definite, following optimizers such as K-FAC and Shampoo \cite{kfac, shampoo, klshampoo}. Under this ansatz, the pseudogradient constraint in \eqref{eq:pseudogradient-lmo} reduces to
\begin{equation}
\sup_{bx^\top \in \Gamma} |\ip{bx^\top, U}| = \norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2,
\label{eq:ours-metric}
\end{equation}
where $\norm{\cdot}_2$ is the spectral norm (see \Cref{lem:pseudogradient-spectral}). For further tractability, we restrict $P$ and $Q$ to be diagonal, as in Adafactor \cite{adafactor}.

Let $G_A=B^\top G$ and $G_B=GA^\top$ denote the factor gradients. We estimate the diagonal factors from these gradients by applying KL-Shampoo's coupled second-moment equations under this diagonal ansatz \cite{klshampoo} (see \Cref{app:matnormal}).

\paragraph{Direction.}
With the diagonal factors $P$ and $Q$ fixed during the step, the direction problem becomes
\begin{equation}
    \begin{array}{ll}
    \underset{\Delta A,\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
    \left\langle G, B\Delta A+\Delta B A \right\rangle \\[2pt]
    \mathrm{subject\ to} &
    \norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_2
    \le \gamma .
    \end{array}
    \label{eq:joint-direction-lmo}
  \end{equation}
  
To handle the coupling between $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ in $U$, we use subadditivity of the spectral norm to split the constraint into two separate budgets, one for each factor. For any $\gamma_A,\gamma_B > 0$ satisfying $\gamma_A+\gamma_B\le\gamma$, this gives two block problems:
\begin{equation}
\setlength{\arraycolsep}{2pt}
\begin{array}{@{}c@{\qquad}c@{}}
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta A}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G_A,\Delta A \right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mathrm{subject\ to} &
\norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \gamma_A ,
\end{array}
&
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G_B,\Delta B \right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mathrm{subject\ to} &
\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \gamma_B .
\end{array}
\end{array}
\label{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo}
\end{equation}
As before, the budgets $\gamma_A$ and $\gamma_B$ only set the scale of the solutions. To solve these block problems, we use the following generalized spectral-norm LMO (see \Cref{app:proofs}).
\begin{lemma}[Generalized spectral-norm LMO]
\label{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}
Let $L$ and $R$ be positive definite. Then
\[
\begin{aligned}
\underset{\norm{LXR}_2\le\gamma}{\mathrm{minimize}}\,
\left\langle M,X\right\rangle
&\quad\text{has solution}\quad -\gamma L^{-1}
\polar\!\left(L^{-1}MR^{-1}\right)
R^{-1}.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}
\noindent Define the contracted curvature factors
\begin{equation}
C_A = B^\top P B,
\qquad
C_B = A Q A^\top .
\label{eq:ours-closure}
\end{equation}
The two constraints in \eqref{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo} then take the form of \Cref{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo} with $(L,R)=(C_A^{1/2},Q^{1/2})$ and $(L,R)=(P^{1/2},C_B^{1/2})$, respectively. Applying the lemma, the two block minimizers are $-\gamma_A W_A$ and $-\gamma_B W_B$, where
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  W_A &=
  C_A^{-1/2}
  \polar\!\left(C_A^{-1/2}G_AQ^{-1/2}\right)
  Q^{-1/2},\\
  W_B &=
  P^{-1/2}
  \polar\!\left(P^{-1/2}G_BC_B^{-1/2}\right)
  C_B^{-1/2}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:factor-directions}
\end{equation}
Thus the block problems determine the descent directions $-W_A$ and $-W_B$.

\paragraph{Magnitude.}
It remains to choose the step magnitude. Directly using $\gamma_A$ and $\gamma_B$ as step sizes would tie the magnitude to the LMO geometry rather than to the merged-weight update. Under an equivalent rescaling of the LoRA factors, the same fixed step size for $A$ and $B$ can make the merged update arbitrarily large (see \Cref{lem:factor-radius-gauge}). We instead set the magnitude by directly bounding the first-order merged update $U$ in spectral norm by a target step size $\eta$. By the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity,
\begin{equation}
\norm{U}_2
=
\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2
\le
\norm{B}_2\norm{\Delta A}_2
+
\norm{A}_2\norm{\Delta B}_2 .
\label{eq:ours-prodcap}
\end{equation}
Treating $A$ and $B$ symmetrically, set $\norm{\Delta A}_2=\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho$. Choosing
\begin{equation}
\rho = \frac{\eta}{\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2}
\label{eq:ours-rho}
\end{equation}
ensures $\norm{U}_2\le\eta$, and gives the updates
\[
\Delta A = -\rho\,\frac{W_A}{\norm{W_A}_2},
\qquad
\Delta B = -\rho\,\frac{W_B}{\norm{W_B}_2}.
\]
\paragraph{Implementation.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ours} summarizes the resulting optimizer step. In the implementation, the direction formula \eqref{eq:factor-directions} is applied to Nesterov momentum in place of the raw factor gradients. We estimate the spectral norms by power iteration (see \Cref{alg:smax}) and use damping in the curvature inverse square roots (see \Cref{app:damping}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\methodname{} step for one LoRA pair $(A,B)$}
\label{alg:ours}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Hyperparameters:} learning rate $\eta$, momentum decay $\beta_1$, curvature decay $\beta_2$
\Statex
\Phase{Nesterov Momentum}
\State $M_A\gets\beta_1 M_A+(1-\beta_1)G_A \qquad M_B\gets\beta_1 M_B+(1-\beta_1)G_B$ \Comment{momentum buffer}
\State $\widehat M_A\gets\beta_1M_A+(1-\beta_1)G_A \qquad \widehat M_B\gets\beta_1M_B+(1-\beta_1)G_B$ \Comment{Nesterov look-ahead}
\Statex
\Phase{Direction}
\State $C_A\gets B^\top P B \qquad C_B\gets A Q A^\top$
\State $W_A\gets C_A^{-1/2}\,\polar\!\big(C_A^{-1/2}\widehat M_AQ^{-1/2}\big)\,Q^{-1/2}$ \Comment{whiten--polar--unwhiten~\eqref{eq:factor-directions}}
\State $W_B\gets P^{-1/2}\,\polar\!\big(P^{-1/2}\widehat M_BC_B^{-1/2}\big)\,C_B^{-1/2}$
\Statex
\Phase{Magnitude}
\State $\rho\gets\eta/(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$ \Comment{spectral-norm control~\eqref{eq:ours-rho}}
\State $A\gets A-\rho\,W_A/\norm{W_A}_2 \qquad B\gets B-\rho\,W_B/\norm{W_B}_2$
\Statex
\Phase{Metric Update}
\State $Q\gets\beta_2 Q+(1-\beta_2)\diag\!\big(G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A\big)/r$ \Comment{coupled estimator~\eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling}}
\State $P\gets\beta_2 P+(1-\beta_2)\diag\!\big(G_B C_B^{-1}G_B^\top\big)/r$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Comparison.} 
Across every comparison only the optimizer and its learning rate vary. We set the LoRA factor $\alpha$ to the rank $r$, in accordance with best practice \cite{loralearnsless, raschka}, which lets us ignore the $\alpha/r$ factor in \eqref{eq:lora}. We apply LoRA to every linear layer except the output layer, and use the standard initialization that sets $B = 0$ and $A$ random. For each method we sweep the learning rate and select the value with the lowest held-out eval loss, under a constant schedule with no weight decay. \Cref{app:exp} gives the base models, datasets, and full configuration.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare \methodname{} (\Cref{sec:setup}) to the following optimizers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Adam} \cite{adam} --- our baseline, applied independently to the two factors $A$ and $B$.
  \item \textbf{Muon} \cite{muon} --- the per-factor Muon update~\eqref{eq:naive}.
  \item \textbf{iMuon} \cite{imuon} --- the spectral LMO~\eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient} applied to a momentum in the product space.\footnote{Even with momentum off, iMuon's codebase update (their Appendix K) is not the decoupled step of \Cref{prop:decoupled} (their Corollary 4.1), as we show in \Cref{app:memoryless}.}
\end{itemize}

\section{Prior Work}
\label{sec:related}

\rob{Some pieces of prior work mixed in with experimental setup?}
\emph{Same operator, three independent incarnations.} The memoryless limit of LoRA-RITE \cite{lorarite} (moment accumulation stripped), iMuon's decoupled form \cite{imuon}, and the concurrent LoRA-Muon \cite{loramuon} all realize \Cref{prop:decoupled} (\Cref{app:memoryless}); \cite{loramuon} proves this equivalence. Our ablation runs this shared update's \emph{direction} as the both-controls-removed arm, with the step magnitude pinned by the unit-spectral-norm rescale of \Cref{alg:ours} (\Cref{sec:exp}); the unpinned update's factor step is uncapped ($\propto1/\sigma_{\min}$ of the partner).
\rob{end pieces}


  
\paragraph{LoRa and optimization of low rank factors.}
LoRA finetuning freezes the pretrained weight matrix and trains a low-rank update
$\DW=(\alpha/r)BA$ through the factors $A$ and $B$ \cite{lora}.
Adam is a standard optimizer choice applied independently to the two
factors \cite{adam}.  
This is a simple and memory efficient strategy, however it introduces a problem since it
optimizes the parameterization rather than the product.
Specifically, the same update
$\DW$ can be represented by $(A,B)$ or by $(NA,BN^{-1})$, while a
factor-wise adaptive optimizer need not give the same update to $\DW$.
Several prior work address this issue indirectly by changing
the parameterization, initialization, or scalar learning rates.  
LoRA+
uses different learning rates for $A$ and $B$ \cite{loraplus};
rank-stabilized LoRA changes the $\alpha/r$ scaling to improve
high-rank behavior \cite{rslora}; $\mu$A and recent LoRA scaling rules
study how learning rates and $\alpha$ should transfer across rank
\cite{mua,loraalpha}.  These works change the scale at which the
factor-based optimizer operates, while \methodname{} keeps the usual
LoRA parameterization and changes the update direction and radius.


\paragraph{Orthogonalized updates.}
Muon~\cite{muon} update is derived as a steepest descent over the spectral norm
ball, in other words spectral descent,
\[
    \Delta W
    =
    \eta\argmin_{\|U\|_2\le 1}\langle G,U\rangle
    =
    -\eta \polar(G),
\]
where $\polar(G)$ is the orthogonal polar factor of $G$ \cite{muon,polarexpress}.
This spectral step controls the worst-case change of the linear operator and
has motivated several spectrally controlled optimizers. 
When Muon is applied separately to LoRa factors, spectral gradient flow exhibits a distinctive spectral phenomenon, 
``equal-rate'' growth of singular values, explaining why factor-wise
orthogonalization can behave very differently from ordinary gradient
flow \cite{kang_specgf}.  Spectron instead uses orthogonalization and
adaptive renormalization to stabilize native low-rank pretraining by
controlling the spectral norm of the induced weight update
\cite{spectron}.  These methods motivate spectral update, but they do
not by themselves provide a product-aware optimizer.  \methodname{} uses the same polar
primitive, but applies it inside a LoRA-aware curvature metric and
then rescales the resulting factor step by a product-level operator-norm
radius.


\paragraph{Curvature and second-moment preconditioning.}
Adam and Adafactor use diagonal or factored second moments
\cite{adam,adafactor}.  Shampoo extends this idea to matrix and tensor
parameters by approximating full matrix Adagrad with Kronecker-factored
matrix
\cite{shampoo}; SOAP improves Shampoo by applying Adam in the
Shampoo preconditioner eigenbasis \cite{soap}.  
Further, a recent KL perspective
interprets Shampoo and SOAP as structured covariance-estimation
procedures and motivates KL-Shampoo/KL-SOAP \cite{klshampoo}, which is
related to our metric estimator.

Curvature-aware spectral optimizers combine this second-moment geometry
with Muon-style orthogonalization.  SOAP-Muon first whitens with a SOAP
preconditioner and then applies Muon~\cite{soapmuon}.  Mousse~\cite{mousse} formulates
a dense full-rank update as a spectral steepest descent step in a
Shampoo-induced anisotropic trust region,
\[
    \Delta W
    =
    -L^{-1/4}
      \polar\left(L^{-1/4} G R^{-1/4}\right)
      R^{-1/4},
\]
where $L$ and $R$ are Kronecker second-moment factors.
\methodname{} is the LoRA member of this family.  Its whiten-polar-
unwhiten step has the same form, but the curvature seen by a LoRA pair
collapses to the small matrices
\[
    C_A=B^\top P B,\qquad C_B=AQA^\top ,
\]
together with coupled diagonal side factors $P$ and $Q$.  Thus it keeps the
spectral-curvature direction, while avoiding dense $d\times d$
preconditioners and their eigenbases.



\paragraph{Product-space and invariant LoRA optimizers.}
The product $BA$ is invariant to the change of coordinates
\[
    BA=(BN^{-1})(NA),
\]
for any invertible matrix $N\in \mathbb{R}^{r\times r}$,
so an optimizer should ideally act on the product $BA$, not on the
individual factors.
LoRA-RITE \cite{lorarite} is prior work that
makes this invariance explicit.  
This rank-side preconditioning cancels the
change of basis $(A,B)\mapsto(NA,BN^{-1})$, making the induced update to
$BA$ transformation-invariant.
Its full
algorithm also includes transported first and second moments and an
escaped-mass correction.

Riemannion/RiemannLoRA \cite{riemannion} is also prior work, but it is a
different member of the same product-space family.  It removes the
factorization ambiguity by optimizing $BA$ as a point on the
fixed-rank manifold.  In particular, it forms a Riemannian gradient in
the tangent space, applies a Muon-like orthogonalization to that whole
tangent vector, and then retracts back to the manifold.  This is not the
same as the decoupled factor update below: Riemannion acts on one
tangent vector, whereas the LoRA-RITE/iMuon/LoRA-Muon core solves two
half-product subproblems.

The interpretation of the invariant factor update as a spectral
steepest-descent step, or linear minimization oracle (LMO), is
concurrent work.  iMuon \cite{imuon} gives a general intrinsic-LMO
framework for matrix manifolds, including the fixed-rank manifold.
LoRA-Muon \cite{loramuon} derives the LoRA specialization directly and
observes that the simplified QR-coordinate core of LoRA-RITE implements
the same spectral update.  Our use of the LMO viewpoint is concurrent
with these works; the underlying moment-free update is not new.

In the memoryless spectral limit, LoRA-RITE, iMuon and LoRA-Muon reduce to the same update
\[
  \Delta A
  =
  -\eta\,S_B^{-1/2}\,
  \polar\!\left(S_B^{-1/2}G_A\right),
  \qquad
  \Delta B
  =
  -\eta\,
  \polar\!\left(G_BS_A^{-1/2}\right)S_A^{-1/2},
\]
with $S_A=AA^\top$, $S_B=B^\top B$, $G_A=B^\top G$, and
$G_B=GA^\top$.  Equivalently, this is the solution of the two half-product subproblems
\eqref{eq:imuon-lmo}.


This shared update controls the two linearized product contributions,
$\|B\Delta A\|_2\le \eta$ and $\|\Delta B A\|_2\le \eta$,
and is invariant at the product level.  It does not, however, control
the raw factor steps:
\[
    \|\Delta A\|_2
    \sim
    \frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\min}(B)},
    \qquad
    \|\Delta B\|_2
    \sim
    \frac{\eta}{\sigma_{\min}(A)} .
\]
Thus the memoryless spectral core is singular at the standard LoRA
initialization $B=0$ unless one uses damping, warmup, or nonzero factor
initialization.  \methodname{} uses this shared invariant spectral core
as the baseline direction, but adds two separate controls: a coupled
two-sided curvature metric,
\[
    C_A=B^\top P B,\qquad C_B=AQA^\top ,
\]
which changes the polar direction, and an explicit operator-norm radius
for the merged update step,
\begin{equation} \label{e-rho-eta-adapter-stepsize}
    \rho=\frac{\eta}{\|A\|_2+\|B\|_2},
    \qquad
    \|B\Delta A+\Delta B A\|_2\le \eta .
\end{equation}
The first control changes the geometry of the direction; the second
stabilizes the factor step from the standard $B=0$ initialization.


\paragraph{Product-step magnitude control.}
Several recent works argue that the important quantity is not only the
norm of a parameter update, but the change induced in a product of
trained matrices.  Spectron controls the spectral norm of updates in
native low-rank factorized weights \cite{spectron}.  QuacK controls
changes to attention logits by scaling the learning rates of query and
key weights by the norm of the partner matrix, e.g.,
\[
    \eta_Q\propto \|W_K\|^{-1},
    \qquad
    \eta_K\propto \|W_Q\|^{-1},
\]
so a large key matrix slows the query update and vice versa
\cite{quack}.  \methodname{} applies the same principle to the product $BA$ in \eqref{e-rho-eta-adapter-stepsize}, but with a spectral rather than Frobenius radius.
This radius is separate from the curvature metric used to choose the
polar direction.  It makes the step magnitude independent of factor
conditioning and allows the optimizer to start from the usual $B=0$
initialization without warmup or nonzero factor initialization.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\methodname{} carries the fast part of spectral, curvature-aware optimization to
LoRA cheaply:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item A polar step in a coupled two-sided \emph{diagonal} curvature metric, sized by a per-factor operator-norm radius.
  \item $1.2$--$1.7\times$ fewer steps than Adam across nine settings; trains from $B{=}0$; one learning rate across rank.
  \item The expensive Kronecker curvature and inner refinement it is usually bundled with add nothing.
  \item $\le 5\%$ per-step wall, ${\approx}\tfrac12$ Adam's state --- the step speedup survives in wall-clock.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral norm linear minimization]\label{lem:muon-lmo}
For $G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $\eta>0$,
\[
    \min_{\|X\|_2\leq \eta}\langle G,X\rangle
    =
    -\eta\|G\|_{\operatorname{nuc}},
\]
and
\[
    -\eta\polar(G)
    \in
    \argmin_{\|X\|_2\leq \eta}\,\langle G,X\rangle .
\]
Consequently,
\[
    W_t-\eta\polar(G_t) \in \argmin_{\|W-W_t\|_2\leq\eta}
    \langle G_t,W-W_t\rangle.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By duality of the spectral and nuclear norms, every feasible $X$ satisfies
\[
    \langle G,X\rangle
    \geq
    -\|G\|_{\operatorname{nuc}}\|X\|_2
    \geq
    -\eta\|G\|_{\operatorname{nuc}}.
\]
Let $G=U\Sigma V^\top$ be a reduced singular value decomposition.  The
choice
\[
    X=-\eta UV^\top=-\eta\polar(G)
\]
is feasible and satisfies
\[
    \langle G,X\rangle
    =
    -\eta\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma)
    =
    -\eta\|G\|_{\operatorname{nuc}}.
\]
It therefore attains the lower bound.  
Substituting $X=W-W_t$ gives the
stated update.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Pseudogradient constraint under a Kronecker ansatz]
\label{lem:pseudogradient-spectral}
Let $U\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$, let
$P\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and
$Q\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be positive definite, and set
$\Sigma=Q\otimes P$. Define
\[
\Gamma=\{bx^\top:\mathrm{vec}(bx^\top)^\top\Sigma^{-1}\mathrm{vec}(bx^\top)\le1\}.
\]
Then
\[
  \sup_{bx^\top\in\Gamma}\left|\ip{bx^\top,U}\right|
  =\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Using the column-major vectorization convention,
$\mathrm{vec}(bx^\top)=x\otimes b$. Therefore
\[
\mathrm{vec}(bx^\top)^\top(Q\otimes P)^{-1}\mathrm{vec}(bx^\top)
=(x^\top Q^{-1}x)(b^\top P^{-1}b).
\]
Substitute $b=P^{1/2}u$ and $x=Q^{1/2}v$. The constraint becomes
$\norm{u}_2^2\norm{v}_2^2\le1$, and the objective becomes
\[
  \left|b^\top Ux\right|
  =\left|u^\top P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}v\right|.
\]
Taking the supremum over $\norm{u}_2\norm{v}_2\le1$ gives
$\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_2$ by the definition of the spectral norm.
\end{proof}


\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}]
Set $Y=LXR$, so $X=L^{-1}YR^{-1}$. The constraint becomes
$\norm{Y}_2\le\gamma$, and the objective becomes
\[
\left\langle M,X\right\rangle
=
\left\langle L^{-1}MR^{-1},Y\right\rangle .
\]
By spectral--nuclear duality, this is minimized over $\norm{Y}_2\le\gamma$ by
\[
Y=-\gamma\,\polar\!\left(L^{-1}MR^{-1}\right).
\]
Substituting back gives the stated solution.
\end{proof}


\begin{lemma}[Fixed factor radii under LoRA rescaling]
\label{lem:factor-radius-gauge}
Let $B_c=cB$ and $A_c=c^{-1}A$ for $c>0$, so that $B_cA_c=BA$. With $P,Q$ and $G$ fixed, the normalized directions obtained from \eqref{eq:factor-directions} are unchanged by this rescaling. If $\widehat W_A=W_A/\norm{W_A}_2$ and $\widehat W_B=W_B/\norm{W_B}_2$, then a fixed factor radius $\rho>0$ gives
\[
U_c
=
-\rho\left(cB\widehat W_A+c^{-1}\widehat W_BA\right).
\]
If either $B\widehat W_A\ne0$ or $\widehat W_BA\ne0$, then
$\sup_{c>0}\norm{U_c}_2=\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Under the rescaling, $G_{A,c}=B_c^\top G=cG_A$ and
$C_{A,c}=B_c^\top PB_c=c^2C_A$, so the polar argument defining $W_A$ is unchanged and $W_{A,c}=c^{-1}W_A$. Hence $W_{A,c}/\norm{W_{A,c}}_2=W_A/\norm{W_A}_2$. Similarly, $G_{B,c}=GA_c^\top=c^{-1}G_B$ and $C_{B,c}=A_cQA_c^\top=c^{-2}C_B$, so $W_{B,c}=cW_B$ and the normalized $B$ direction is also unchanged. Therefore the fixed-radius first-order update is the displayed $U_c$. If $B\widehat W_A\ne0$, then $\norm{U_c}_2/c\to\rho\norm{B\widehat W_A}_2$ as $c\to\infty$. If $B\widehat W_A=0$ but $\widehat W_BA\ne0$, then $c\norm{U_c}_2\to\rho\norm{\widehat W_BA}_2$ as $c\to0$. In either case the supremum is infinite.
\end{proof}



\DecoupledFactorStep*
\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:decoupled}]
Consider the problem w.r.t. the $A$-subproblem, and define $\Delta A := A - A_t$ and $Z:=S_B^{1/2}\Delta A$.
By the definition of the spectral norm,
\[
\|B_t \Delta A\|_{2}
= \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(\Delta A^\top S_B \Delta A)}
= \|S_B^{1/2} \Delta A\|_{2}
=\|Z\|_2.
\]
Moreover, since $B_t$ has full column rank, $S_B\succ 0$,
and
\[
\langle G_A, \Delta A \rangle
= \langle S_B^{-1/2}G_A, S_B^{1/2} \Delta A\rangle
= \langle S_B^{-1/2}G_A, Z\rangle.
\]
Combining the above, the $A$-subproblem is equivalent to
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\|Z\|_2 \leq \eta_A}{\mbox{minimize}}&
\langle S_B^{-1/2}G_A, Z \rangle.
\end{array}
\]
By \Cref{lem:muon-lmo}, one update is
\[
  \Delta A=S_B^{-1/2}Z=-\eta_A S_B^{-1/2}\polar \big(S_B^{-1/2}G_A\big).
\]
We assume $S_B^{-1/2}G_A\neq 0$, then its polar factor has spectral norm one.
Consequently,
\[
  \|B_t\Delta A\|_2
  = \left\|S_B^{1/2}\Delta A\right\|_2
  =\eta_A \left\| \polar \big(S_B^{-1/2}G_A\big)\right\|_2
  = \eta_A.
\]

The argument for the  $B$-update is symmetric.

\end{proof}

\section{Memoryless Limits of LoRA-RITE and iMuon}
\label{app:memoryless}
LoRA-RITE \cite{lorarite} and iMuon \cite{imuon} both reduce to the decoupled factor step of
\Cref{prop:decoupled} once their moment accumulation is removed. We show this below. The one
caveat is iMuon: it is the \emph{momentum-free} algorithm that collapses, while the shipped
momentum variant does not (last paragraph) --- the code-versus-theory gap discussed in
\Cref{sec:related}.

\paragraph{Setup.} As in \Cref{sec:method}: $G=\partial L/\partial(BA)$ is the product
gradient, $G_A=B^\top G$ and $G_B=GA^\top$ the factor gradients, $S_A=AA^\top$ and
$S_B=B^\top B$ the $r\times r$ Grams. Both derivations use one fact --- the first half of a
matrix's polar decomposition is its polar factor: for full-column-rank $M=U\Sigma V^\top$,
\begin{equation}
  M\,(M^\top M)^{-1/2}=UV^\top=\polar(M).
  \label{eq:polar-id}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{LoRA-RITE.} (LoRA-RITE writes the two factors in the opposite order; we state its
update directly for our $A,B$.) Its Alg.~1 adds three pieces of memory to a base step:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item an EMA first moment (decay $\beta_1$);
  \item an accumulated second moment, transported across the (changing) factor basis each step;
  \item an ``escaped-mass'' floor added to that second moment.
\end{itemize}
Dropping all three --- $\beta_1{=}0$, and the second moment taken as the single-step value
with no transport and no floor --- is the authors' momentum-free update (their Eq.~(14)),
which in our notation reads
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta B=-\eta\,\widehat G_B\,(\widehat G_B^\top\widehat G_B)^{-1/2}\,S_A^{-1/2},
  \qquad \widehat G_B:=G_B S_A^{-1/2}
\end{equation*}
($\widehat G_B$ is the factor gradient $G_B$ whitened on the right by the other factor's Gram root $S_A^{-1/2}$).
With no accumulation the middle factor is a polar map by \eqref{eq:polar-id}, so
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta B=-\eta\,\polar(\widehat G_B)\,S_A^{-1/2}
  =-\eta\,\polar\!\big(G_B S_A^{-1/2}\big)\,S_A^{-1/2},
\end{equation*}
the $B$-update of \Cref{prop:decoupled}; the $A$-update is the mirror.

\paragraph{iMuon, momentum-free.} iMuon uses our orientation ($X=BA$). Its fixed-rank
intrinsic spectral LMO (their Cor.~4.1) maps any ambient matrix
$H\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ to the factor update
\begin{equation}
  \Delta B(H)=\polar\!\big(HA^\top S_A^{-1/2}\big)S_A^{-1/2},\qquad
  \Delta A(H)=S_B^{-1/2}\polar\!\big(S_B^{-1/2}B^\top H\big).
  \label{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient}
\end{equation}
The base algorithm (Alg.~1, no momentum) feeds the raw gradient $H=G$, so $HA^\top=G_B$ and
$B^\top H=G_A$ --- and \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient} is \Cref{prop:decoupled} verbatim.

\paragraph{iMuon, with momentum.} The shipped momentum form (their App.~K) does \emph{not}
feed the gradient to \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient}. It first combines the two factor
look-aheads $\tilde G_A=G_A+\beta m_A$ and $\tilde G_B=G_B+\beta m_B$ (buffers $m_A,m_B$,
decay $\beta$) into a single ambient matrix, then feeds that:
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M=\tilde G_B A+B\tilde G_A .
\end{equation*}
Turning momentum off ($\beta{=}0$) removes the buffers but not the recombination:
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M=G_B A+B G_A=G\,A^\top A+BB^\top G\ \neq\ G .
\end{equation*}
Feeding $\widehat M$ through \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient} (using $G_A A^\top=B^\top G_B$),
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M A^\top=G_B S_A+(BB^\top)G_B
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Delta B(\widehat M)=\polar\!\big(G_B S_A^{1/2}+(BB^\top)G_B S_A^{-1/2}\big)S_A^{-1/2},
\end{equation*}
which carries an extra term $(BB^\top)G_B$ that the decoupled factor step
$\Delta B=\polar(G_B S_A^{-1/2})S_A^{-1/2}$ of \Cref{prop:decoupled} does not ($\Delta A$ is symmetric).
So switching momentum off does not recover \Cref{prop:decoupled}; that needs the recombination
dropped as well, leaving the momentum-free base algorithm. \Cref{prop:decoupled} is the
memoryless limit of iMuon's \emph{theory} (Cor.~4.1), not of its shipped optimizer.

\section{Diagonal--Kronecker Second-Moment Estimator for the Curvature Diagonals}
\label{app:matnormal}
This derives the estimator \eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling} and its coupling
\eqref{eq:ours-closure} from the second-moment model \eqref{eq:ours-sigma} alone --- no
distributional assumption on $G$.

\paragraph{Model and moment identities.} Under the diagonal--Kronecker ansatz,
$\Sigma=\E[\mathrm{vec}(G)\mathrm{vec}(G)^\top]=Q\otimes P$, equivalently
$\E[G_{ij}G_{kl}]=P_{ik}Q_{jl}$. The only consequence we use is the pair of
moment identities, obtained by contracting that entrywise form: for any
$M\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and
$N\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$,
\begin{equation}
  \E[G\,M\,G^\top]=\operatorname{tr}(MQ)\,P,\qquad
  \E[G^\top N\,G]=\operatorname{tr}(NP)\,Q.
  \label{eq:moment-id}
\end{equation}
$P,Q$ are fixed only up to scale, $(P,Q)\mapsto(cP,c^{-1}Q)$, which the norm
\eqref{eq:ours-metric} and the operator-norm rescale absorb.

\paragraph{Coupling: covariance of a linear image.} Each factor gradient is a linear
image of $G$, so its row second moment is the partner side conjugated by the factor:
\begin{equation*}
  \mathrm{Cov}\,\mathrm{vec}(G_A)=(I\otimes B^\top)(Q\otimes P)(I\otimes B)
  =Q\otimes\underbrace{B^\top P B}_{C_A},\qquad C_B=A\,Q\,A^\top,
\end{equation*}
which is \eqref{eq:ours-closure}. The partner $C_A$ carries the current $P$ and $C_B$ the
current $Q$; that shared dependence is the coupling between the sides.

\paragraph{Estimator: method of moments.} Put $N=B\,C_A^{-1}B^\top$ in the second
identity of \eqref{eq:moment-id} and use the trace collapse
$\operatorname{tr}(B C_A^{-1}B^\top P)=\operatorname{tr}(C_A^{-1}B^\top P B)=\operatorname{tr}(C_A^{-1}C_A)=r$:
\begin{equation*}
  \E\!\big[\tfrac1r\,G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A\big]
  =\E\!\big[\tfrac1r\,G^\top(B C_A^{-1}B^\top)G\big]
  =\tfrac1r\operatorname{tr}(B C_A^{-1}B^\top P)\,Q=Q,
\end{equation*}
so $\tfrac1r\,G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A$ is unbiased for $Q$, and its diagonal is the $q_j$ of
\eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling}. The mirror with $M=A^\top C_B^{-1}A$ and
$\operatorname{tr}(A^\top C_B^{-1}A\,Q)=\operatorname{tr}(C_B^{-1}C_B)=r$ gives
$\E[\tfrac1r\,G_B C_B^{-1}G_B^\top]=P$. Setting $C_A=C_B=I$ decouples the sides into two
independent Adafactor diagonals \cite{adafactor}. In running ($\beta_2$-EMA) form, the
two unbiased estimators give the per-coordinate coupled update
\begin{equation}
  q_j=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r\,(G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}\Big],\qquad
  p_i=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r\,(G_B)_{i,:}\,C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top\Big],
  \label{eq:ours-klcoupling}
\end{equation}
the diagonals of $Q$ and $P$ applied in \Cref{alg:ours}.

\paragraph{Relation to KL-Shampoo.} The alternation --- each diagonal estimated under the
partner's current value, one EMA step per iteration --- is the coupled two-sided
estimator of \cite{klshampoo}, run on the factor gradients $G_A,G_B$ rather than the
dense gradient (their Table~2 lists this diagonal--Kronecker form as moment matching).

\section{Spectral-Norm Estimation}
\label{app:smax}
\Cref{alg:smax} estimates every spectral norm $\norm{\cdot}_2$ in \Cref{alg:ours}. The floor at the larger row/column $\ell_2$ norm (both lower-bound $\smax$) guards against under-estimation: a cold or stale power iteration can under-estimate $\smax$, and an under-estimate at the rescale $W/\norm{W}_2$ inflates the step past $\rho$ and can diverge.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Guarded spectral-norm estimate of $\norm{M}_2$}
\label{alg:smax}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require matrix $M$; warm-start vector $v$ (cached across steps); iterations $K$
\Statex

\State $\ell\gets\max\!\big(\max_i\norm{M_{i,:}}_2,\ \max_j\norm{M_{:,j}}_2\big)$
\Statex

\For{$k=1,\dots,K$} \Comment{power iteration on $M^\top M$}
  \State $w\gets Mv$
  \State $v\gets M^\top w \,/\, \norm{M^\top w}_2$
\EndFor
\Statex

\State \Return $\max\!\big(\norm{Mv}_2,\ \ell\big)$ \Comment{$\ell$ floor}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Matrix-Sign and Inverse-Sqrt via Gram Newton--Schulz}
\label{app:gramns}
Both spectral primitives of \Cref{alg:ours} --- the matrix sign $\polar(\cdot)$ (the
orthogonal polar factor, \Cref{sec:method}) and the curvature inverse-sqrt $C^{-1/2}$ ---
run as one degree-$5$ Newton--Schulz iteration on the $r\times r$ Gram matrix, sharing a
single coefficient family and using no eigendecomposition (\Cref{alg:gramns}).

\paragraph{Coefficients.} Each iteration applies the odd quintic
$p_t(x)=a_t x+b_t x^3+c_t x^5$, the minimax polynomial that pushes the singular values
toward $1$; composing the $p_t$ carries the whole spectrum to $1$. These are the
PolarExpress coefficients \cite{polarexpress}, computed once at startup and reused at
every step and call site.

\paragraph{Gram iteration.} Since $r\ll d$, both maps iterate on the $r\times r$ Gram
$R$ rather than the rectangular factor \cite{gram_ns} --- about $d/r$ cheaper. With
accumulator $Q$, each step applies
\begin{equation*}
  M_t=a_t I+b_t R+c_t R^2,\qquad Q\gets M_t Q,\qquad R\gets M_t R\,M_t .
\end{equation*}
Both maps run this same iteration, and the accumulator $Q$ is the product of the $M_t$;
they differ only in the seed $R_0$ and what is returned:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Matrix sign} $\polar(X)$ --- seed $R$ from the Gram of the normalized input, return $Q\tilde X$.
  \item \textbf{Inverse-sqrt} $C^{-1/2}$ --- seed $R$ from the normalized, damped curvature (\Cref{app:damping}), return a rescaled $Q$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Stability and precision.} The coefficients grow quickly across iterations,
so a spurious negative eigenvalue of $R$ can blow up; a restart at iteration $\tau$
re-anchors $R$ from the current iterate \cite{gram_ns}. The iteration runs in fp32 ---
bf16 diverges near the damping floor --- which is cheap on $r\times r$ matrices.
Production uses $K{=}8$ iterations.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Gram Newton--Schulz (shared by $\polar$ and the inverse-sqrt)}
\label{alg:gramns}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Gram/curvature $R_0$ (SPD, $r\times r$); coefficients $\{(a_t,b_t,c_t)\}_{t=1}^{K}$; restart $\tau$
\State $R\gets R_0$;\quad $Q\gets I$
\For{$t=1,\dots,K$}
  \If{$t=\tau{+}1$} \State $R_0\gets Q R_0 Q^\top$;\quad $R\gets R_0$;\quad $Q\gets I$ \Comment{re-anchor} \EndIf
  \State $M\gets a_t I+b_t R+c_t R^2$
  \State $Q\gets M Q$;\quad $R\gets M R M$
\EndFor
\State \Return $Q$ \Comment{$\to$ polar factor (post-multiply $\tilde X$), or inverse-sqrt (post-scale)}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Relative Damping}
\label{app:damping}
Each curvature inverse-sqrt in \Cref{alg:ours} is relative-damped: for a curvature factor $X$ with top eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}$,
\begin{equation*}
  X^{-1/2}\ \longmapsto\ \big(X/\lambda_{\max}+\delta I\big)^{-1/2},\qquad \delta>0 .
\end{equation*}
The normalization and the floor each follow from the step's structure.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Normalize by $\lambda_{\max}$.} The whitened polar depends only on the curvature's \emph{shape}, not its scale: $\polar\!\big(C_A^{-1/2}\hat M_A Q^{-1/2}\big)$ is invariant under $X\mapsto cX$, and the operator-norm rescale $W_A/\norm{W_A}_2$ reabsorbs any global factor. The scale-free representative is the normalized spectrum $X/\lambda_{\max}\in(0,1]$.
  \item \emph{Floor by $\delta$.} On that spectrum $\delta$ is one conditioning knob: it caps the metric's condition number at ${\sim}1/\delta$, so a near-null curvature direction cannot amplify the step without bound.
  \item \emph{Warmup.} A still-zero factor ($\lambda_{\max}=0$) maps to $I$, so steps before the EMAs warm up are plain momentum.
\end{itemize}

\section{FLOP Overhead of the Optimizer}
\label{app:flops}
The wall measurement (\Cref{sec:exp-walltime}) is hardware-specific; the FLOP count is
its architecture-only counterpart, and shows the same near-free result.

\paragraph{Cost per step.} With the base weights frozen, a LoRA step skips the
base-parameter-gradient pass, so the forward/backward costs four matmul-equivalents per
parameter per token (versus six for full fine-tuning):
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{fb}}=4N\,n_{\mathrm{tok}},
  \label{eq:flop-fb}
\end{equation}
with $N$ the (base $+$ LoRA) parameter count and $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ the tokens per step. The
optimizer touches only the factors $A,B$ and is independent of $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$. Per pair its step
(\Cref{alg:ours,alg:gramns}) is a fixed set of matmuls of two shapes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{$r\times d$ matmuls --- six per factor}, each costing $2r^2d$: the partner
        Gram, the momentum whiten, the polar's Gram and its application, the un-whiten,
        and the flip-flop curvature solve.
  \item \emph{$r\times r$ matmuls}: the two polars and two inverse-sqrts, each a
        $K$-step Gram--Newton--Schulz whose iteration is four $r\times r$ products
        ($R^2$, $MQ$, $MR$, $(MR)M$), so $8r^3$ per iteration.
\end{itemize}
Summing both factors gives the leading-order cost
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{opt}}=\sum_{\text{pairs}}\Big[
    \underbrace{12\,r^2(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})}_{6\ \text{matmuls/side}\,\times\,2}
    +\underbrace{32\,K\,r^3}_{4\ \text{calls}\,\times\,8Kr^3}\Big],
  \label{eq:flop-opt}
\end{equation}
every term $r\times r$ or $r\times d$ ($r\ll d$), eigh-free. $C_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is a
fixed per-step cost, so the fraction $C_{\mathrm{opt}}/C_{\mathrm{fb}}\propto1/n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ shrinks
as the batch grows: at the production setting ($r = 256$, $n_{\mathrm{tok}}{=}32768$) it is under $1\%$
on every base model, far below the measured ${\le}5\%$ wall (\Cref{sec:exp-walltime}).
The optimizer's overhead is thus latency-bound on its many small $r\times r$ kernels, not
FLOP-bound; the FLOP fraction is the floor the wall approaches as the batch grows. The ${\le}4\%$ wall uses the gram-form matrix-sign, the polar on the $r{\times}r$ Gram. Running it instead on the rectangular $d{\times}r$ factor is ${\approx}1.5\times$ costlier in the optimizer step, raising the per-step overhead to $5$--$7\%$.

\section{Gauge Sensitivity and Factor Self-Balancing}
\label{app:gauge}
The radius is not gauge-invariant: under $(A,B)\mapsto(cA,c^{-1}B)$, $\norm{A}_2{+}\norm{B}_2$ is minimized (largest step) at $\norm{A}_2{=}\norm{B}_2$, so an imbalanced factorization changes the trajectory --- the concern raised for Spectron-style radii \cite{loramuon}.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Self-balancing.} Under \methodname{} the factors balance: $\norm{B}_2/\norm{A}_2$ rises from the $B{=}0$ start toward $1$ and holds (\Cref{fig:gauge}), so the radius runs near its balanced optimum throughout training.
  \item \emph{Tightens with rank.} The balance is closest to $1$ at high rank ($r = 256$) and looser at low rank ($r = 32$).
\end{itemize}
