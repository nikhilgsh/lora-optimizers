\section{Online Curvature Estimation}
\label{app:matnormal-04}
\Cref{alg:ours} stores raw curvature vectors $p,q$ and uses their normalized
versions
\[
  P=\diag(p/\norm{p}_\infty),
  \qquad
  Q=\diag(q/\norm{q}_\infty)
\]
inside the small closures $B^\top P B$ and $AQA^\top$ that score factor
gradients. The question in this appendix is when those normalized vectors can be
read as the diagonal curvature shapes predicted by the Kronecker moment model.

The useful conclusion is conditional and a posteriori. If the replay moment at
step $t$ has the diagonal-Kronecker block form, then any positive definite
closure held fixed across the weighted sum recovers the model diagonal after
infinity-norm normalization. This invariance is exactly why the fixed-fit
calculation alone does not justify the implemented EMA. The implemented
recurrence scores each factor gradient once, with the closure and LoRA factors
available at that step, so its normalized state is certified by the fixed-fit
model only when the exact residual ratios below are small.

\subsection{The Online Quantity}
\label{sec:weighted-estimation-04}

\paragraph{Curvature weights.}
Let $G_{s,i}(W)=\nabla\ell_{x_{s,i}}(W)$ be the gradient of sample $i$ from
step $s$ evaluated at weights $W$. The observed batch gradient is
$G_s=n_s^{-1}\sum_iG_{s,i}(W_s)$, and $g_s=\vec(G_s)$. The observed LoRA factor
gradients are
\[
  G_{A,s}=B_s^\top G_s\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}},
  \qquad
  G_{B,s}=G_sA_s^\top\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}.
\]
For comparison with the current-factor moment model, also define the
current-factor replay gradients
\[
  \widetilde G_{A,t,s}=B_t^\top G_s,
  \qquad
  \widetilde G_{B,t,s}=G_sA_t^\top .
\]
The curvature recurrence uses the geometric weights
\[
  \EMA_{s\leq t}[\phi_s]
  :=
  \sum_{s\leq t}w_{t,s}\phi_s,
  \qquad
  w_{t,s}=(1-\beta_2)\beta_2^{t-s}.
\]
Only the curvature input is relevant in this subsection: it is the
second-moment matrix in the outer-gradient constraint that determines the
diagonal metric used by the update. If old samples could be recomputed at the
current weights, that curvature input would be
\[
  \overline\Sigma^{\mathrm{ideal}}_t
  =
  \sum_{s\le t}\frac{w_{t,s}}{n_s}\sum_{i=1}^{n_s}
  \vec(G_{s,i}(W_t))\vec(G_{s,i}(W_t))^\top .
\]
The online estimator never forms this moment. The sequence of objects used in
the rest of the section is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item Replace the current-weight per-sample moment by the observed
  batch-gradient moment
  \[
    \overline\Sigma_t
    =
    \EMA_{s\leq t}[g_sg_s^\top].
  \]
  This is the stale-gradient and minibatch second-moment approximation.
  \item Map the dense observed moment to replay factor-gradient moments using
  the current LoRA factors $A_t,B_t$ inside the weighted sum.
  \item Model the resulting factor-gradient moments by diagonal Kronecker
  blocks.
  \item Apply a fixed-closure diagonal fit to those factor-gradient moments.
  \item Compare that fixed-closure replay fit with the stored EMA through the
  exact residual ratios in \Cref{lem:online-decomp-04}.
\end{enumerate}
The first three steps are modeling approximations. The fourth is the
fixed-closure fitting calculation. The fifth is an exact online reduction.

\paragraph{Curvature observations.}
Given the raw vectors $p_t,q_t$, \Cref{alg:ours} forms
\[
  P_t=\diag\!\bigl(p_t/\norm{p_t}_\infty\bigr),
  \qquad
  Q_t=\diag\!\bigl(q_t/\norm{q_t}_\infty\bigr),
\]
then builds the rank-$r$ closures
\[
  C_{A,t}=B_t^\top P_tB_t,
  \qquad
  C_{B,t}=A_tQ_tA_t^\top .
\]
These closures are the small matrices used to score a factor-gradient matrix
coordinatewise. Let $K^A_t,K^B_t$ be the symmetric scoring matrices that appear
in that score:
\[
  \hat q_t
  =
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(G_{A,t}^\top K^A_tG_{A,t}\right),
  \qquad
  \hat p_t
  =
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(G_{B,t}K^B_tG_{B,t}^\top\right).
\]
In the idealized displayed algorithm, $K^A_t=C_{A,t}^{-1}$ and
$K^B_t=C_{B,t}^{-1}$. With relative damping, $K^A_t$ and $K^B_t$ are the
damped inverse matrices specified by that implementation. The online argument
below compares the scoring matrices actually used at old steps with the scoring
matrix used at step $t$.

At step $t$, the optimizer forms one curvature observation from the current
factor gradients and updates the raw curvature vectors,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ours-klcoupling-04}
\begin{aligned}
  q_{t+1}
  &=
  \beta_2q_t+(1-\beta_2)\hat q_t,
  &
  p_{t+1}
  &=
  \beta_2p_t+(1-\beta_2)\hat p_t,
  \\
  Q_{t+1}
  &=
  \diag\!\bigl(q_{t+1}/\norm{q_{t+1}}_\infty\bigr),
  &
  P_{t+1}
  &=
  \diag\!\bigl(p_{t+1}/\norm{p_{t+1}}_\infty\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The proposition below compares the stored EMA with a replay comparator that
uses the current factors and current scoring matrix in every term:
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t
  &:=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \sum_{s\le t}w_{t,s}
    \widetilde G_{A,t,s}^\top K^A_t\widetilde G_{A,t,s}
  \right),
  \\
  \widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t
  &:=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \sum_{s\le t}w_{t,s}
    \widetilde G_{B,t,s}K^B_t\widetilde G_{B,t,s}^\top
  \right).
\end{aligned}
\]

\begin{lemma}[Online certificate]\label{lem:online-decomp-04}
For any realized trace with observed gradients, replay gradients, and
step-local scoring matrices defined as above, suppose
$\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t$ and $\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t$ are nonzero.
The stored vectors in~\eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling-04} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:online-current-closure-04}
\begin{aligned}
  q_{t+1}
  &=
  \beta_2^{t+1}q_0+\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t+d^A_t+f^A_t,
  \\
  p_{t+1}
  &=
  \beta_2^{t+1}p_0+\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t+d^B_t+f^B_t .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here the scoring-matrix residuals are
\[
\begin{aligned}
  d^A_t
  &=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \sum_{s\le t}w_{t,s}
    G_{A,s}^\top(K^A_s-K^A_t)G_{A,s}
  \right),
  \\
  d^B_t
  &=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \sum_{s\le t}w_{t,s}
    G_{B,s}(K^B_s-K^B_t)G_{B,s}^\top
  \right),
\end{aligned}
\]
and the factor-map residuals are
\[
\begin{aligned}
  f^A_t
  &=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \sum_{s\le t}w_{t,s}
    \bigl[
      G_{A,s}^\top K^A_tG_{A,s}
      -
      \widetilde G_{A,t,s}^\top K^A_t\widetilde G_{A,t,s}
    \bigr]
  \right),
  \\
  f^B_t
  &=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \sum_{s\le t}w_{t,s}
    \bigl[
      G_{B,s}K^B_tG_{B,s}^\top
      -
      \widetilde G_{B,t,s}K^B_t\widetilde G_{B,t,s}^\top
    \bigr]
  \right).
\end{aligned}
\]
Let
\[
  e^A_t=\beta_2^{t+1}q_0+d^A_t+f^A_t,
  \qquad
  e^B_t=\beta_2^{t+1}p_0+d^B_t+f^B_t,
  \qquad
  \rho^A_t=\frac{\norm{e^A_t}_\infty}{\norm{\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}_\infty},
  \qquad
  \rho^B_t=\frac{\norm{e^B_t}_\infty}{\norm{\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}_\infty}.
\]
If $\rho^A_t,\rho^B_t\le1/2$, then
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \left\|
  \frac{q_{t+1}}{\norm{q_{t+1}}_\infty}
  -
  \frac{\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}{\norm{\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}_\infty}
  \right\|_\infty
  &\le 4\rho^A_t,
  \\
  \left\|
  \frac{p_{t+1}}{\norm{p_{t+1}}_\infty}
  -
  \frac{\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}{\norm{\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}_\infty}
  \right\|_\infty
  &\le 4\rho^B_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Unrolling the raw recurrence in~\eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling-04}, adding and
subtracting the current-scoring observed-gradient sums, and then adding and
subtracting the current-scoring replay-gradient sums gives
\eqref{eq:online-current-closure-04}. The normalized bounds use the elementary
inequality
\[
  \left\|
  \frac{x+e}{\norm{x+e}_\infty}
  -
  \frac{x}{\norm{x}_\infty}
  \right\|_\infty
  \le
  4\frac{\norm{e}_\infty}{\norm{x}_\infty},
  \qquad
  \norm{e}_\infty\le\frac12\norm{x}_\infty .
\]
\end{proof}

The lemma is the online certificate. It does not replace the EMA by the replay
fit through an informal slow-drift statement. It gives the exact terms that
separate them: $d^A_t,d^B_t$ measure scoring-matrix drift, while
$f^A_t,f^B_t$ measure the change from old factor gradients to current-factor
replay gradients. The ratios $\rho^A_t,\rho^B_t$ can be evaluated from a trace
that stores these quantities. In a theoretical reading, small ratios are the
explicit condition under which the fixed-closure model below transfers to the
stored EMA.

\subsection{The Fixed-Closure Fit}
\label{sec:fixed-closure-comparator-04}

\paragraph{Moment model.}
The fixed-closure term in~\eqref{eq:online-current-closure-04} is a weighted fit to
replay factor gradients. The modeling step assumes that the observed dense
moment from the sequence above has normalized diagonal Kronecker form
\begin{equation}\label{e-diag-kron-second-mom-model-04}
  \overline\Sigma_t
  =
  \kappa_t(Q^\star_t\otimes P^\star_t),
  \qquad
  \norm{\diag P^\star_t}_\infty
  =
  \norm{\diag Q^\star_t}_\infty
  =
  1 .
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa_t>0$ carries the magnitude, while $P^\star_t,Q^\star_t$ carry the
coordinate shape. The proposition treats the induced replay block form as exact
so that the statement isolates the online error rather than adding a separate
modeling-error term.

\paragraph{Factor-gradient block form.}
Use the current LoRA factors in the factor-gradient map before forming the
weighted factor-gradient moments. The vectorization identity on the A side is
\[
  \vec(B_t^\top G_s)=(I\otimes B_t^\top)g_s ,
\]
with the analogous identity on the B side. Therefore the Kronecker model in
\eqref{e-diag-kron-second-mom-model-04} implies
\begin{align}
  \Sigma_{A,t}
  &:=
  \EMA_{s\leq t}
  \big[\vec(\widetilde G_{A,t,s})
       \vec(\widetilde G_{A,t,s})^\top\big]
  \approx
  \kappa_t\,Q^\star_t\otimes C^\star_{A,t},
  \nonumber\\
  \Sigma_{B,t}
  &:=
  \EMA_{s\leq t}
  \big[\vec(\widetilde G_{B,t,s}^\top)
       \vec(\widetilde G_{B,t,s}^\top)^\top\big]
  \approx
  \kappa_t\,P^\star_t\otimes C^\star_{B,t},
  \label{eq:observable-moments-04}
\end{align}
where
\[
  C^\star_{A,t}=B_t^\top P^\star_tB_t,
  \qquad
  C^\star_{B,t}=A_tQ^\star_tA_t^\top .
\]
Thus the A-side factor-gradient moment has diagonal shape $Q^\star_t$ and
closure block $C^\star_{A,t}$, while the B-side moment has diagonal shape
$P^\star_t$ and closure block $C^\star_{B,t}$. This is the fixed-closure target
to which the online recurrence is compared.

\paragraph{Fixed-closure fit.}
Following KL-Shampoo~\cite{klshampoo}, the positive definite full-moment case
motivates the fixed-closure fit by minimizing the log-determinant divergence
\[
  D(\Sigma,S)=\Tr(\Sigma S^{-1})-\log\det(\Sigma S^{-1})-d
\]
over matrices of common dimension $d$. For the $A$ side, writing
$(\Sigma_A)_{jj}$ for the $j$th $r\times r$ diagonal block, the terms depending
on $q_j$ are
\[
  q_j^{-1}\Tr\!\left((\Sigma_A)_{jj}\widehat C_A^{-1}\right)
  +
  r\log q_j .
\]
The first-order condition gives
$\hat q_j=r^{-1}\Tr((\Sigma_A)_{jj}\widehat C_A^{-1})$, and similarly for the
$B$ side. We use this first-order expression as the fixed-closure fit for the
empirical weighted replay-gradient moments:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:factor-fit-04}
\begin{aligned}
  \hat q(\widehat C_A)
  &=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \EMA_{s\leq t}[
      \widetilde G_{A,t,s}^\top
      \widehat C_A^{-1}
      \widetilde G_{A,t,s}]
  \right),
  \\
  \hat p(\widehat C_B)
  &=
  \frac1r\diag\!\left(
    \EMA_{s\leq t}[
      \widetilde G_{B,t,s}
      \widehat C_B^{-1}
      \widetilde G_{B,t,s}^\top]
  \right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
This formula remains well-defined once the closures
$\widehat C_A,\widehat C_B$ are positive definite or damped, even when the
empirical outer-product moment is singular.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-closure scalar invariance]\label{lem:closure-invariance-04}
Suppose the factor-gradient moments have the exact block form
\[
  \Sigma_A=\kappa\,Q^\star\otimes C^\star_A,
  \qquad
  \Sigma_B=\kappa\,P^\star\otimes C^\star_B,
\]
where the diagonal entries of $P^\star,Q^\star$ are positive,
$\norm{\diag P^\star}_\infty=\norm{\diag Q^\star}_\infty=1$, and
$C^\star_A,C^\star_B\succ0$. For any positive definite closures
$\widehat C_A,\widehat C_B$, the fits in~\eqref{eq:factor-fit-04} satisfy
\[
  \hat q(\widehat C_A)
  =
  a\,\diag(Q^\star),
  \qquad
  \hat p(\widehat C_B)
  =
  b\,\diag(P^\star)
\]
for positive scalars $a,b$. Hence the normalized fitted vectors equal
$\diag(Q^\star)$ and $\diag(P^\star)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $j$th block of $\Sigma_A$ is $\kappa q^\star_jC^\star_A$, while the
$j$th block of $\diag(q)\otimes\widehat C_A$ is $q_j\widehat C_A$. The
log-determinant objective separates across blocks, and each block is minimized
by the best scalar multiple of $\widehat C_A$ for
$\kappa q^\star_jC^\star_A$. Thus
\[
  \hat q_j
  =
  \kappa q^\star_j
  \frac{\Tr(C^\star_A\widehat C_A^{-1})}{r}.
\]
The multiplier is independent of $j$, and the normalization removes it. The
$B$ side is identical.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequence.}
The lemma says that, once the weighted factor-gradient moment has the block form
in~\eqref{eq:observable-moments-04}, any positive definite closure held fixed
across the weighted sum changes only an overall scalar on each side. The
infinity-norm normalization removes that scalar in the fixed-closure model. It
does not identify a unique closure for the replay fit. A fixed identity closure
would have the same scalar-invariance property in this idealized calculation.
The learned online recurrence is justified here only through
\Cref{lem:online-decomp-04}: scoring-matrix drift and factor-map drift appear in
the residual ratios $\rho^A_t,\rho^B_t$.

\begin{proposition}[Online recovery certificate]\label{prop:online-recovery-04}
For a realized trace, assume the replay-gradient moments at step $t$ have the
exact block form
\[
  \Sigma_{A,t}
  =
  \kappa_t\,Q^\star_t\otimes C^\star_{A,t},
  \qquad
  \Sigma_{B,t}
  =
  \kappa_t\,P^\star_t\otimes C^\star_{B,t},
\]
with the normalizations in~\eqref{e-diag-kron-second-mom-model-04}. Also assume
the current scoring matrices $K^A_t,K^B_t$ are positive definite, and let
$\widehat C_A=(K^A_t)^{-1}$ and $\widehat C_B=(K^B_t)^{-1}$. If the residual
ratios in \Cref{lem:online-decomp-04} satisfy $\rho^A_t,\rho^B_t\le1/2$, then
the normalized online curvature state satisfies
\[
\begin{aligned}
  \norm{\diag(Q_{t+1})-\diag(Q^\star_t)}_\infty
  &\le 4\rho^A_t,
  \\
  \norm{\diag(P_{t+1})-\diag(P^\star_t)}_\infty
  &\le 4\rho^B_t .
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
With $\widehat C_A=(K^A_t)^{-1}$ and
$\widehat C_B=(K^B_t)^{-1}$, the fixed-closure fit in
\eqref{eq:factor-fit-04} is exactly
$\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t,\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t$.
\Cref{lem:closure-invariance-04}
therefore gives
$\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t/
 \norm{\widetilde q^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}_\infty=\diag(Q^\star_t)$ and
$\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t/
 \norm{\widetilde p^{\mathrm{cur}}_t}_\infty=\diag(P^\star_t)$.
\Cref{lem:online-decomp-04} transfers these identities to the stored vectors
with errors at most $4\rho^A_t$ and $4\rho^B_t$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The section gives a certificate, not a stationarity theorem. The fixed-closure
fit explains why the replay moment has the desired normalized diagonal factors
under the block model. The online lemma then names the two ways the implemented
EMA can fail to inherit that conclusion. The scoring-matrix terms
$d^A_t,d^B_t$ are where the use of old closures enters. The factor-map terms
$f^A_t,f^B_t$ are where the use of old LoRA factors enters. The initialization
tail, these scoring-matrix terms, and these factor-map terms must be small
relative to the replay fit. Under that condition,
\Cref{prop:online-recovery-04} justifies using the stored normalized $p,q$ as
the model curvature shapes. Without that condition, the fixed-fit calculation
alone makes no online tracking claim.
