LoRA \cite{lora} freezes the pretrained weight $W_0$ and trains a rank-$r$ update
$\DW=(\alpha/r)\,BA$, $A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$,
$B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. The optimizer that trains it is the
under-examined lever; practice defaults to Adam on the factors $A,B$. We introduce
\methodname{}, a spectral-polar LoRA optimizer that reaches Adam's eval loss in
$1.2$--$1.7\times$ fewer steps across nine (model, dataset, rank) settings.

\paragraph{Motivation.} Naive Muon (\Cref{lem:muon-lmo}) and iMuon (\Cref{prop:decoupled}) are the same spectral LMO under the Euclidean inner product, differing only in what they cap. \methodname{} keeps a spectral LMO for the \emph{direction} but measures it in a learned two-sided metric, and sets the \emph{magnitude} by a separate rule. Three stages:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.7em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item[(A)] \emph{Curvature.} Estimate diagonal $P,Q$ online as the Kronecker factors of the gradient second moment (coupled diagonal second moment matching \cite{klshampoo}; \eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling}).
  \item[(B)] \emph{Direction.} Spectral-norm LMO of the loss in that metric --- a whiten--polar--unwhiten step \eqref{eq:factor-directions}.
  \item[(C)] \emph{Magnitude.} Discard the LMO budget and bound the merged update in spectral norm by $\eta$~\eqref{eq:ours-prodcap}.
\end{itemize}
Stages (A) and (B) are separate problems --- estimation, then a step under the \emph{frozen} estimate --- composed by alternation, not one objective.

\section{Spectral Geometry with LoRA}
\label{sec:method}
% NOT A PURE LMO: the metric P,Q is a coupled diagonal KL flip-flop (estimation,
% not an LMO), and the MAGNITUDE is the operator-norm pin rho=eta/(smax A+smax B)
% (a separate rule, not the LMO budget tau). Keep those three ingredients distinct.
% Prose is terse here by design (author expands); the equations are the content.
% Citation keys (verify in references.bib): kang_specgf, imuon, spectron,

\label{sec:method-ours}
Consider a single linear layer with frozen pretrained weight
$
W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}.
$
Following LoRA~\cite{lora}, we finetune this layer by learning a low rank update
\begin{equation}
\DW = \frac{\alpha}{r}\,BA, \qquad
B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times r}, \quad
A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\mathrm{in}}},
\label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
of rank \(r\) with scaling \(\alpha\). To simplify the derivation, we suppress the scaling \(\alpha/r\) so that the merged weight $W = W_0 + BA$. This simplification matches our experimental setting in \Cref{sec:exp}, where $\alpha = r$. The goal is to learn factors $A$ and $B$ that solve
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{A, B}{\mbox{minimize}}&
\mathcal{L}(W_0 + BA),
\end{array}
\]
where $\mathcal{L}$ is the task loss. Our loss will be the average over a training set $\mathcal{L}(W) = \E_{x}\left[ \ell_x(W)\right]$ where $\ell_x(W)$ denotes the loss over input $x$ and parameters $W$.

\paragraph{Spectral Descent.}
Inspired by the success of Muon~\cite{muon,carlson2015preconditioned}, we develop our method using a Linear Minimization Oracle (LMO).  For now,  we ignore the particular low rank structure of our parameters, and consider $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ with associated gradient $G = \nabla_W \mathcal{L}(W)$. 
The LMO approach minimizes the local linearization of our loss subject to a ball constraint, that is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, \Delta W \right\rangle \\[2pt]
  \mbox{subject to} &
  \displaystyle  
  \left\| \Delta W   \right\|
  \le \tau,
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:lmo}
\end{equation}
where $\|\cdot\|$ is a chosen norm. Given a solution $\Delta W$ to problem~\eqref{eq:lmo}, we then update our parameter according to 
\[
W \leftarrow W + \Delta W.
\]
% Since the LMO is homogeneous, $\eta$ only sets the scale of its solution. We use the solution as the update direction. Later we will choose its magnitude separately so that the merged update has a controlled spectral norm.

To define a method using~\eqref{eq:lmo} we only need to choose a norm. The key idea behind Muon is that we must control the size of the updates to the weight matrices in the spectral norm:
\[ 
\|W\|_2 := \max_{\|x\|_2 =1} \|W x \|_2,
\]
where $x\in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and $\|x\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}} x_i^2}$ is the standard Euclidean norm.
The spectral norm is a conservative norm over linear operators, since it focuses on the \emph{worst-case} possible input $x$, that will give the largest output. Thus constraining against a large change in the spectral norm, can lead to more stable training and feature learning~\cite{yang2023spectral,bernstein2025modular}.

\paragraph{A new linear minimization oracle.} Inspired by this conservative view of Muon and the spectral norm, we develop a new pseudo-norm that is conservative with respect to a batch of incoming activations. Let $\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}$ be a batch of input activations to the layer that $W$ encodes, and let $G_i$ be the gradient associated with $x_i$, that is, with a slight abuse of notation $G_i = \nabla \ell_{x_i}(W)$, where the abuse comes from using $\ell_{x_i}$ to denote the loss with respect to this activation (as opposed to an input).

To be conservative with respect to every activation, we enforce that no single activation results in a large change in the loss, that is we want
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss-diff}
     \ell_{x_i}(W +\Delta W) - \ell_{x_i} (W)\leq \tau, \qquad  i=1,\ldots, n.
\end{equation} 
This would ensure stable training with no spikes or jumps in the loss. \nikhil{is this really true? because on a disjoint batch the loss might spike?}
\rob{But on the next batch, we would again enforce this for that batch. So we would never observe a significant spike in the loss, if we could actually implement the above.}
But the loss is highly non-linear and non-convex, so instead we use the local linearization of the loss given by
\[  
\ell_{x_i}(W +\Delta W) -\ell_{x_i} (W)  \approx \langle G_i, \Delta W \rangle. 
\]
Using the above and~\eqref{eq:loss-diff} in the LMO~\eqref{eq:lmo} we arrive at
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, \Delta W\right\rangle \\
  \mbox{subject to} &
  \displaystyle  
\left\langle G_i, \Delta W \right\rangle 
  \le \tau, \qquad i=1,\ldots, n.
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:sample-lmo}
\end{equation}
Since we do not have efficient direct access to the per-input gradients $G_i$, we will now define a \emph{outergradient} set $\mathcal{G}$ such that $G_i \in \mathcal{G}$,
use the following inequality
\begin{equation} \label{eq:outgrad1}
    \max_{i=1,\ldots, n}  \left\langle G_i, \Delta W \right\rangle  
    \leq   \max_{\tilde G \in \mathcal{G}} \left\langle \tilde G, \Delta W \right\rangle,
\end{equation} 
and then impose  that the right hand side of \eqref{eq:outgrad1} 
is less than $\tau$. 
To build $\mathcal{G}$ we collect some standard properties of the gradient matrices in the following lemma.
\begin{restatable}[Properties of gradients]{lemma}{gradprop} 
  \label{lem:gradprop}
    Let $g_i=\mathrm{vec}(G_i) \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ for all $i=1, \ldots, n$,
    and $\Sigma := \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n g_i g_i^\top$. It follows that the gradients satisfy
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item {\bf Leverage bound:} $ \dotprod{\Sigma^\dagger g_i, g_i} \leq n$, \\[-0.5cm]
        \item {\bf Rank one:} $G_i = b_i x_i^\top$,
        \end{enumerate}
    for some $x_i \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and $b_i \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$.
\end{restatable}

The above properties suggest the following outergradient set
\begin{equation} 
\label{eq:outgrad} 
\mathcal{G}(\Sigma) := \left \{ \tilde G = b x^\top ~\bigg |~  
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}},
    \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}, \
\dotprod{\Sigma^\dagger \mathrm{vec}(\tilde G), \mathrm{vec}(\tilde G)} \leq n \right \}.
\end{equation}
By~\Cref{lem:gradprop} we have that $G_i \in \mathcal{G}$, thus we can use this $\mathcal{G}$ in our upper bound~\eqref{eq:outgrad1}.
The last remaining issue is that $\Sigma$ is too large of a matrix to store or use directly. To address this issue we replace $\Sigma$ with a structured approximation.

\paragraph{Kronecker approximation.}
Following the Kronecker-factored approximations used by Shampoo~\cite{shampoo, klshampoo}
and KFAC~\cite{kfac}, let $P\in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and $Q  \in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}} $ be symmetric positive definite matrices such that
     \begin{equation} \label{eq:pq-factor}
     \Sigma  \approx Q \otimes P.\end{equation}
     Using the factorization above, if we use our outergradient set~\eqref{eq:outgrad} to relax the constraint in~\eqref{eq:sample-lmo} through~\eqref{eq:outgrad1}, then we arrive at the following method
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, \Delta W\right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mbox{subject to} &
  \displaystyle  
\max_{\tilde G \in \mathcal{G}(Q \otimes P)}\left\langle \tilde G, \Delta W \right\rangle 
  \le \tau.
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:polorafull-hard}
\end{equation}
Fortunately the above can be simplified. By~\Cref{lem:pseudogradient-spectral} the constraint in~\eqref{eq:polorafull-hard} can be re-written using the identity 
\[ 
\max_{G \in \mathcal{G}(Q \otimes P)}\left\langle G, \Delta W \right\rangle  
= \sqrt{n} \| P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}\|_2.
\]
Then problem~\eqref{eq:polorafull-hard} can be re-written as 
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G, \Delta W\right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mathrm{subject~ to} &
\displaystyle  
\|P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}\|_2 \leq \tilde \tau,
\end{array}
\label{eq:polorafull}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde\tau = \frac{\tau}{\sqrt{n}}$.
This problem can be cast as an instance of the following Preconditioned LMO.
\begin{restatable}[Preconditioned spectral-norm LMO]{lemma}{GeneralLmoUpdate}
\label{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}
Let $L$ and $R$ be symmetric positive definite. Then
\begin{equation}\label{e-gen-spectral-lmo-update}
X^\star = -\tau L^{-1}
\polar\left(L^{-1}MR^{-1}\right) R^{-1}
\end{equation}
is a solution of
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\|LXR\|_2\leq\tau}{\mathrm{minimize}}&
\langle M,X\rangle.
\end{array}
\]
Moreover, $X^\star$ is the unique minimizer of
$\|LXR\|_F$ over the solution set.
\end{restatable}
Applying the lemma above with $(L,R)=(P^{1/2},Q^{1/2})$, a solution to \eqref{eq:polorafull} is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polorafull-update}
    \Delta W =  -\tilde \tau P^{-1/2} \polar(P^{-1/2} G Q^{-1/2}) Q^{-1/2}.
\end{equation} 


\paragraph{Direction.}
So far we have described an update for the full weight matrix $W \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$. We now adapt this update to LoRA~\eqref{eq:lora}, computing its \emph{direction} and its \emph{magnitude} separately. The spectral LMO gives the direction, and we then set the magnitude by bounding the merged update $\Delta W$ in spectral norm. If we update LoRA factors according to
\[
A \leftarrow A + \Delta A,
\qquad
B \leftarrow B + \Delta B,
\]
then the first-order approximation of the total update is
\[
\Delta W = (B + \Delta B)(A + \Delta A) - BA \approx B \Delta A + \Delta B A.
\]
If we replace $\Delta W$ in \eqref{eq:polorafull} with the approximation above then we get the problem
\begin{equation}
    \begin{array}{ll}
    \underset{\Delta A,\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
    \left\langle G, B\Delta A+\Delta B A \right\rangle \\[2pt]
    \mbox{subject to} &
    \norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_2
    \le \tilde\tau.
    \end{array}
    \label{eq:joint-direction-lmo}
  \end{equation}
The constraint in \eqref{eq:joint-direction-lmo} couples $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ inside a single spectral norm. Subadditivity of the spectral norm bounds it by the two factor steps separately,
\[
\norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_2
\leq \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2
+ \norm{P^{1/2} \Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2,
\]
so bounding each factor step bounds the merged update. We therefore impose one spectral-norm constraint per factor,
\[
\norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau_A,
\qquad
\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau_B ,
\]
which splits the problem into two independent subproblems, one per factor.
If we denote the factor gradients as 
\begin{equation}\label{e-grad-wrt-factors}
G_A=B^\top G, \qquad G_B=GA^\top,
\end{equation}
then the problem \eqref{eq:joint-direction-lmo} decouples into two block subproblems
\begin{equation}
\setlength{\arraycolsep}{2pt}
\begin{array}{@{}c@{\qquad}c@{}}
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta A}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G_A,\Delta A \right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mbox{subject to} &
\norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau_A ,
\end{array}
&
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G_B,\Delta B \right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mbox{subject to} &
\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau_B .
\end{array}
\end{array}
\label{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo}
\end{equation}
Define the $r\times r$ matrices
\begin{equation}
C_A = B^\top P B,
\qquad
C_B = A Q A^\top .
\label{eq:ours-closure}
\end{equation}
By definition of the spectral norm, and using the above definitions of $C_A$ and $C_B$,  the left hand side of the two constraints in \eqref{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo} can be re-written as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:collapse-constraint}
\norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2=\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2,
\qquad
\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2=\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B C_B^{1/2}}_2 .
\end{equation}
Using~\eqref{eq:collapse-constraint} in~\eqref{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo} it is now clear that each of the two subproblems is an instance of \Cref{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}, with $(L,R)=(C_A^{1/2},Q^{1/2})$ for the $\Delta A$ subproblem and $(L,R)=(P^{1/2},C_B^{1/2})$ for the $\Delta B$ subproblem. Thus the two minimizers are $-\tau_A W_A$ and $-\tau_B W_B$, where
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  W_A &=
  C_A^{-1/2}\,
  \polar\!\left(C_A^{-1/2}\,G_A\,Q^{-1/2}\right)
  \,Q^{-1/2},\\
  W_B &=
  P^{-1/2}\,
  \polar\!\left(P^{-1/2}\,G_B\,C_B^{-1/2}\right)
  \,C_B^{-1/2}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:factor-directions}
\end{equation}
The minimizers scale linearly with the budgets, $\Delta A=-\tau_A W_A$ and $\Delta B=-\tau_B W_B$, so $\tau_A$ and $\tau_B$ set only the magnitude while the directions $W_A$ and $W_B$ are fixed independently of them. We therefore leave the budgets free and set the step magnitude directly through the learning rate $\eta$.

\paragraph{Magnitude.}
%\rob{This commented out text is not clear enough. I think it add more confusion}
% It remains to choose the step magnitude. 
% Using the LMO budgets $\tau_A$ and $\tau_B$ directly would tie the magnitude to the LMO geometry rather than to the merged-weight update. 
% Under an equivalent rescaling of the LoRA factors, a fixed per-factor magnitude can make the merged update arbitrarily large (\Cref{lem:factor-radius-gauge}).
We will now set the magnitude for updating each factor with two constraints in mind: our first constraint is that the linear approximation of the total update $\Delta W$ is bounded in  spectral norm by a user specified learning rate $\eta$; our second constraint is that the update for each factor $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ have the same magnitude in spectral norm.
By the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity,
\begin{equation}
\norm{\Delta W}_2
\approx
\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2
\le
\norm{B}_2\norm{\Delta A}_2
+
\norm{A}_2\norm{\Delta B}_2 .
\label{eq:ours-prodcap}
\end{equation}

Treating $A$ and $B$ symmetrically, we use a single step size $\rho$ for both factors, setting $\norm{\Delta A}_2=\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho$. Choosing
\begin{equation}
\rho = \frac{\eta}{\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2}
\label{eq:ours-rho}
\end{equation}
ensures $\norm{\Delta W}_2\leq \eta$, and gives the updates
\[
\Delta A = -\rho\,\frac{W_A}{\norm{W_A}_2},
\qquad
\Delta B = -\rho\,\frac{W_B}{\norm{W_B}_2}.
\]
\Cref{app:gauge} examines how the step size $\rho$ depends on the relative scale of $A$ and $B$.
\paragraph{Implementation.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ours} summarizes the resulting optimizer step. In the implementation, the direction formula \eqref{eq:factor-directions} is applied to Nesterov momentum in place of the raw factor gradients. We compute the matrix sign and inverse square roots by Gram Newton--Schulz iterations (\Cref{app:gramns}), estimate the spectral norms by power iteration (\Cref{alg:smax}), and use damping in the curvature inverse square roots (\Cref{app:damping}). For further tractability, we restrict matrices 
$P$ and $Q$ to be diagonal, as in Adafactor \cite{adafactor}. To estimate the diagonal factors $P$ and $Q$ we apply KL-Shampoo's \cite{klshampoo}
coupled second moment equations (\Cref{app:matnormal}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\methodname{} step for one LoRA pair $(A,B)$}
\label{alg:ours}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Hyperparameters:} learning rate $\eta$, momentum decay $\beta_1$, curvature decay $\beta_2$
\Statex
\Phase{Nesterov Momentum}
\State $M_A\gets\beta_1 M_A+(1-\beta_1)G_A, \qquad M_B\gets\beta_1 M_B+(1-\beta_1)G_B$ \Comment{momentum buffer}
\State $\widehat M_A\gets\beta_1M_A+(1-\beta_1)G_A, \qquad \widehat M_B\gets\beta_1M_B+(1-\beta_1)G_B$ \Comment{Nesterov look-ahead}
\Statex
\Phase{Direction}
\State $C_A\gets B^\top\,P\,B, \qquad C_B\gets A\,Q\,A^\top$
\State $W_A\gets C_A^{-1/2}\,\polar \big(C_A^{-1/2}\,\widehat M_A\,Q^{-1/2}\big)\,Q^{-1/2}$ \Comment{whiten--polar--unwhiten~\eqref{eq:factor-directions}}
\State $W_B\gets P^{-1/2}\,\polar \big(P^{-1/2}\,\widehat M_B\,C_B^{-1/2}\big)\,C_B^{-1/2}$
\Statex
\Phase{Magnitude}
\State $\rho\gets\eta/(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$ \Comment{spectral-norm control~\eqref{eq:ours-rho}}
\State $A\gets A-\rho W_A/\norm{W_A}_2, \qquad B\gets B-\rho W_B/\norm{W_B}_2$
\Statex
\Phase{Metric Update}

\section{Prior Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hyperparameter rescaling for LoRA.}
LoRA~\cite{lora} enables parameter-efficient finetuning by freezing the pretrained
weights and training only a low-rank update~\eqref{eq:lora} through factors $A$ and
$B$, typically optimized with Adam~\cite{adam}. Several recent methods refine this
approach by rescaling hyperparameters such as the learning rates, adapter scale, or
initialization variance to account for the low-rank structure. Motivated by large-width
feature-learning arguments, LoRA+~\cite{loraplus} assigns $B$ a larger learning rate
than $A$, and follow-up work~\cite{hayou2024initialization, li2025beyond} extends this
to other initializations. Rank-stabilized LoRA~\cite{rslora} replaces the $\alpha/r$
adapter scale with $\alpha/\sqrt{r}$ to avoid the rank-dependent shrinkage that makes
high-rank adapters hard to train, and $\mu$A~\cite{mua} studies how these
hyperparameters should scale with rank to keep the optimal learning rate stable. In
contrast, \methodname{} changes the update geometry itself, computing factor update
directions in a curvature-aware metric and scaling them to control the induced change
in the merged weight.

\paragraph{Matrix-aware updates.}
Recently, optimizers that operate at the matrix level, rather than treating parameters as
a flattened vector, have attracted attention for their gains over Adam~\cite{liu2025muon,
qiu2026hyperparameter, wen2025fantastic}. Muon~\cite{muon} solves the spectral-norm LMO
for a dense weight, producing an update equal to the polar factor of the momentum
gradient~\cite{polarexpress} (\Cref{lem:muon-lmo}). Shampoo~\cite{shampoo},
SOAP~\cite{soap}, and KL-Shampoo~\cite{klshampoo} estimate the gradient curvature as
Kronecker-factored second moments. Mousse~\cite{mousse} uses such an estimate to
whiten the gradient before a polar step (\Cref{app:related-spectral}). \methodname{} similarly uses an update which applies a polar step to a whitened gradient, however the update is derived by requiring that no single input in the batch change the loss too much. Furthermore, \methodname{} adapts this to LoRA training and adds magnitude control which allows for efficient training without the need for learning rate grafting \cite{shi2023distributed, mousse}.

\paragraph{Product-invariant LoRA optimizers.}
The learned update $BA$ is a product of two matrices, so it is determined only up to the
change of basis $(A,B)\mapsto(NA,BN^{-1})$ for invertible $N$. Since Adam is not invariant
to this transformation, a line of work has proposed LoRA optimizers that are. LoRA-RITE
removes the basis dependence with an adaptive preconditioner~\cite{lorarite}, while Riemannion
optimizes $BA$ directly on the fixed-rank matrix manifold~\cite{riemannion}. iMuon and
LoRA-Muon derive the invariant update from a spectral-norm
LMO~\cite{imuon,loramuon}. As discussed in \Cref{app:memoryless}, in their momentum-free forms the LoRA-RITE, LoRA-Muon,
and iMuon LMO updates all coincide with \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}. \methodname{} does not satisfy this invariance, but it outperforms iMuon in our experiments (\Cref{sec:exp-main}) which leads us to believe that this invariance is not crucial.


\paragraph{Magnitude control for low-rank products.}
The LMO step \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} at the core of methods like LoRA-Muon bounds $\norm{B\Delta A}_2$ and $\norm{\Delta B A}_2$
but does not bound the factor steps $\norm{\Delta A}_2$ and $\norm{\Delta B}_2$, which blow up when $A$ or
$B$ has small singular values. Spectron controls this in low-rank pretraining, rescaling both
steps to spectral norm $\eta/(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2+1)$ (\Cref{app:related-spectral}). QuacK
controls it in attention, scaling the query and key learning rates by the inverse norm of the
other weight. \methodname{} bounds both the factor steps and the merged update in spectral norm.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\methodname{} carries the fast part of spectral, curvature-aware optimization to
LoRA cheaply:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item A polar step in a coupled two-sided \emph{diagonal} curvature metric, with each factor step set to spectral norm $\rho$.
  \item $1.2$--$1.7\times$ fewer steps than Adam across nine settings; trains from $B{=}0$; one learning rate across rank.
  \item The expensive Kronecker curvature and inner refinement it is usually bundled with add nothing.
  \item $\le 5\%$ per-step wall, ${\approx}\tfrac12$ Adam's state --- the step speedup survives in wall-clock.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proofs}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral norm LMO]\label{lem:muon-lmo}
For $G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $\eta>0$,
\[
    \min_{\|X\|_2\leq \eta}\langle G,X\rangle
    =
    -\eta\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}},
\]
and 
\[
X^\star = -\eta\polar(G)
\]
is unique least Frobenius norm minimizer of
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\|X\|_2\leq \eta}{\mathrm{minimize}}&
\langle G,X\rangle.
\end{array}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:muon-lmo}]
By duality of the spectral and nuclear norms, every feasible $X$ satisfies
\[
    \langle G,X\rangle
    \geq
    -\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}}\|X\|_2
    \geq
    -\eta\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}}.
\]
Let $G=U\Sigma V^\top$ be a reduced singular value decomposition.  The
choice
\[
    X=-\eta UV^\top=-\eta\polar(G)
\]
is feasible and satisfies
\[
    \langle G,X\rangle
    =
    -\eta\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)
    =
    -\eta\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}}.
\]
Thus it attains the lower bound, and therefore, is optimal. 
The minimum-norm statement follows from Lemma C.2~\cite{parshakova2026muon}.
\end{proof}



\gradprop*
\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:gradprop}]
Let $g_i=\mathrm{vec}(G_i)$ and $M=[g_1,\ldots,g_n]$. 
Hence $g_i = Me_i$ for every $i =1,\ldots, n$. Consequently
\[
\dotprod{\Sigma^\dagger g_i, g_i} = ng_i^\top (MM^\top)^{\dagger} g_i = n e_i^\top M^\top  (MM^\top)^{\dagger} M e_i  \leq n,
\]
since $M^\top  (MM^\top)^{\dagger} M$ is an orthogonal projector
onto the range on $M^\top$. 
This proves the {\bf Leverage bound}. 

The {\bf Rank one} property follows since $W$ encodes a linear layer $x_i \mapsto W x_i$, and by the chain rule   
\[
\nabla \ell_{x_i}(Wx_i) =  \left. \frac{d }{dz} \ell_{x_i}(z)  
\right|_{z =Wx_i} x_i^\top
=b_i x_i^\top,
\]
where $b_i = \left.\frac{d }{dz} \ell_{x_i}(z)  \right|_{z =Wx_i} $ is the so called backward gradient.
\end{proof} 



\begin{lemma}[Outergradient set under a Kronecker factorization]
\label{lem:pseudogradient-spectral}
Let
$P\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and
$Q\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be symmetric positive definite, 
and let
$\Sigma=Q\otimes P$. 
Then for every $\Delta W\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$,
\[
  \max_{\tilde G \in \mathcal{G}(\Sigma)} \ip{ \tilde G , \Delta W}
  =\sqrt{n}\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta W Q^{1/2}}_2,
\]
where set $\mathcal{G}(\Sigma)$ is defined in \eqref{eq:outgrad}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:pseudogradient-spectral}]
Since $P$ and $Q$ are positive definite,
\[
    \Sigma^\dagger
    =
    \Sigma^{-1}
    =
    Q^{-1}\otimes P^{-1}.
\]
Consider $b x^\top \in \mathcal{G}(\Sigma)$.
Using column-major vectorization,
\[
    \operatorname{vec}(bx^\top)=x\otimes b.
\]
It follows that
\[
\left\langle
    \Sigma^\dagger\operatorname{vec}(bx^\top),
    \operatorname{vec}(bx^\top)
\right\rangle
=
(x\otimes b)^\top
(Q^{-1}\otimes P^{-1})
(x\otimes b)=
(x^\top Q^{-1}x)
(b^\top P^{-1}b).
\]
Let $b=P^{1/2}u$ and $x=Q^{1/2}v$. 
The constraint defining $\mathcal{G}(\Sigma)$ becomes
\[
    \|u\|_2^2\|v\|_2^2\leq n,
\]
and 
\[
\ip{bx^\top, \Delta W}=
 b^\top \Delta W x 
  = u^\top P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}v .
\]
Finally,
\[
\max_{\tilde G  \in\mathcal{G}(\Sigma)}
\langle \tilde G ,U\rangle
=
\max_{\|u\|_2\|v\|_2\leq\sqrt{n}}
u^\top P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}v 
=
\sqrt{n}
\left\|P^{1/2} \Delta WQ^{1/2}\right\|_2.
\]
\end{proof}

\GeneralLmoUpdate*
\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}]
Set $Y=LXR$, so $X=L^{-1}YR^{-1}$. The constraint becomes
$\norm{Y}_2\le\eta$, and the objective becomes
\[
\left\langle M,X\right\rangle
=
\left\langle L^{-1}MR^{-1},Y\right\rangle .
\]
By \Cref{lem:muon-lmo}, unique least Frobenius norm minimizer is
\[
Y=-\eta \polar \left(L^{-1}MR^{-1}\right).
\]
Substituting back gives the stated solution.
\end{proof}



\begin{lemma}[Fixed factor step sizes under LoRA rescaling]
\label{lem:factor-radius-gauge}
Fix $P$, $Q$, and the product gradient $G$, and let
$\widehat W_A=W_A/\norm{W_A}_2$ and $\widehat W_B=W_B/\norm{W_B}_2$ be the
normalized directions from \eqref{eq:factor-directions}. For $c>0$, rescale the
factors by
\[
  B_c=cB,\qquad A_c=c^{-1}A,
\]
which leaves the product fixed, $B_cA_c=BA$. These normalized directions are
invariant under the rescaling, so a common factor step size $\rho>0$ gives the
first-order merged update
\[
  U_c=-\rho\bigl(cB\,\widehat W_A+c^{-1}\widehat W_B A\bigr).
\]
If $B\widehat W_A\ne0$ or $\widehat W_B A\ne0$, then $\sup_{c>0}\norm{U_c}_2=\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The normalized directions are gauge-invariant.} Under the rescaling, the
$A$-block factor gradient and contracted curvature scale as
\[
  G_{A,c}=B_c^\top G=c\,G_A,\qquad
  C_{A,c}=B_c^\top P B_c=c^2C_A,
\]
so the polar argument in \eqref{eq:factor-directions} is unchanged,
\[
  C_{A,c}^{-1/2}G_{A,c}\,Q^{-1/2}=C_A^{-1/2}G_A\,Q^{-1/2}.
\]
Hence $W_{A,c}=c^{-1}W_A$, and normalizing removes the scalar:
$\widehat W_{A,c}=\widehat W_A$. Symmetrically,
\[
  G_{B,c}=G A_c^\top=c^{-1}G_B,\qquad
  C_{B,c}=A_cQA_c^\top=c^{-2}C_B,
\]
give $W_{B,c}=cW_B$ and $\widehat W_{B,c}=\widehat W_B$. With both directions
fixed, the merged update is the stated $U_c$.

\emph{The merged update is unbounded.} The two terms of $U_c$ scale oppositely
in $c$:
\[
  \frac{\norm{U_c}_2}{c}\to\rho\norm{B\widehat W_A}_2\ \ (c\to\infty),
  \qquad
  c\,\norm{U_c}_2\to\rho\norm{\widehat W_B A}_2\ \ (c\to0).
\]
If $B\widehat W_A\ne0$, the first limit forces $\norm{U_c}_2\to\infty$ as
$c\to\infty$; otherwise, if $\widehat W_B A\ne0$, the second forces
$\norm{U_c}_2\to\infty$ as $c\to0$. In either case $\sup_{c>0}\norm{U_c}_2=\infty$.
\end{proof}



\section{Momentum-Free LoRA-RITE, iMuon and LoRA-Muon}
\label{app:memoryless}
LoRA-RITE \cite{lorarite}, iMuon \cite{imuon} and LoRA-Muon \cite{loramuon} 
reduce to the decoupled factor step of
\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} once their moment accumulation is removed. We show this below.


\paragraph{Setup.} As in \Cref{sec:method}, 
let $G=\nabla_W \mathcal{L}(W) \big |_{W=BA}$ be the product
gradient, $G_A=B^\top G$ and $G_B=GA^\top$ be the factor gradients, 
$S_B=AA^\top$ and
$S_A=B^\top B$ be the $r\times r$ Gram matrices.
The decoupled factor step is the solution of the two half-product subproblems
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \Delta A &\in    \argmin_{\Delta A}~ \langle G_A, \Delta A \rangle \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad
\norm{B \Delta A}_2\le\eta \\
\Delta B &\in   \argmin_{\Delta B}~ \langle G_B,  \Delta B \rangle \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad  \norm{\Delta B A}_2\le\eta,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}
\end{equation}
equivalently $\Delta A=-\eta\,S_A^{-1/2}\polar(S_A^{-1/2}G_A)$ and
$\Delta B=-\eta\,\polar(G_BS_B^{-1/2})S_B^{-1/2}$.
We will use the fact that for any matrix $M$ and its reduced SVD $M=U\Sigma V^\top$,
the polar factor equals
\begin{equation}
  M(M^\top M)^{-1/2}=UV^\top=\polar(M).
  \label{eq:polar-id}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{LoRA-RITE.} LoRA-RITE writes the two factors in the opposite order; we state its
update directly for our $A,B$. 
Its Algorithm~1 adds three pieces of memory to a base step:
1) an EMA first moment w.r.t. parameter $\beta_1$;
2) an accumulated second moment, transported across the (changing) factor basis each step;
3) an ``escaped-mass'' floor added to that second moment.
Dropping all three, we get the authors' momentum-free update (their Eq.~(14)),
which in our notation becomes
\[
  \Delta B=-\eta \widehat G_B (\widehat G_B^\top\widehat G_B)^{-1/2} S_B^{-1/2},
  \qquad \widehat G_B:=G_B S_B^{-1/2}.
\]
With no accumulation by \eqref{eq:polar-id} the middle factor is a polar factor, so
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta B=-\eta \polar(\widehat G_B) S_B^{-1/2}
  =-\eta \polar \big(G_B S_B^{-1/2}\big) S_B^{-1/2},
\end{equation*}
the $B$-update of \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}; same arguments hold for the $A$-update.


\paragraph{iMuon, momentum-free.} iMuon uses our order of factors $BA$. Its fixed-rank
intrinsic spectral LMO (their Corollary~4.1) maps any ambient matrix
$H\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ to the factor update
\begin{equation}
  \Delta B(H)=\polar \big(HA^\top S_A^{-1/2}\big)S_A^{-1/2},\qquad
  \Delta A(H)=S_B^{-1/2}\polar \big(S_B^{-1/2}B^\top H\big).
  \label{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient}
\end{equation}
The base algorithm (Algorithm~1, no momentum) uses the raw gradient $H=G$, so $HA^\top=G_B$ and
$B^\top H=G_A$, therefore, \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient} is precisely \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}.

\paragraph{iMuon implementation versus decoupled limit.}
Our experiments benchmark the official implementation of iMuon optimizer from Appendix K of \cite{imuon}. This
optimizer should not be identified with the momentum-free decoupled update in \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} as stated below.

\paragraph{iMuon, with momentum.} In the official implementation with momentum (their Appendix~K) does {not}
use the gradient as input to \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient}. 
It first combines the two factor
look-aheads $\tilde G_A=G_A+\beta m_A$ and $\tilde G_B=G_B+\beta m_B$ (buffers $m_A,m_B$,
w.r.t. parameter $\beta$) into a single ambient matrix, then uses that matrix as an input
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M=\tilde G_B A+B\tilde G_A .
\end{equation*}
Turning momentum off ($\beta=0$) removes the buffers but not the recombination, i.e.,
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M=G_B A+B G_A=G\,A^\top A+BB^\top G\ \neq\ G .
\end{equation*}
Plugging in $\widehat M$ into \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient} and using $G_A A^\top=B^\top G_B$,
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M A^\top=G_B S_B+(BB^\top)G_B
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Delta B(\widehat M)=\polar\!\big(G_B S_B^{1/2}+(BB^\top)G_B S_B^{-1/2}\big)S_B^{-1/2},
\end{equation*}
which is different from the decoupled factor step. The argument for $\Delta A$ is symmetric.
So switching momentum off does not recover \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}; while
\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} is the
memoryless limit of iMuon's theory (Corollary~4.1), 
it is not of its implemented optimizer.



\paragraph{LoRA-Muon.}
LoRA-Muon derives the same direction directly from two decoupled
spectral-norm LMOs. Their momentum-free subproblems Eq. (7) and (8) are
precisely \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}.
Thus LoRA-Muon has the same direction as~\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}; the
factor $1/2$ is only a convention for splitting the product-step
budget between the two factor updates. LoRA-Muon also shows that this
Gram-matrix form is equivalent to the simplified QR-coordinate core of
LoRA-RITE~\cite{loramuon}.



\section{Extended Prior Work: Spectral and Curvature-Aware Optimizers}
\label{app:related-spectral}
We expand the body's placement of \methodname{} among spectral and curvature-aware
optimizers (\Cref{sec:related}).

\paragraph{Mousse and the whitening power.}
Mousse fixes its trust region by a Shampoo curvature $H\approx(R\otimes L)^{1/2}$, with
$L$ and $R$ being the moving averages of $GG^\top$ and $G^\top G$. Whitening by
$H^{1/2}=(R\otimes L)^{1/4}$ gives the dense update
\[
  \Delta W=-L^{-1/4}\,\polar\!\big(L^{-1/4} G R^{-1/4}\big)\,R^{-1/4}.
\]
\methodname{} instead uses the full gradient covariance $\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$ as its
metric, whitening by $P^{-1/2}$ and $Q^{-1/2}$ (\Cref{alg:ours}), an inverse-square-root
rather than Mousse's fourth-root power; the first-order LoRA product map then contracts
these dense factors to $C_A=B^\top P B$ and $C_B=AQA^\top$.

\paragraph{SOAP-Muon.}
SOAP-Muon whitens the gradient with a SOAP preconditioner and then orthogonalizes the
result \cite{soapmuon}.

\paragraph{Kang et al.}
For spectral gradient flow in a simplified matrix-factorization model,
Kang et al.~\cite{kang_specgf} prove that the singular values of the merged adapter
product grow at a nearly uniform rate even though the two factors are orthogonalized
independently.

\paragraph{Spectron.}
Spectron \cite{spectron} bounds the induced product update by rescaling orthogonalized
factor steps with the current factor norms, in native low-rank pretraining. It is close in
spirit to \methodname{}'s magnitude rule but is not curvature-aware and is not derived
from a single LoRA LMO.

\paragraph{Negligibility of the quadratic term.}
Our magnitude rule \eqref{eq:ours-rho} bounds the first-order merged update
$B\Delta A+\Delta B A$ and drops the quadratic term $\Delta B\Delta A$. Dropping
it shifts the step by only $O(\eta)$. The merged update is
\[
  \Delta W = B\Delta A+\Delta B A+\Delta B\Delta A .
\]
With the per-factor cap $\norm{\Delta A}_2,\norm{\Delta B}_2\le\rho$ and
$s=\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2$, the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity give
\[
  \norm{\Delta W}_2 \le \rho s + \rho^2 .
\]
Bounding the full right-hand side by $\eta$ gives the step
\[
  \rho^\star = \bigl(-s+\sqrt{s^2+4\eta}\bigr)/2
  = \frac{\eta}{s}-\frac{\eta^2}{s^3}+O(\eta^3/s^5),
\]
while dropping $\rho^2$ gives our $\rho=\eta/s$. The quadratic term thus shifts
the step by a relative $(\rho-\rho^\star)/\rho=\eta/s^2$. At standard
initialization $A$ has independent entries of variance $\Theta(1/d_{\mathrm{in}})$
and $B=0$, so $s=\norm{A}_2=\Theta(1)$ with high probability. The shift
$\eta/s^2$ is then $O(\eta)$, decreasing as $s$ grows.

\section{Diagonal Kronecker Second Moment Estimator for the Curvature Diagonals}
\label{app:matnormal}
In this section we derive the estimator \eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling} and its coupling
\eqref{eq:ours-closure} from the second moment model \eqref{eq:pq-factor} alone.

\paragraph{Model and moment identities.} 
Let 
$G\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ 
be stochastic merged-weight gradient.
We use the diagonal Kronecker approximation of \eqref{eq:pq-factor} as follows
\[
\Sigma
=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{vec}(G)\operatorname{vec}(G)^\top]
\approx Q\otimes P,
\qquad
P=\diag(p)\succ0,
\quad
Q=\diag(q)\succ0,
\]
where $p\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and $q\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$. Thus
$\mathbb{E}[G_{ij}^2]\approx p_iq_j$.
For the $\Sigma$ to be a covariance model we further assume $\E[G]=0$.
By \eqref{e-grad-wrt-factors} and \eqref{eq:ours-closure},
\[
\operatorname{vec}(G_A)=(I_{d_{\mathrm{in}}}\otimes B^\top)\operatorname{vec}(G),
\qquad \operatorname{vec}(G_B)=(A\otimes I_{d_{\mathrm{out}}})\operatorname{vec}(G),
\]
therefore, covariance model gives
\[
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{vec}(G_A)\operatorname{vec}(G_A)^\top]\approx Q\otimes C_A,
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{vec}(G_B)\operatorname{vec}(G_B)^\top]\approx C_B\otimes P.
\]
Note that the rescaling
$(P,Q)\mapsto(cP,c^{-1}Q)$ leaves  $Q\otimes P$ 
unchanged.

\paragraph{Conditional KL equations.}
Hold $C_A\succ0$ fixed and fit the covariance  of $G_A$ by
$Q\otimes C_A$, with $Q=\operatorname{Diag}(q)$. As in \cite{klshampoo}, up to constants,
we minimize the KL divergence between two Gaussian distributions
to get $q$
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{q}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \frac12\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}
    \left(
        r\log q_j
        +\frac{1}{q_j}
          \mathbb{E}[(G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}]
    \right)
  \end{array}
\]
The objective is strictly convex in $\log q$ and is coordinatewise separable. Applying the
same calculation to $G_B$ gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    q_j
    &=\frac1r\,
      \mathbb{E}[(G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}],
    &&j=1,\ldots,d_{\mathrm{in}},\\
    p_i
    &=\frac1r\,
      \mathbb{E}[(G_B)_{i,:}C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top],
    &&i=1,\ldots,d_{\mathrm{out}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:diag-kron-population}
\end{equation}
For fixed $C_A$ or $C_B$, these are the diagonal specialization of the
conditional two-sided KL-Shampoo equations \cite{klshampoo}. They are coupled
because $C_A$ contains $P$, while $C_B$ contains $Q$.

If diagonal Kronecker model is exact and $C_A,C_B\succ0$, then
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A]=rQ,
    \qquad
    \mathbb{E}[G_BC_B^{-1}G_B^\top]=rP.
    \label{eq:diag-kron-unbiased}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the first identity is
$\operatorname{tr}(C_A^{-1}C_A)Q=rQ$; the second is identical. This proves the
factor $1/r$ in \eqref{eq:diag-kron-population}.

\paragraph{Online update.}
In practice we use exponentially moving average (EMA) with parameter $\beta_2$ to get metric update.
Specifically, 
update \eqref{eq:diag-kron-population} becomes
\begin{equation}
  q_j=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r (G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}\Big],\qquad
  p_i=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r (G_B)_{i,:} C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top\Big],
  \label{eq:ours-klcoupling}
\end{equation}
as outlined in \Cref{alg:ours}.


\paragraph{Relation to KL-Shampoo.} The alternation --- each diagonal estimated under the
partner's current value, one EMA step per iteration --- is the coupled two-sided
estimator of \cite{klshampoo}, run on the factor gradients $G_A,G_B$ rather than the
dense gradient (their Table~2 lists this diagonal Kronecker form as moment matching).



\section{Numerical Primitives for the Step}
\label{app:primitives}

\subsection{Spectral-Norm Estimation}
\label{app:smax}
\Cref{alg:smax} estimates every spectral norm $\norm{\cdot}_2$ in \Cref{alg:ours}. The floor at the larger row/column $\ell_2$ norm (both lower-bound $\smax$) guards against under-estimation: a cold or stale power iteration can under-estimate $\smax$, and an under-estimate at the rescale $W/\norm{W}_2$ inflates the step past $\rho$ and can diverge.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Guarded spectral-norm estimate of $\norm{M}_2$}
\label{alg:smax}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require matrix $M$; warm-start vector $v$ (cached across steps); iterations $K$
\Statex

\State $l \gets\max\big(\max_i\norm{M_{i,:}}_2,\ \max_j\norm{M_{:,j}}_2\big)$
\Statex

\For{$k=1,\dots,K$} \Comment{power iteration on $M^\top M$}
  \State $w\gets Mv$
  \State $v\gets M^\top w /\norm{M^\top w}_2$
\EndFor
\Statex

\State \Return $\max\big(\norm{Mv}_2,\ l\big)$ \Comment{$l$ floor}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Matrix-Sign and Inverse-Sqrt via Gram Newton--Schulz}
\label{app:gramns}
Both spectral primitives of \Cref{alg:ours} --- the matrix sign $\polar(\cdot)$ (the
orthogonal polar factor, \Cref{sec:method}) and the curvature inverse-sqrt $C^{-1/2}$ ---
run as one degree-$5$ Newton--Schulz iteration on the $r\times r$ Gram matrix, sharing a
single coefficient family and using no eigendecomposition (\Cref{alg:gramns}).

\paragraph{Coefficients.} Each iteration applies the odd quintic
$p_t(x)=a_t x+b_t x^3+c_t x^5$, the minimax polynomial that pushes the singular values
toward $1$; composing the $p_t$ carries the whole spectrum to $1$. These are the
PolarExpress coefficients \cite{polarexpress}, computed once at startup and reused at
every step and call site.

\paragraph{Gram iteration.} Since $r\ll d$, both maps iterate on the $r\times r$ Gram
$R$ rather than the rectangular factor \cite{gram_ns} --- about $d/r$ cheaper. With
accumulator $Q$, each step applies
\begin{equation*}
  M_t=a_t I+b_t R+c_t R^2,\qquad Q\gets M_t Q,\qquad R\gets M_t R\,M_t .
\end{equation*}
Both maps run this same iteration, and the accumulator $Q$ is the product of the $M_t$;
they differ only in the seed $R_0$ and what is returned:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Matrix sign} $\polar(X)$ --- seed $R$ from the Gram of the normalized input, return $Q\tilde X$.
  \item \textbf{Inverse-sqrt} $C^{-1/2}$ --- seed $R$ from the normalized, damped curvature (\Cref{app:damping}), return a rescaled $Q$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Stability and precision.} The coefficients grow quickly across iterations,
so a spurious negative eigenvalue of $R$ can blow up; a restart at iteration $\tau$
re-anchors $R$ from the current iterate \cite{gram_ns}. The iteration runs in fp32 ---
bf16 diverges near the damping floor --- which is cheap on $r\times r$ matrices.
Production uses $K{=}8$ iterations.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Gram Newton--Schulz (shared by $\polar$ and the inverse-sqrt)}
\label{alg:gramns}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Gram/curvature $R_0$ (SPD, $r\times r$); coefficients $\{(a_t,b_t,c_t)\}_{t=1}^{K}$; restart $\tau$
\State $R\gets R_0$;\quad $Q\gets I$
\For{$t=1,\dots,K$}
  \If{$t=\tau{+}1$} \State $R_0\gets Q R_0 Q^\top$;\quad $R\gets R_0$;\quad $Q\gets I$ \Comment{re-anchor} \EndIf
  \State $M\gets a_t I+b_t R+c_t R^2$
  \State $Q\gets M Q$;\quad $R\gets M R M$
\EndFor
\State \Return $Q$ \Comment{$\to$ polar factor (post-multiply $\tilde X$), or inverse-sqrt (post-scale)}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Relative Damping}
\label{app:damping}
Each curvature inverse-sqrt in \Cref{alg:ours} is relative-damped: for a curvature factor $X$ with top eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}$,
\begin{equation*}
  X^{-1/2}\ \longmapsto\ \big(X/\lambda_{\max}+\delta I\big)^{-1/2},\qquad \delta>0 .
\end{equation*}
The normalization and the floor each follow from the step's structure.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Normalize by $\lambda_{\max}$.} The whitened polar depends only on the curvature's \emph{shape}, not its scale: $\polar\!\big(C_A^{-1/2}\hat M_A Q^{-1/2}\big)$ is invariant under $X\mapsto cX$, and the spectral-norm rescale $W_A/\norm{W_A}_2$ reabsorbs any global factor. The scale-free representative is the normalized spectrum $X/\lambda_{\max}\in(0,1]$.
  \item \emph{Floor by $\delta$.} On that spectrum $\delta$ is one conditioning knob: it caps the metric's condition number at ${\sim}1/\delta$, so a near-null curvature direction cannot amplify the step without bound.
  \item \emph{Warmup.} A still-zero factor ($\lambda_{\max}=0$) maps to $I$, so steps before the EMAs warm up are plain momentum.
\end{itemize}

\section{FLOP Overhead of the Optimizer}
\label{app:flops}
The wall measurement (\Cref{sec:exp-walltime}) is hardware-specific. The FLOP count is its
architecture-only counterpart, and tells the same near-free story. We count it for one
adapted layer, a frozen weight $W\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ with its LoRA
pair $(A,B)$ at rank $r\ll d_{\mathrm{in}},d_{\mathrm{out}}$, and combine layers at the end.
Here $K$ is the number of Newton--Schulz iterations and $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ the tokens
processed per step.

\paragraph{Forward and backward.} With the base weight frozen, the backward pass skips its
weight gradient, so it costs the same as the forward rather than twice it: four matmul-flops
per parameter per token in all, against six for full fine-tuning \cite{kaplan2020scaling}.
For one layer,
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{fwd}}=C_{\mathrm{bwd}}=2\,d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}.
  \label{eq:flop-fb}
\end{equation}
Summed over layers this is $4N\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ for total parameter count $N$. The LoRA
factors add only $2r(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})$ parameters per layer, smaller by a
factor $r/d$, and we drop them.

\paragraph{Optimizer.} The optimizer step (\Cref{alg:ours}) runs on the factors $A,B$ alone
and does not depend on $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$. Its matmuls fall into two groups. The first group
spans the long dimension $d$: forming the curvature factors $C_A,C_B$ \eqref{eq:ours-closure},
the whiten--polar--unwhiten directions \eqref{eq:factor-directions}, and the metric update
\eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling}. There is a fixed number of these per factor, each costing
$O(r^2 d)$, for $12\,r^2(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})$ in total. The second group is the
matrix sign $\polar(\cdot)$ and the curvature inverse-square-roots, computed entirely in the
small $r\times r$ space by the Gram Newton--Schulz of \Cref{app:gramns}, for $32\,K\,r^3$.
Together, with no eigendecomposition anywhere,
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{opt}}=12\,r^2(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})+32\,K\,r^3 .
  \label{eq:flop-opt}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Overhead fraction.} Dividing \eqref{eq:flop-opt} by \eqref{eq:flop-fb} gives the
per-layer overhead in closed form,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{C_{\mathrm{opt}}}{C_{\mathrm{fwd}}+C_{\mathrm{bwd}}}
  =\frac{12\,r^2(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})+32\,K\,r^3}{4\,d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}}
  =\frac{3r^2}{n_{\mathrm{tok}}}\Big(\tfrac1{d_{\mathrm{in}}}+\tfrac1{d_{\mathrm{out}}}\Big)
  +\underbrace{\frac{8Kr^3}{d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}}}_{\text{Gram Newton--Schulz}}.
  \label{eq:flop-ratio}
\end{equation}
The fraction falls as $1/n_{\mathrm{tok}}$, so it shrinks as the batch grows. The first term is
governed by the smaller of the two layer dimensions. The second is divided by their product,
so relative to the first it weighs most on the square attention matrices, where the two
dimensions are comparable, and less on the wide feed-forward matrices. The whole-model overhead is an average of these per-layer fractions weighted by layer size, so
it is dominated by the large feed-forward matrices, which have the smallest fraction. 

\paragraph{Why the Gram form.} All of the dependence on $K$ sits in the $32Kr^3$ term, the
Gram Newton--Schulz. It computes the matrix sign and the curvature inverse-square-roots by
iterating on the small $r\times r$ Gram matrix rather than on the tall $d\times r$ factor
(\Cref{app:gramns}), which is about $d/r$ cheaper per iteration. Iterating on the factor
instead would make the optimizer step roughly $1.5\times$ costlier, increasing its share of
the wall (\Cref{tab:breadth}).

\section{Factor Self-Balancing}
\label{app:gauge}
By \eqref{eq:ours-rho}, \methodname{}'s step size $\rho$ depends on the two factor norms $\norm{A}_2$ and $\norm{B}_2$. Rescaling $(A,B)\mapsto(cA,c^{-1}B)$ leaves the product $BA$, and hence the model, unchanged, but changes these norms, and with them $\rho$. Among the factorizations of a given product $BA$, $\rho$ is largest when the two factor norms are equal. An imbalanced factorization therefore takes a smaller step and follows a different trajectory. LoRA-Muon raises this concern for Spectron-style step sizes \cite{loramuon}.

In our runs this does not arise, because the factors balance dynamically. Starting from $B=0$, the ratio $\norm{B}_2/\norm{A}_2$ climbs and settles near $1$ (\Cref{fig:gauge}), so the factorization stays near the balanced point where $\rho$ is largest.
