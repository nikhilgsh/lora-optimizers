Deployment of large-scale pretrained models increasingly depends on adapting a
general model to many specialized tasks~\cite{foundation_models}. Finetuning a separate full copy for each task is expensive, because training must hold gradients and optimizer state for the whole model, and deploying each task means storing and serving multiple full-sized models. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA~\cite{lora}) avoids both issues by freezing the base weight $W_0 \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and adding a small low-rank update,
\begin{equation}
\DW = \frac{\alpha}{r}\,BA, \qquad
B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times r}, \quad
A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\mathrm{in}}},
\label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
to each layer, so the adapted weight is $W = W_0 + \DW$. With only the adapter trained, finetuning can run on nearly the same hardware layouts as inference, and the shared base lets one server serve many adapters at once~\cite{punica,bitdelta,schulman2025lora}.

The standard optimization recipe for LoRA applies Adam~\cite{adam} to the two factors $A$ and $B$, treating all the parameters as a single flat vector and ignoring any matrix structure. In language-model pretraining, matrix-aware optimizers such as Muon and Shampoo treat each weight as a matrix and outperform Adam~\cite{liu2025muon,shampoo,benchmark_practices,optimizer_benchmarking,qiu2026hyperparameter}, so it is natural to ask whether the same gains carry over to LoRA.

However, naively applying a matrix-aware optimizer to each factor does not improve on Adam. The model sees the \emph{product} $BA$, while the optimizer acts on the \emph{factors} $A$ and $B$ individually. Several concurrent works try to address this by making the update product-aware~\cite{imuon,loramuon,tilde_csd}, but we find that this alone does not consistently improve over Adam. 

This motivates \methodname{}, which is also derived using a product-aware viewpoint, but differs in how the step is chosen. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Curvature-aware direction.} We derive a curvature-preconditioned update direction by controlling the per-sample loss change across a batch, with a product-aware version for LoRA.
    
    \item \textbf{Magnitude control.} Unlike other product-aware updates, we control the factor update sizes in addition to the size of the merged weight update.

    \item \textbf{Component ablation.} We isolate the effect of the previous two contributions and show each provides a meaningful speedup.

    \item \textbf{Learning-rate transfer.} We demonstrate empirically that the optimal learning rate of \methodname{} is stable across rank, unlike for Adam, and the performance is less learning rate sensitive.

    \item \textbf{Empirical Performance.} Across a range of base models and datasets, \methodname{} reaches Adam's held-out loss in $1.2$–$1.7\times$ fewer steps, while adding at most 3\% per-step overhead.
\end{itemize}

\section{\texorpdfstring{\methodname{}}{PoLoRA}: Preconditioned Orthogonalized LoRA}



\label{sec:method}

A LoRA adapter changes the model through the product $BA$ of its two
factors~\eqref{eq:lora}, while the optimizer acts on $A$ and $B$. This section
derives an update that treats the adapter as a matrix update while still
operating on the factors. We first recall Muon, where the polar update
direction solves a spectral-norm LMO. We then apply the same LMO to a LoRA
product, which shows why independent per-factor orthogonalization is not the
right object. The final update incorporates a diagonal curvature metric into
this product-aware direction and uses a separate scaling rule to keep the two
factor steps balanced.

\subsection{Muon}

Muon chooses the polar factor of a matrix gradient as its update direction~\cite{muon,carlson2015preconditioned}. This direction solves a spectral-norm linear minimization oracle (LMO). For a weight $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ with gradient $G = \nabla_W \mathcal{L}(W)$, an LMO minimizes the local linearization of the loss subject to a ball constraint,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, \Delta W \right\rangle \\[2pt]
  \mbox{subject to} &
  \displaystyle
  \left\| \Delta W \right\|
  \le \tau,
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:lmo}
\end{equation}
where $\|\cdot\|$ is a chosen norm. A solution $\Delta W$ then gives the update direction for $W$.

Choosing the spectral norm,
\[
\|W\|_2 := \max_{\|x\|_2 =1} \|W x \|_2,
\]
controls the worst-case amplification of a linear operator. With this choice, the LMO direction is the polar factor of the gradient, up to sign and scale (\Cref{lem:muon-lmo}). Bounding the spectral norm of the update can therefore stabilize training and feature learning~\cite{yang2023spectral,bernstein2025modular}. To adapt this direction to LoRA, we must account for the product structure in~\eqref{eq:lora}.

\subsection{Product Muon}

Consider a LoRA step that updates the two factors by a given matrix $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ as follows
\[
A \leftarrow A + \Delta A,
\qquad
B \leftarrow B + \Delta B.
\]
Applying Muon naively would impose a spectral bound separately on $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$. But what matters is the resulting product $BA$. Indeed, 
the update to the merged weight, after linearizing the product, gives
\begin{equation} \label{eq:DeltaW-lin}
\Delta W = (B + \Delta B)(A + \Delta A) - BA \approx B \Delta A + \Delta B A,
\end{equation}
where we dropped the nonlinear term $\Delta B\Delta A$. 
To take into account the resulting product, a spectral norm bound should be applied to thus linearized product update. Substituting~\eqref{eq:DeltaW-lin} into~\eqref{eq:lmo}, the spectral constraint becomes
\[
\left\| B \Delta A + \Delta B A \right\|
\le \tau.
\]
This constraint couples the two unknowns $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$. We decouple them with the upper bound
\[
\left\| B \Delta A + \Delta B A \right\|
\le
\left\| B \Delta A \right\| + \left\|\Delta B A \right\|.
\]
With this bound we get the \emph{Product Muon} LMO
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \Delta A &\in \argmin_{\Delta A}~ \langle G_A, \Delta A \rangle
  \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad
  \norm{B \Delta A}\le\tau, \\
  \Delta B &\in \argmin_{\Delta B}~ \langle G_B, \Delta B \rangle
  \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad
  \norm{\Delta B A}\le\tau,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}
\end{equation}
where the factor gradients are
\begin{equation}\label{e-grad-wrt-factors}
    G_A := \nabla_A \mathcal{L}(W_0+BA) = B^\top G,
    \qquad
    G_B := \nabla_B \mathcal{L}(W_0+BA) = G A^\top.
\end{equation}
Thus \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} is the Product Muon LMO. \methodname{} builds on this product-aware object, but changes both the metric used for the direction and the rule used to set the factor-step magnitude.

\subsection{Per-Sample Loss Control}

We now derive the curvature metric used by the spectral update. Let $\{x_1, \ldots, x_n\}$ be a batch of input activations to the layer that $W$ encodes, and let $G_i$ be the gradient associated with $x_i$, that is, with a slight abuse of notation $G_i = \nabla \ell_{x_i}(W)$, where the abuse comes from using $\ell_{x_i}$ to denote the loss with respect to this activation rather than a full input.

To be conservative with respect to every activation, we enforce that no single activation results in a large change in the loss, that is we want
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loss-diff}
     \ell_{x_i}(W +\Delta W) - \ell_{x_i} (W)\leq \tau, \qquad  i=1,\ldots, n.
\end{equation} 
This would ensure stable training with no spikes or jumps for the current batch loss.  
But the loss is highly non-linear and non-convex, so instead we use the local linearization of the loss given by
\[  
\ell_{x_i}(W +\Delta W) -\ell_{x_i} (W)  \approx \langle G_i, \Delta W \rangle. 
\]
Using the above and~\eqref{eq:loss-diff} in the LMO~\eqref{eq:lmo} we arrive at
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, \Delta W\right\rangle \\
  \mbox{subject to} &
  \displaystyle  
\left\langle G_i, \Delta W \right\rangle 
  \le \tau, \qquad i=1,\ldots, n.
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:sample-lmo}
\end{equation}
Since we do not have efficient direct access to the per-input gradients $G_i$, we will now define a \emph{outergradient} set $\mathcal{G}$ such that $G_i \in \mathcal{G}$,
use the following inequality
\begin{equation} \label{eq:outgrad1}
    \max_{i=1,\ldots, n}  \left\langle G_i, \Delta W \right\rangle  
    \leq   \max_{\tilde G \in \mathcal{G}} \; \langle \tilde G, \Delta W \rangle,
\end{equation} 
and then impose  that the right hand side of \eqref{eq:outgrad1} 
is less than $\tau$. 
To build $\mathcal{G}$ we collect some standard properties of the gradient matrices in the following lemma.
\begin{restatable}[Properties of gradients]{lemma}{gradprop} 
  \label{lem:gradprop}
    Let $g_i=\mathrm{vec}(G_i) \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ for all $i=1, \ldots, n$,
    and $\Sigma := \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n g_i g_i^\top$. It follows that the gradients satisfy
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item {\bf Leverage bound:} $ \dotprod{\Sigma^\dagger g_i, g_i} \leq n$, \\[-0.5cm]
        \item {\bf Rank one:} $G_i = b_i x_i^\top$,
        \end{enumerate}
    for some $x_i \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and $b_i \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$.
\end{restatable}

The above properties suggest the following outergradient set
\begin{equation} 
\label{eq:outgrad} 
\mathcal{G}(\Sigma) := \left \{ \tilde G = b x^\top ~\bigg |~  
b\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}},
    \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}, \
\dotprod{\Sigma^\dagger \mathrm{vec}(\tilde G), \mathrm{vec}(\tilde G)} \leq n \right \}.
\end{equation}
By~\Cref{lem:gradprop} we have that $G_i \in \mathcal{G}$, thus we can use this $\mathcal{G}$ in our upper bound~\eqref{eq:outgrad1}.
The last remaining issue is that $\Sigma$ is too large of a matrix to store or use directly. To address this issue we replace $\Sigma$ with a structured approximation.

\paragraph{Kronecker approximation.}
Following the Kronecker-factored approximations used by Shampoo~\cite{shampoo, klshampoo}
and KFAC~\cite{kfac}, let $P\in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and $Q  \in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}} $ be symmetric positive definite matrices such that
     \begin{equation} \label{eq:pq-factor}
     \Sigma  \approx Q \otimes P.\end{equation}
     Using the factorization above, if we use our outergradient set~\eqref{eq:outgrad} to relax the constraint in~\eqref{eq:sample-lmo} through~\eqref{eq:outgrad1}, then we arrive at the following LMO
\begin{equation}
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \left\langle G, \Delta W\right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mbox{subject to} &
  \displaystyle  
\max_{\tilde G \in \mathcal{G}(Q \otimes P)} \langle \tilde G, \Delta W \rangle 
  \le \tau.
  \end{array}
  \label{eq:polorafull-hard}
\end{equation}
Fortunately the above can be simplified. By~\Cref{lem:pseudogradient-spectral} the constraint in~\eqref{eq:polorafull-hard} can be re-written using the identity 
\[ 
\max_{G \in \mathcal{G}(Q \otimes P)}\left\langle G, \Delta W \right\rangle  
= \sqrt{n} \| P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}\|_2.
\]
Then \eqref{eq:polorafull-hard} can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta W}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G, \Delta W\right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mathrm{subject~ to} &
\displaystyle  
\|P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}\|_2 \leq  \tau,
\end{array}
\label{eq:polorafull}
\end{equation}
where we have absorbed a $\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}$ into $\tau$, that is $\tau \leftarrow \frac{\tau}{\sqrt{n}}$.
The LMO in~\eqref{eq:polorafull} is an instance of the following preconditioned spectral-norm LMO.
\begin{restatable}[Preconditioned spectral-norm LMO]{lemma}{GeneralLmoUpdate}
\label{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}
Let $L$ and $R$ be symmetric positive definite. Then
\begin{equation}\label{e-gen-spectral-lmo-update}
X^\star = -\tau L^{-1}
\polar\left(L^{-1}MR^{-1}\right) R^{-1}
\end{equation}
is a solution of
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\|LXR\|_2\leq\tau}{\mathrm{minimize}}&
\langle M,X\rangle.
\end{array}
\]
Moreover, $X^\star$ is the unique minimizer of
$\|LXR\|_F$ over the solution set.
\end{restatable}
Applying the lemma above with $(L,R)=(P^{1/2},Q^{1/2})$, a solution to \eqref{eq:polorafull} is given by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:polorafull-update}
    \Delta W =  - \tau P^{-1/2} \polar(P^{-1/2} G Q^{-1/2}) Q^{-1/2}.
\end{equation} 


\paragraph{Direction.}
So far we have described an update for the full weight matrix $W \in \R^{d_{\mathrm{out}} \times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$. We now adapt this update to LoRA~\eqref{eq:lora}, computing its \emph{direction} and its \emph{magnitude} separately. 
To set the direction, we adapt the preconditioned spectral-norm
  LMO~\eqref{eq:polorafull}. The magnitude is set separately below.
  
 
Replacing $\Delta W$ in \eqref{eq:polorafull} with its linearized product update~\eqref{eq:DeltaW-lin} gives the LMO
\begin{equation}
    \begin{array}{ll}
    \underset{\Delta A,\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
    \left\langle G, B\Delta A+\Delta B A \right\rangle \\[2pt]
    \mbox{subject to} &
    \norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_2
    \le \tau.
    \end{array}
    \label{eq:joint-direction-lmo}
  \end{equation}
  Since we will set the magnitude later, the reader can ignore the exact value of $\tau$.
The constraint in \eqref{eq:joint-direction-lmo} couples $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ inside a single spectral norm. Subadditivity of the spectral norm bounds it by the two factor steps separately,
\[
\norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_2
\leq \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2
+ \norm{P^{1/2} \Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2,
\]
so bounding each factor step bounds the merged update. Using the above upper bound in~\eqref{eq:joint-direction-lmo}
% We therefore impose one spectral-norm constraint per factor,
% \[
% \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau_A,
% \qquad
% \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau_B ,
% \]
 decouples the product LMO into two factorwise LMOs.
That is, using factor gradients~\eqref{e-grad-wrt-factors},
the LMO~\eqref{eq:joint-direction-lmo} decouples into two factorwise LMOs
\begin{equation}
\setlength{\arraycolsep}{2pt}
\begin{array}{@{}c@{\qquad}c@{}}
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta A}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G_A,\Delta A \right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mbox{subject to} &
\norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau ,
\end{array}
&
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\Delta B}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
\left\langle G_B,\Delta B \right\rangle \\[2pt]
\mbox{subject to} &
\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2 \le \tau .
\end{array}
\end{array}
\label{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo}
\end{equation}
Define the $r\times r$ matrices
\begin{equation}
C_A = B^\top P B,
\qquad
C_B = A Q A^\top .
\label{eq:ours-closure}
\end{equation}
By definition of the spectral norm, and using the above definitions of $C_A$ and $C_B$,  the left hand side of the two constraints in \eqref{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo} can be re-written as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:collapse-constraint}
\norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2=\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A Q^{1/2}}_2,
\qquad
\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B A Q^{1/2}}_2=\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B C_B^{1/2}}_2 .
\end{equation}
Using~\eqref{eq:collapse-constraint} in~\eqref{eq:decoupled-direction-lmo}, each factorwise LMO is an instance of \Cref{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}, with $(L,R)=(C_A^{1/2},Q^{1/2})$ for the $\Delta A$ update and $(L,R)=(P^{1/2},C_B^{1/2})$ for the $\Delta B$ update. Thus the two minimizers are $-\tau D_A$ and $-\tau D_B$, where
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  D_A &=
  C_A^{-1/2}\,
  \polar\!\left(C_A^{-1/2}\,G_A\,Q^{-1/2}\right)
  \,Q^{-1/2},\\
  D_B &=
  P^{-1/2}\,
  \polar\!\left(P^{-1/2}\,G_B\,C_B^{-1/2}\right)
  \,C_B^{-1/2}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:factor-directions}
\end{equation}
The minimizers scale linearly with the budgets, $\Delta A=-\tau D_A$ and $\Delta B=-\tau D_B$, so $\tau$ controls the magnitude while $D_A$ and $D_B$ give the factor update directions.
% We therefore leave the budgets free and set the step magnitude directly through the learning rate $\eta$.

\paragraph{Magnitude.}
% It remains to choose the step magnitude. 
% Using the LMO budgets $\tau_A$ and $\tau_B$ directly would tie the magnitude to the LMO geometry rather than to the merged-weight update. 
% Under an equivalent rescaling of the LoRA factors, a fixed per-factor magnitude can make the merged update arbitrarily large (\Cref{lem:factor-radius-gauge}).
We now choose the update magnitudes for the two factors subject to two constraints. First, the linearized total update $\Delta W$ should have a spectral norm bounded by a user-specified learning rate $\eta$. Second, the factor updates $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ should have equal spectral-norm magnitude.

By the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity,
\begin{equation}
\norm{\Delta W}_2
\approx
\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2
\le
\norm{B}_2\norm{\Delta A}_2
+
\norm{A}_2\norm{\Delta B}_2 .
\label{eq:ours-prodcap}
\end{equation}

Treating $A$ and $B$ symmetrically, we use a single step size $\rho$ for both factors, setting $\norm{\Delta A}_2=\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho$. Choosing
\begin{equation}
\rho = \frac{\eta}{\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2}
\label{eq:ours-rho}
\end{equation}
ensures $\norm{\Delta W}_2\leq \eta$, and gives the updates
\[
\Delta A = -\rho\,\frac{D_A}{\norm{D_A}_2},
\qquad
\Delta B = -\rho\,\frac{D_B}{\norm{D_B}_2}.
\]
\Cref{app:gauge} examines how the step size $\rho$ depends on the relative scale of $A$ and $B$.
\paragraph{Implementation.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ours} summarizes the resulting optimizer step. In the implementation, the direction formula \eqref{eq:factor-directions} is applied to Nesterov momentum in place of the raw factor gradients. We compute the matrix sign and inverse square roots by Gram Newton--Schulz iterations (\Cref{app:gramns}), estimate the spectral norms by power iteration (\Cref{alg:smax}), and use damping in the curvature inverse square roots (\Cref{app:damping}). For further tractability, we restrict matrices 
$P$ and $Q$ to be diagonal, as in Adafactor \cite{adafactor}. To estimate the diagonal factors $P$ and $Q$ we apply KL-Shampoo's \cite{klshampoo}
coupled second moment equations (\Cref{app:matnormal}).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\methodname{} step for one LoRA pair $(A,B)$}
\label{alg:ours}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Statex \textbf{Hyperparameters:} learning rate $\eta$, momentum decay $\beta_1$, curvature decay $\beta_2$
\Statex
\Phase{Nesterov Momentum}
\State $M_A\gets\beta_1 M_A+(1-\beta_1)G_A, \qquad M_B\gets\beta_1 M_B+(1-\beta_1)G_B$ \Comment{momentum buffer}
\State $\widehat M_A\gets\beta_1M_A+(1-\beta_1)G_A, \qquad \widehat M_B\gets\beta_1M_B+(1-\beta_1)G_B$ \Comment{Nesterov look-ahead}
\Statex
\Phase{Direction}
\State $C_A\gets B^\top\,P\,B, \qquad C_B\gets A\,Q\,A^\top$
\State $D_A\gets C_A^{-1/2}\,\polar \big(C_A^{-1/2}\,\widehat M_A\,Q^{-1/2}\big)\,Q^{-1/2}$ \Comment{whiten--polar--unwhiten~\eqref{eq:factor-directions}}
\State $D_B\gets P^{-1/2}\,\polar \big(P^{-1/2}\,\widehat M_B\,C_B^{-1/2}\big)\,C_B^{-1/2}$
\Statex
\Phase{Magnitude}
\State $\rho\gets\eta/(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$ \Comment{spectral-norm control~\eqref{eq:ours-rho}}
\State $A\gets A-\rho D_A/\norm{D_A}_2, \qquad B\gets B-\rho D_B/\norm{D_B}_2$
\Statex
\Phase{Metric Update}
\State $Q\gets\beta_2 Q+(1-\beta_2)\diag\big(G_A^\top\,C_A^{-1}\,G_A\big)/r$ \Comment{coupled estimator~\eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling}}
\State $P\gets\beta_2 P+(1-\beta_2)\diag\big(G_B\,C_B^{-1}\,G_B^\top\big)/r$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

Here we perform an extensive ablation, comparing \methodname{} to several competitive baselines in~\Cref{sec:exp-main}, investigating which design choices of \methodname{} are responsible for its efficacy in~\Cref{sec:exp-ablation}, and testing robustness across rank and an out-of-distribution finetuning task in~\Cref{sec:exp-taskdep}.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\paragraph{Comparison.}
For every model, dataset, and rank, we only vary the optimizer and learning rate. All methods use LoRA on every linear layer except the output layer, with the standard initialization $B=0$ and random $A$ \cite{lora}. We set the LoRA scaling $\alpha=r$, following best practice \cite{loralearnsless, raschka}, so the $\alpha/r$ factor in \eqref{eq:lora} is one. For each optimizer, we sweep the learning rate under a constant schedule with no weight decay and select the run with the lowest held-out evaluation loss.

The main comparisons finetune OLMo-2-1B~\cite{olmo2}, Llama-3.2-1B~\cite{llama3}, Qwen2.5-1.5B~\cite{qwen25}, and Llama-3-8B on code (OpenCoder \cite{opencoder}) and math (OpenMathInstruct-2 \cite{openmathinstruct2}) datasets, using rank $r=256$ unless stated otherwise. Additional experiments in \Cref{sec:exp-taskdep} vary the rank as well as change the finetuning dataset to a low-resource language. \Cref{app:exp} gives the full experimental configurations.

\paragraph{Baselines.} We compare \methodname{} (\Cref{sec:method}) to the following optimizers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Adam} \cite{adam} --- our baseline, applied independently to the two factors $A$ and $B$.
  \item \textbf{Muon} \cite{muon} --- the Muon update~\eqref{eq:lmo} applied independently to each factor.
  \item \textbf{iMuon} \cite{imuon} --- the spectral LMO~\eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient} applied to momentum in the product space.
  \item \textbf{LoRA-RITE} \cite{lorarite} --- an adaptive, transformation-invariant LoRA optimizer that preconditions the factor gradients in a rotation-invariant basis. We run the authors' official PyTorch implementation at its default configuration; following their recommendation, gradient clipping is performed inside the optimizer in the invariant coordinates and the global gradient-norm clip is disabled, since a raw-gradient-norm clip is not transformation-invariant.
\end{itemize}

\section{Prior Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Hyperparameter rescaling for LoRA.}
LoRA~\cite{lora} enables parameter-efficient finetuning by freezing the pretrained
weights and training only a low-rank update~\eqref{eq:lora} through factors $A$ and
$B$, typically optimized with Adam~\cite{adam}. Several recent methods refine this
approach by rescaling hyperparameters such as the learning rates, adapter scale, or
initialization variance to account for the low-rank structure. Motivated by large-width
feature-learning arguments, LoRA+~\cite{loraplus} assigns $B$ a larger learning rate
than $A$, and follow-up work~\cite{hayou2024initialization, li2025beyond} extends this
to other initializations. Rank-stabilized LoRA~\cite{rslora} replaces the $\alpha/r$
adapter scale with $\alpha/\sqrt{r}$ to avoid the rank-dependent shrinkage that makes
high-rank adapters hard to train, and $\mu$A~\cite{mua} studies how these
hyperparameters should scale with rank to keep the optimal learning rate stable. In
contrast, \methodname{} ensures each factor receives an update of equal size in the operator norm, while simultaneously ensuring the total update to the weight is also bounded in operator norm.

\paragraph{Matrix-aware updates.}
Recently, optimizers that operate at the matrix level, rather than treating parameters as
a flattened vector, have attracted attention for their gains over Adam~\cite{liu2025muon,
qiu2026hyperparameter, benchmark_practices}. Muon~\cite{muon} solves the spectral-norm LMO
for a dense weight, producing an update equal to the polar factor of the momentum
gradient~\cite{polarexpress} (\Cref{lem:muon-lmo}). Shampoo~\cite{shampoo},
SOAP~\cite{soap}, and KL-Shampoo~\cite{klshampoo} estimate the gradient curvature as
Kronecker-factored second moments. Mousse~\cite{mousse} uses such a second moment estimate to
whiten the gradient before a polar step (\Cref{app:related-spectral}). \methodname{} also applies a polar step to a whitened gradient. Its metric is derived from controlling per-sample loss change in the batch. We adapt this update to LoRA training and add magnitude control, which removes the need for learning-rate grafting \cite{shi2023distributed, mousse}.

\paragraph{Product-invariant LoRA optimizers.}
The learned update $BA$ is a product of two matrices, so it is determined only up to the
change of basis $(A,B)\mapsto(NA,BN^{-1})$ for invertible $N$. Since Adam is not invariant
to this transformation, a line of work has proposed LoRA optimizers that are. LoRA-RITE
removes the basis dependence with an adaptive preconditioner~\cite{lorarite}, while Riemannion
optimizes $BA$ directly on the fixed-rank matrix manifold~\cite{riemannion}. iMuon and
LoRA-Muon derive invariant updates from spectral-norm
LMOs~\cite{imuon,loramuon}. As discussed in \Cref{app:memoryless}, the momentum-free LoRA-RITE, LoRA-Muon,
and iMuon LMO updates all coincide with \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}. \methodname{} does not satisfy this invariance, but it outperforms iMuon in our experiments (\Cref{sec:exp-main}), suggesting that invariance is not the limiting factor in this setting.

\paragraph{Magnitude control for low-rank products.}
Product Muon~\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} bounds $\norm{B\Delta A}_2$ and $\norm{\Delta B A}_2$ but not the factor steps $\norm{\Delta A}_2$ and $\norm{\Delta B}_2$. As a result, when $A$ or $B$ have small singular values, the updates $\Delta A$ and $\Delta B$ can be very large, which can destabilize training. Spectron~\cite{spectron} and QuacK~\cite{quack}, which are not based on the LMO step~\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo},
explicitly control the factor steps by scaling them with the factor norms. Spectron rescales both steps to spectral norm $\eta/(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2+1)$, and QuacK scales each factor's learning rate by the inverse norm of the other. Similar to Spectron, \methodname{} rescales both factor steps to a common spectral norm $\rho=\eta/(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$ (\Cref{rem:quadratic}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\methodname{} carries the fast part of spectral, curvature-aware optimization to
LoRA at low cost.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item A polar step in a coupled two-sided \emph{diagonal} curvature metric, with each factor step set to spectral norm $\rho$.
  \item $1.2$--$1.7\times$ fewer steps than Adam across nine settings, with training from $B{=}0$ and one learning rate across rank.
  \item The expensive Kronecker curvature and inner refinement it is usually bundled with add nothing.
  \item $\le 5\%$ per-step wall and ${\approx}\tfrac12$ Adam's state, so the step speedup survives in wall-clock.
\end{itemize}

The per-sample loss-control view suggests several possible follow-up directions.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Using other outergradient approximations could lead to other efficient methods
    \item Using other approximations other than linear for the total change, such as a quadratic approximation
    \item ,....
\end{itemize}

\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{Direction}

\begin{lemma}[Spectral norm LMO]\label{lem:muon-lmo}
For $G\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$ and $\eta>0$,
\[
    \min_{\|X\|_2\leq \eta}\langle G,X\rangle
    =
    -\eta\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}},
\]
and 
\[
X^\star = -\eta\polar(G)
\]
is unique least Frobenius norm minimizer of
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\underset{\|X\|_2\leq \eta}{\mathrm{minimize}}&
\langle G,X\rangle.
\end{array}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:muon-lmo}]
By duality of the spectral and nuclear norms, every feasible $X$ satisfies
\[
    \langle G,X\rangle
    \geq
    -\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}}\|X\|_2
    \geq
    -\eta\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}}.
\]
Let $G=U\Sigma V^\top$ be a reduced singular value decomposition.  The
choice
\[
    X=-\eta UV^\top=-\eta\polar(G)
\]
is feasible and satisfies
\[
    \langle G,X\rangle
    =
    -\eta\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma)
    =
    -\eta\|G\|_{\mathrm{nuc}}.
\]
Thus it attains the lower bound, and therefore, is optimal. 
The minimum-norm statement follows from Lemma C.2~\cite{parshakova2026muon}.
\end{proof}



\gradprop*
\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:gradprop}]
Let $g_i=\mathrm{vec}(G_i)$ and $M=[g_1,\ldots,g_n]$. 
Hence $g_i = Me_i$ for every $i =1,\ldots, n$. Consequently
\[
\dotprod{\Sigma^\dagger g_i, g_i} = ng_i^\top (MM^\top)^{\dagger} g_i = n e_i^\top M^\top  (MM^\top)^{\dagger} M e_i  \leq n,
\]
since $M^\top  (MM^\top)^{\dagger} M$ is an orthogonal projector
onto the range on $M^\top$. 
This proves the {\bf Leverage bound}. 

The {\bf Rank one} property follows since $W$ encodes a linear layer $x_i \mapsto W x_i$, and by the chain rule   
\[
\nabla \ell_{x_i}(Wx_i) =  \left. \frac{d }{dz} \ell_{x_i}(z)  
\right|_{z =Wx_i} x_i^\top
=b_i x_i^\top,
\]
where $b_i = \left.\frac{d }{dz} \ell_{x_i}(z)  \right|_{z =Wx_i} $ is the so called backward gradient.
\end{proof} 



\begin{lemma}[Outergradient set under a Kronecker factorization]
\label{lem:pseudogradient-spectral}
Let
$P\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and
$Q\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be symmetric positive definite, 
and let
$\Sigma=Q\otimes P$. 
Then for every $\Delta W\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$,
\[
  \max_{\tilde G \in \mathcal{G}(\Sigma)} \ip{ \tilde G , \Delta W}
  =\sqrt{n}\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta W Q^{1/2}}_2,
\]
where set $\mathcal{G}(\Sigma)$ is defined in \eqref{eq:outgrad}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:pseudogradient-spectral}]
Since $P$ and $Q$ are positive definite,
\[
    \Sigma^\dagger
    =
    \Sigma^{-1}
    =
    Q^{-1}\otimes P^{-1}.
\]
Consider $b x^\top \in \mathcal{G}(\Sigma)$.
Using column-major vectorization,
\[
    \operatorname{vec}(bx^\top)=x\otimes b.
\]
It follows that
\[
\left\langle
    \Sigma^\dagger\operatorname{vec}(bx^\top),
    \operatorname{vec}(bx^\top)
\right\rangle
=
(x\otimes b)^\top
(Q^{-1}\otimes P^{-1})
(x\otimes b)=
(x^\top Q^{-1}x)
(b^\top P^{-1}b).
\]
Let $b=P^{1/2}u$ and $x=Q^{1/2}v$. 
The constraint defining $\mathcal{G}(\Sigma)$ becomes
\[
    \|u\|_2^2\|v\|_2^2\leq n,
\]
and 
\[
\ip{bx^\top, \Delta W}=
 b^\top \Delta W x 
  = u^\top P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}v .
\]
Finally,
\[
\max_{\tilde G  \in\mathcal{G}(\Sigma)}
\langle \tilde G ,U\rangle
=
\max_{\|u\|_2\|v\|_2\leq\sqrt{n}}
u^\top P^{1/2} \Delta W Q^{1/2}v 
=
\sqrt{n}
\left\|P^{1/2} \Delta WQ^{1/2}\right\|_2.
\]
\end{proof}

\GeneralLmoUpdate*
\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:whitened-spectral-lmo}]
Set $Y=LXR$, so $X=L^{-1}YR^{-1}$. The constraint becomes
$\norm{Y}_2\le\eta$, and the objective becomes
\[
\left\langle M,X\right\rangle
=
\left\langle L^{-1}MR^{-1},Y\right\rangle .
\]
By \Cref{lem:muon-lmo}, unique least Frobenius norm minimizer is
\[
Y=-\eta \polar \left(L^{-1}MR^{-1}\right).
\]
Substituting back gives the stated solution.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Magnitude}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed factor step sizes under LoRA rescaling]
\label{lem:factor-radius-gauge}
Fix $P$, $Q$, and the product gradient $G$, and let
$\widehat D_A=D_A/\norm{D_A}_2$ and $\widehat D_B=D_B/\norm{D_B}_2$ be the
normalized directions from \eqref{eq:factor-directions}. For $c>0$, rescale the
factors by
\[
  B_c=cB,\qquad A_c=c^{-1}A,
\]
which leaves the product fixed, $B_cA_c=BA$. These normalized directions are
invariant under the rescaling, so a common factor step size $\rho>0$ gives the
first-order merged update
\[
  U_c=-\rho\bigl(cB\,\widehat D_A+c^{-1}\widehat D_B A\bigr).
\]
If $B\widehat D_A\ne0$ or $\widehat D_B A\ne0$, then $\sup_{c>0}\norm{U_c}_2=\infty$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{The normalized directions are rescaling-invariant.} Under the rescaling, the
$A$-block factor gradient and contracted curvature scale as
\[
  G_{A,c}=B_c^\top G=c\,G_A,\qquad
  C_{A,c}=B_c^\top P B_c=c^2C_A,
\]
so the polar argument in \eqref{eq:factor-directions} is unchanged,
\[
  C_{A,c}^{-1/2}G_{A,c}\,Q^{-1/2}=C_A^{-1/2}G_A\,Q^{-1/2}.
\]
Hence $D_{A,c}=c^{-1}D_A$, and normalizing removes the scalar:
$\widehat D_{A,c}=\widehat D_A$. Symmetrically,
\[
  G_{B,c}=G A_c^\top=c^{-1}G_B,\qquad
  C_{B,c}=A_cQA_c^\top=c^{-2}C_B,
\]
give $D_{B,c}=cD_B$ and $\widehat D_{B,c}=\widehat D_B$. With both directions
fixed, the merged update is the stated $U_c$.

\emph{The merged update is unbounded.} The two terms of $U_c$ scale oppositely
in $c$:
\[
  \frac{\norm{U_c}_2}{c}\to\rho\norm{B\widehat D_A}_2\ \ (c\to\infty),
  \qquad
  c\,\norm{U_c}_2\to\rho\norm{\widehat D_B A}_2\ \ (c\to0).
\]
If $B\widehat D_A\ne0$, the first limit forces $\norm{U_c}_2\to\infty$ as
$c\to\infty$; otherwise, if $\widehat D_B A\ne0$, the second forces
$\norm{U_c}_2\to\infty$ as $c\to0$. In either case $\sup_{c>0}\norm{U_c}_2=\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Negligibility of the quadratic term]
\label{rem:quadratic}
Our magnitude rule \eqref{eq:ours-rho} bounds the first-order merged update
$B\Delta A+\Delta B A$ and drops the quadratic term $\Delta B\Delta A$. Dropping
it shifts the step by only $O(\eta)$. The merged update is
\[
  \Delta W = B\Delta A+\Delta B A+\Delta B\Delta A .
\]
With the per-factor cap $\norm{\Delta A}_2,\norm{\Delta B}_2\le\rho$ and
$s=\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2$, the triangle inequality and submultiplicativity give
$\norm{\Delta W}_2 \le \rho s + \rho^2$. Bounding this full right-hand side by
$\eta$ gives the step
\[
  \rho^\star = \bigl(-s+\sqrt{s^2+4\eta}\bigr)/2
  = \frac{\eta}{s}-\frac{\eta^2}{s^3}+O(\eta^3/s^5),
\]
while dropping $\rho^2$ gives our $\rho=\eta/s$. The quadratic term thus shifts
the step by a relative $(\rho-\rho^\star)/\rho=\eta/s^2$. At standard
initialization $A$ has independent entries of variance $\Theta(1/d_{\mathrm{in}})$
and $B=0$, so $s=\norm{A}_2=\Theta(1)$ with high probability. The shift
$\eta/s^2$ is then $O(\eta)$, decreasing as $s$ grows.
\end{remark}



\section{Momentum-Free LoRA-RITE, iMuon, and LoRA-Muon}
\label{app:memoryless}
LoRA-RITE \cite{lorarite}, iMuon \cite{imuon}, and LoRA-Muon \cite{loramuon}
reduce to Product Muon~\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} once their moment accumulation is removed. We show this below.


\paragraph{Setup.} As in \Cref{sec:method}, 
let $G=\nabla_W \mathcal{L}(W) \big |_{W=BA}$ be the product
gradient, $G_A=B^\top G$ and $G_B=GA^\top$ be the factor gradients, 
and $S_B=AA^\top$ and
$S_A=B^\top B$ be the $r\times r$ Gram matrices.
The two factorwise LMOs in Product Muon~\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo} are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \Delta A &\in    \argmin_{\Delta A}~ \langle G_A, \Delta A \rangle \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad
\norm{B \Delta A}_2\le\tau \\
\Delta B &\in   \argmin_{\Delta B}~ \langle G_B,  \Delta B \rangle \quad \textnormal{subject to}\quad  \norm{\Delta B A}_2\le\tau.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo-app} 
\end{equation}
They have the closed-form solution
\begin{align} 
    \Delta A &=-\tau\,S_A^{-1/2}\polar(S_A^{-1/2}G_A) \nonumber \\
    \Delta B &=-\tau\,\polar(G_BS_B^{-1/2})S_B^{-1/2}.  \label{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo-closed}
\end{align}
We will use the fact that for any matrix $M$ with full column rank and reduced SVD $M=U\Sigma V^\top$,
the polar factor equals
\begin{equation}
  % Not true unless we assume full rank
  M(M^\top M)^{-1/2}=
  UV^\top=\polar(M).
  \label{eq:polar-id}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{LoRA-RITE.}
Algorithm~1 of LoRA-RITE~\cite{lorarite} adds three pieces of memory to a base step. These are an EMA first moment with parameter $\beta_1$, an accumulated second moment transported across the changing factor basis, and an ``escaped-mass'' floor added to that second moment.

Dropping all three, we get the authors' momentum-free update (their Eq.~(14)),
which in our notation for $ \widehat G_B=G_B S_B^{-1/2}$ becomes
\[
  \Delta B=-\eta \widehat G_B (\widehat G_B^\top\widehat G_B)^{-1/2} S_B^{-1/2}.
\]
With no accumulation by \eqref{eq:polar-id} the middle factor is a polar factor, so
\begin{equation*}
  \Delta B=-\eta \polar(\widehat G_B) S_B^{-1/2}
  =-\eta \polar \big(G_B S_B^{-1/2}\big) S_B^{-1/2},
\end{equation*}
which is equal to the $B$-update given in~\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo-closed}. Analogous arguments hold for the $A$-update.


\paragraph{iMuon, momentum-free.}
iMuon relies on a choice of \emph{ambient matrix} $H\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$, which is the dense-space signal passed to the intrinsic spectral LMO. The intrinsic spectral LMO of Corollary~4.1 maps this ambient matrix to the factor updates as follows
\begin{equation}
  \Delta B(H) =\polar \big(HA^\top S_B^{-1/2}\big)S_B^{-1/2},\qquad
  \Delta A(H) =S_A^{-1/2}\polar \big(S_A^{-1/2}B^\top H\big).
  \label{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient}
\end{equation}
For the base Algorithm~1 in~\cite{imuon}, the ambient matrix is the raw gradient $G$, so~\eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient}
is again equivalent to \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo-closed}. Our experiments benchmark the official iMuon implementation from Appendix~K of~\cite{imuon}, which differs from this momentum-free decoupled update.

\paragraph{iMuon, with momentum.} The official implementation of iMuon with momentum (their Appendix~K) does not
use the gradient as input to \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient}. 
It instead first combines the two factor
look-aheads $\tilde G_A=G_A+\beta m_A$ and $\tilde G_B=G_B+\beta m_B$ (buffers $m_A,m_B$,
with parameter $\beta$) into a single ambient matrix, then uses that matrix as input
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M=\tilde G_B A+B\tilde G_A .
\end{equation*}
Turning momentum off ($\beta=0$) removes the buffers but not the recombination, i.e.,
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M=G_B A+B G_A=G\,A^\top A+BB^\top G\ \neq\ G .
\end{equation*}
Plugging $\widehat M$ into \eqref{eq:imuon-lmo-ambient} and using $G_A A^\top=B^\top G_B$,
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat M A^\top=G_B S_B+(BB^\top)G_B
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \Delta B(\widehat M)=\polar\!\big(G_B S_B^{1/2}+(BB^\top)G_B S_B^{-1/2}\big)S_B^{-1/2},
\end{equation*}
which is different from Product Muon. The argument for $\Delta A$ is symmetric.
Thus switching momentum off does not recover \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}. While
\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo-app} is the
momentum-free limit of iMuon's theory (Corollary~4.1), 
it is not the momentum-free limit of its implemented optimizer.


\paragraph{LoRA-Muon.}
The concurrent LoRA-Muon paper~\cite{loramuon} also arrives at the two decoupled
factorwise LMOs in~\eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}.
Specifically, its momentum-free LMOs in Eq. (7) and (8) of~\cite{loramuon} are precisely \eqref{eq:decoupled-lora-lmo}.
LoRA-Muon also shows that this
Gram-matrix form is equivalent to the simplified QR-coordinate form of
LoRA-RITE~\cite{loramuon}.



\section{Extended Prior Work: Spectral and Curvature-Aware Optimizers}
\label{app:related-spectral}
Here we will better contextualize how
 \methodname{} relates to other spectral and curvature-aware
optimizers (\Cref{sec:related}).

\paragraph{Mousse and the whitening power.}
Mousse fixes its trust region by a Shampoo curvature $H\approx(R\otimes L)^{1/2}$, with
$L$ and $R$ being the moving averages of $GG^\top$ and $G^\top G$. Whitening by
$H^{1/2}=(R\otimes L)^{1/4}$ gives the dense update
\[
  \Delta W=-L^{-1/4}\,\polar\!\big(L^{-1/4} G R^{-1/4}\big)\,R^{-1/4}.
\]
\methodname{} instead uses the full gradient covariance $\Sigma\approx Q\otimes P$ as its
metric, whitening by $P^{-1/2}$ and $Q^{-1/2}$ (\Cref{alg:ours}), an inverse-square-root
rather than Mousse's fourth-root power; the first-order LoRA product map then contracts
these dense factors to $C_A=B^\top P B$ and $C_B=AQA^\top$.

\paragraph{SOAP-Muon.}
SOAP-Muon whitens the gradient with a SOAP preconditioner and then orthogonalizes the
result \cite{soapmuon}.

\paragraph{Kang et al.}
For spectral gradient flow in a simplified matrix-factorization model,
Kang et al.~\cite{kang_specgf} prove that the singular values of the merged adapter
product grow at a nearly uniform rate even though the two factors are orthogonalized
independently.

\paragraph{Spectron.}
Spectron \cite{spectron} bounds the induced product update by rescaling orthogonalized
factor steps with the current factor norms, in native low-rank pretraining. It is close in
spirit to \methodname{}'s magnitude rule but is not curvature-aware and is not derived
from a single LoRA LMO.

\section{Diagonal Kronecker Second Moment Estimator for the Curvature Diagonals}
\label{app:matnormal}
In this section we derive the estimator \eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling} and its coupling
\eqref{eq:ours-closure} from the second moment model \eqref{eq:pq-factor} alone.

\paragraph{Model and moment identities.} 
Let 
$G\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ 
be stochastic merged-weight gradient.
We use the diagonal Kronecker approximation of \eqref{eq:pq-factor} as follows
\begin{equation}\label{e-diag-kron-second-mom-model}
\Sigma
=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{vec}(G)\operatorname{vec}(G)^\top]
\approx Q\otimes P,
\qquad
P=\diag(p)\succ0,
\quad
Q=\diag(q)\succ0,
\end{equation}
where $p\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ and $q\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$. Thus
$\mathbb{E}[G_{ij}^2]\approx p_iq_j$.
By \eqref{e-grad-wrt-factors} and \eqref{eq:ours-closure},
\[
\operatorname{vec}(G_A)=(I_{d_{\mathrm{in}}}\otimes B^\top)\operatorname{vec}(G),
\qquad \operatorname{vec}(G_B)=(A\otimes I_{d_{\mathrm{out}}})\operatorname{vec}(G),
\]
therefore, second moment model gives
\[
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{vec}(G_A)\operatorname{vec}(G_A)^\top]\approx Q\otimes C_A,
\qquad
\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{vec}(G_B)\operatorname{vec}(G_B)^\top]\approx C_B\otimes P.
\]
Note that the rescaling
$(P,Q)\mapsto(cP,c^{-1}Q)$ leaves  $Q\otimes P$ 
unchanged.

\paragraph{Divergence-based projection.}
We fix $C_A\succ0$ and fit the second moment of $G_A$ by
$Q\otimes C_A$, where $Q=\operatorname{Diag}(q)$. As in \cite{klshampoo}, 
we get $q$ by projecting the second moment of $G_A$  onto a set of Kronecker-structured 
symmetric positive definite matrices
w.r.t. 
KL divergence
\[
 \begin{array}{ll}
  \underset{q}{\mathrm{minimize}} &
  \frac12\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}
    \left(
        r\log q_j
        +\frac{1}{q_j}
          \mathbb{E}[(G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}]
    \right)
  \end{array}
\]
The objective above is strictly convex in $\log q$ and is coordinatewise separable. Applying the
same calculation to $G_B$ gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    q_j
    &=\frac1r\,
      \mathbb{E}[(G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}],
    &&j=1,\ldots,d_{\mathrm{in}},\\
    p_i
    &=\frac1r\,
      \mathbb{E}[(G_B)_{i,:}C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top],
    &&i=1,\ldots,d_{\mathrm{out}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:diag-kron-population}
\end{equation}
For fixed $C_A$ or $C_B$, these are the diagonal specialization of the
conditional two-sided KL-Shampoo equations \cite{klshampoo}. They are coupled
because $C_A$ contains $P$, while $C_B$ contains $Q$.

If diagonal Kronecker model \eqref{e-diag-kron-second-mom-model} 
is exact, then
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}[G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A]=rQ,
    \qquad
    \mathbb{E}[G_BC_B^{-1}G_B^\top]=rP.
    \label{eq:diag-kron-unbiased}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the first identity is
$\operatorname{tr}(C_A^{-1}C_A)Q=rQ$; the second is identical. This proves the
factor $1/r$ in \eqref{eq:diag-kron-population}.


\paragraph{Online update.}
We track the population equations \eqref{eq:diag-kron-population} by an
exponential moving average (EMA) with parameter $\beta_2$. 
Specifically, 
update \eqref{eq:diag-kron-population} becomes
\begin{equation}
  q_j=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r (G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}\Big],\qquad
  p_i=\mathrm{EMA}\Big[\tfrac1r (G_B)_{i,:} C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top\Big],
  \label{eq:ours-klcoupling}
\end{equation}
as outlined in \Cref{alg:ours}.

\subsection{Spectral-Norm Estimation}
\label{app:smax}
\Cref{alg:ours} requires repeated estimates of spectral norms.  
We
estimate each norm with a warm-started power iteration, summarized in
\Cref{alg:smax}.
The vector $v$ is cached for each matrix and reused
across optimizer steps, so after the first few steps the power iteration
is typically already close to the leading right singular vector.
Floor $l$ is an important safeguard.
Both the largest row norm and the largest column norm are lower bounds on
$\|M\|_2$, since
\[
    \|M_{i,:}\|_2 = \|M^\top e_i\|_2 \leq \|M\|_2,
    \qquad
    \|M_{:,j}\|_2 = \|M e_j\|_2 \leq \|M\|_2 .
\]
which
prevents severe under-estimation from a cold or stale power iteration.
This matters because the optimizer rescales by the estimated norm.  If
$\|M\|_2$ is underestimated by $\widehat{\sigma}(M)$, then $M/\widehat{\sigma}(M)$ is too
large, and the induced product step can exceed the intended radius
$\rho$, which may destabilize training.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Guarded spectral-norm estimate of $\norm{M}_2$}
\label{alg:smax}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require matrix $M$; warm-start vector $v$ (cached across steps); iterations $K$
% \Statex

\State $l \gets\max\big\{\max_i\norm{M_{i,:}}_2,\ \max_j\norm{M_{:,j}}_2\big \}$
% \Statex

\For{$k=1,\dots,K$} \Comment{power iteration on $M^\top M$}
  \State $w\gets Mv$
  \State $v\gets M^\top w /\norm{M^\top w}_2$
\EndFor
% \Statex

\State \Return $\max\big\{\norm{Mv}_2,\ l\big\}$ \Comment{$l$ floor}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Matrix Sign and Inverse Square Root via Gram Newton--Schulz}
\label{app:gramns}

\Cref{alg:ours} uses two spectral primitives:
the sign of a matrix $\polar(\cdot)$ and the inverse square root
of $r \times r$ curvature matrix.  We compute both with the
same Gram Newton--Schulz method.  The method uses only $r\times r$
matrix multiplications and does not require an eigendecomposition or an
SVD (\Cref{alg:gramns}).

\paragraph{Quintic Newton--Schulz.}
Each iteration applies an odd degree-five polynomial
\[
    p_t(s)
    =
    a_t s + b_t s^3 + c_t s^5 .
\]
The coefficients $(a_t,b_t,c_t)$ are the PolarExpress coefficients
\cite{polarexpress}.  They are chosen so that repeated composition of the
maps $p_t$ drives singular values toward $1$.  The coefficients are
computed once at startup and reused for every optimizer step.


\paragraph{Gram iteration.}
Rather than iterating on a rectangular matrix, we iterate on its
$r\times r$ Gram matrix \cite{gram_ns}.  Suppose the current Gram matrix is
\[
    R_t = Y_t Y_t^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r},
\]
where $Y_t$ is the current rectangular iterate on the small side.  If
we update
\[
    Y_{t+1}
    =
    M_t Y_t,
    \qquad
    M_t
    =
    a_t I + b_t R_t + c_t R_t^2,
\]
then
\[
    R_{t+1}
    =
    Y_{t+1}Y_{t+1}^\top
    =
    M_t R_t M_t .
\]
Thus the entire iteration can be run on the Gram matrix $R_t$.  If $s_t$ is a singular value of $Y_t$, then
corresponding eigenvalue of $R_t$ is $\lambda_t=s_t^2$, and the singular value evolves as
\[
    s_{t+1}
    =
    \left(a_t+b_t s_t^2+c_t s_t^4\right)s_t
    =
    p_t(s_t).
\]
Therefore the Gram update implements the same quintic Newton--Schulz map
on singular values, but costs only $r\times r$ matrix multiplications.

\paragraph{Shared accumulator.}
The method also maintains an accumulator $Q_t$.  Starting from
$Q_0=I$, each step sets
\[
    Q_{t+1}=M_t Q_t,
    \qquad
    R_{t+1}=M_t R_t M_t .
\]
Hence by induction we can show that
\[
    R_t = Q_t R_0 Q_t^\top .
\]
At the end of the iteration, $Q_K$ is the accumulated matrix.
The two call sites differ only in how $R_0$ is initialized and how
$Q_K$ is interpreted.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Matrix sign.}
    For a rectangular matrix $X$, first normalize
    \[
        \widetilde X = X/\alpha,
        \qquad
        \alpha \geq \|X\|_2,
    \]
    to ensure that the singular values lie in the range $[0, 1]$.  Using the small-side Gram
    \[
        R_0 = \widetilde X\widetilde X^\top ,
    \]
    the algorithm returns $Q_K$, and the matrix sign is approximated by
    \[
        \polar(X)
        =
        \polar(\widetilde X)
        \approx
        Q_K\widetilde X .
    \]
    If the small-side Gram is instead $\widetilde X^\top\widetilde X$,
    the same formula is used on the transpose, returning
    $\widetilde X Q_K$.

    \item \textbf{Inverse square root.}
    For a damped curvature matrix $C$ (\Cref{app:damping}), first normalize
    \[
        \widetilde C = C/\alpha,
        \qquad \alpha \geq \|C\|_2.
    \]
    We set
    $R_0=\widetilde C$.  The same algorithm returns
    $Q_K\approx \widetilde C^{-1/2}$, so
    \[
        C^{-1/2}
        =
        \alpha^{-1/2}\widetilde C^{-1/2}
        \approx
        \alpha^{-1/2} Q_K .
    \]
\end{itemize}



\paragraph{Stability and precision.}
We run the Gram Newton--Schulz iteration in fp32, which is cheap because
all matrices are only $r\times r$, and it avoids the bf16 instability
we observed near the damping floor.  The main numerical risk is loss of
positive semidefiniteness: later coefficients can be large, so a small
spurious negative eigenvalue of $R$ may be amplified.  We therefore
optionally re-anchor the Gram matrix once, at iteration $\tau$, by
recomputing it from the accumulated map.
In exact arithmetic this is the same Gram matrix as the recursive update
$R\gets MRM$; in finite precision it removes accumulated drift
\cite{gram_ns}.  We use $K=8$ iterations.


\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Gram Newton--Schulz iteration}
\label{alg:gramns}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require normalized Gram $R_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times r}$, coefficients $\{(a_t,b_t,c_t)\}_{t=1}^{K}$, restart time $\tau$
\State $R\gets R_0$;\quad $Q\gets I$
\For{$t=1,\dots,K$}
  \If{$t=\tau{+}1$} \State $R_0\gets Q R_0 Q^\top$;\quad $R\gets R_0$;\quad $Q\gets I$ \Comment{re-anchor} \EndIf
  \State $M\gets a_t I+b_t R+c_t R^2$
  \State $Q\gets M Q$;\quad $R\gets M R M$
\EndFor
\State \Return $Q$ 
% \Comment{$\to$ polar factor (post-multiply $\tilde X$), or inverse-sqrt (post-scale)}
\Comment{used for $\polar(\cdot)$, or post-scaled for $C^{-1/2}$}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Relative Damping}\label{app:damping}
We use relative damping for each inverse square root computation in \Cref{alg:ours}.
On the normalized spectrum, the damping parameter $\delta$ has a fixed
meaning.  If $X = U\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)U^\top$ is the eigendecomposition of $X$,
then inverse square root of $X$ with relative damping is defined as
\[
    H_\delta(X)
    =
    U\operatorname{diag}
    \left(
        \left(\frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_{\max}}+\delta\right)^{-1/2}
    \right)
    U^\top .
\]
The damped eigenvalues lie in
\[
    \frac{\lambda_i}{\lambda_{\max}}+\delta
    \in [\delta,1+\delta]
    ~\implies~
    \kappa\left(
        \frac{X}{\lambda_{\max}}+\delta I
    \right)
    \leq
    \frac{1+\delta}{\delta}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
    \|H_\delta(X)\|_2 \leq \delta^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus $\delta$  prevents directions with
nearly zero curvature from being amplified without bound.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Relation to ordinary damping.}
Since
\[
    \frac{X}{\lambda_{\max}}+\delta I
    =
    \frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}}
    \left(X+\delta\lambda_{\max}I\right),
\]
we have
\[
    H_\delta(X)
    =
    \lambda_{\max}^{1/2}
    \left(X+\delta\lambda_{\max}I\right)^{-1/2}.
\]
Thus relative damping is ordinary damping with floor
$\delta\lambda_{\max}$, up to a global scalar.  That scalar is harmless
because the subsequent polar and spectral-normalization steps are
scale-invariant.

\item \textbf{Warmup.}
At the beginning of training, an EMA curvature factor may still be zero.
When $\lambda_{\max}=0$, we set $H_\delta(X)=I$.  The corresponding
side is then unpreconditioned, so the update reduces to the plain
momentum direction until the curvature estimate becomes nonzero.
\end{itemize}




\section{FLOP Overhead of the Optimizer}
\label{app:flops}
The wall measurement (\Cref{sec:exp-walltime}) is hardware-specific. The FLOP count is its
architecture-only counterpart, and tells the same near-free story. We count it for one
adapted layer, a frozen weight $W\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ with its LoRA
pair $(A,B)$ at rank $r\ll d_{\mathrm{in}},d_{\mathrm{out}}$, and combine layers at the end.
Here $K$ is the number of Newton--Schulz iterations and $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ the tokens
processed per step.

\paragraph{Forward and backward.} With the base weight frozen, the backward pass skips its
weight gradient, so it costs the same as the forward rather than twice it: four matmul-flops
per parameter per token in all, against six for full fine-tuning \cite{kaplan2020scaling}.
For one layer,
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{fwd}}=C_{\mathrm{bwd}}=2\,d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}.
  \label{eq:flop-fb}
\end{equation}
Summed over layers this is $4N\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}$ for total parameter count $N$. The LoRA
factors add only $2r(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})$ parameters per layer, smaller by a
factor $r/d$, and we drop them.

\paragraph{Optimizer.} The optimizer step (\Cref{alg:ours}) runs on the factors $A,B$ alone
and does not depend on $n_{\mathrm{tok}}$. Its matmuls fall into two groups. The first group
spans the long dimension $d$: forming the curvature factors $C_A,C_B$ \eqref{eq:ours-closure},
the whiten--polar--unwhiten directions \eqref{eq:factor-directions}, and the metric update
\eqref{eq:ours-klcoupling}. There is a fixed number of these per factor, each costing
$O(r^2 d)$, for $12\,r^2(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})$ in total. The second group is the
matrix sign $\polar(\cdot)$ and the curvature inverse-square-roots, computed entirely in the
small $r\times r$ space by the Gram Newton--Schulz of \Cref{app:gramns}, for $32\,K\,r^3$.
Together, with no eigendecomposition anywhere,
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{opt}}=12\,r^2(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})+32\,K\,r^3 .
  \label{eq:flop-opt}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Overhead fraction.} Dividing \eqref{eq:flop-opt} by \eqref{eq:flop-fb} gives the
per-layer overhead in closed form,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{C_{\mathrm{opt}}}{C_{\mathrm{fwd}}+C_{\mathrm{bwd}}}
  =\frac{12\,r^2(d_{\mathrm{in}}{+}d_{\mathrm{out}})+32\,K\,r^3}{4\,d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}}
  =\frac{3r^2}{n_{\mathrm{tok}}}\Big(\tfrac1{d_{\mathrm{in}}}+\tfrac1{d_{\mathrm{out}}}\Big)
  +\underbrace{\frac{8Kr^3}{d_{\mathrm{in}}d_{\mathrm{out}}\,n_{\mathrm{tok}}}}_{\text{Gram Newton--Schulz}}.
  \label{eq:flop-ratio}
\end{equation}
The fraction falls as $1/n_{\mathrm{tok}}$, so it shrinks as the batch grows. The first term is
governed by the smaller of the two layer dimensions. The second is divided by their product,
so relative to the first it weighs most on the square attention matrices, where the two
dimensions are comparable, and less on the wide feed-forward matrices. The whole-model overhead is an average of these per-layer fractions weighted by layer size, so
it is dominated by the large feed-forward matrices, which have the smallest fraction. 

\paragraph{Why the Gram form.} All of the dependence on $K$ sits in the $32Kr^3$ term, the
Gram Newton--Schulz. It computes the matrix sign and the curvature inverse-square-roots by
iterating on the small $r\times r$ Gram matrix rather than on the tall $d\times r$ factor
(\Cref{app:gramns}), which is about $d/r$ cheaper per iteration. Iterating on the factor
instead would make the optimizer step roughly $1.5\times$ costlier, increasing its share of
the wall (\Cref{tab:breadth}).

\section{Factor Self-Balancing}
\label{app:gauge}
By \eqref{eq:ours-rho}, \methodname{}'s step size $\rho$ depends on the two factor norms $\norm{A}_2$ and $\norm{B}_2$. Rescaling $(A,B)\mapsto(cA,c^{-1}B)$ leaves the product $BA$, and hence the model, unchanged, but changes these norms, and with them $\rho$. Among scalar rescalings $(A,B)\mapsto(cA,c^{-1}B)$ of a fixed 
factorization $BA$, $\rho$ is largest when the two factor norms are equal. An imbalanced factorization therefore takes a smaller step and follows a different trajectory. LoRA-Muon raises this concern for Spectron-style step sizes \cite{loramuon}.

In our runs this problem does not arise, because the factors balance dynamically. Starting from $B=0$, the ratio $\norm{B}_2/\norm{A}_2$ climbs and settles near $1$ (\Cref{fig:gauge}), so the factorization stays near the balanced point where $\rho$ is largest.
