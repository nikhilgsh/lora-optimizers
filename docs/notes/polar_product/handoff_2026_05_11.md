## Current debug efforts

### The chord_slack > 1 "bound violation"

**Status: almost certainly a probe bug, but not 100% confirmed at late training under the fixed probe.**

History:
1. Original chord_slack probe used `torch.linalg.eigvals` on the non-symmetric product `(R Rᵀ)(Lᵀ L)` (where L = [B+dB, B], R = [A+dA, -A]). This routinely over-estimates spectral norm by 30–60% on ill-conditioned matrices.
2. We observed chord_slack > 1 starting around step 80+ in variant 1 (`spectral_chord_direction`) runs, even though variant 1 is mathematically constructed to saturate the chord bound (slack ≤ 1 by construction).
3. The user posited probe-vs-algorithm uncertainty. Cross-check added: `chord_slack_svd_direct` using the materialized chord matrix `B·dA + dB·A + dB·dA` and direct SVD.
4. Probe replacement: `_sigma_max_chol_eigvalsh(G_outer, G_inner)` does the symmetric reduction (Cholesky factor L of G_inner, eigvalsh of LᵀG_outer L, sqrt of max eigenvalue). This is mathematically equivalent to σ_max of the non-symmetric product but exact under fp32. Verified to match exact-SVD to 1e-7 in synthetic tests.
5. In v3 (commit ec95622) at step 20 with both fixed probe AND svd_direct: chord_slack=0.9947, svd_direct=0.9846. Agreement, both under 1.

What's left to verify: chord_slack stays < 1 at late training (step 200+). v3 will tell us once it emits more probes. Earlier "breach" trajectories in `chord_direction_4k_r16r64_blackwell` (probe values up to 9.4) lack the svd_direct cross-check field entirely — they're pre-fix and not informative.

Methodology rule was added to global CLAUDE.md: "suspect the probe before the theorem."

### Whitening importance — UNRESOLVED, single-seed only

**The empirical observation:** at r=16, no-whitening (0.5266) is 0.0016 better than whitened chord-tight (0.5282). At r=64 matched step 3600, no-whitening (0.5209) is 0.0014 better than whitened (0.5223). Both ~2–2.7σ at the proxy noise floor.

**The diagnostic story so far** (all from `chord_direction_4k_r16r64_blackwell` r=16, 50 probe steps):
- `cond(S_B)`: 10–180 (S_B is highly anisotropic, so whitening SHOULD do something)
- `cond(S_A)`: 1.4–2.1 (S_A nearly isotropic; A-side whitening would be near-identity even if it were applied)
- `higham_SB_rel_err_F`: 6e-4 (whitening is computed accurately, not introducing numerical error)
- `cos_polar_clip`: 0.99 (polar map is a faithful substitute for the clip prox in whitened space)
- `sat_frac_tight = 1.0` throughout (saturating-regime assumption is exact, not approximate)
- `adam_gauge_residual_rel`: 0.07 → 0.34 over 4k steps (Adam's preconditioning increasingly breaks the natural LoRA gauge identity)
- `|A|`: 2.39 → 3.54, `|B|`: 0.63 → 2.81 (so `|A|/|B|` goes from 3.8× to 1.26× over training; B grows faster than A but doesn't overtake)

**Failed explanation attempts (assistant's, with the user's pushback):**

1. **"B is small, so B·dA branch barely contributes to ΔW, so A-side whitening barely matters."** Wrong at endpoint: at step 4k, ‖B·dA‖≈0.056 vs ‖dB·A‖≈0.085, ratio 1.5× — NOT dominant. Was correct at step 80 (4×) but extrapolated incorrectly to all of training. The user caught this.

2. **"As B grows the two terms become comparable, so whitening should start helping in late training."** Not supported by data — whitening's gap doesn't shrink in late training (we only have endpoints, but the gap is in the wrong direction throughout). Possibly wrong direction of effect entirely.

**Standing hypothesis (NOT verified):** Adam preconditioning leaks u_A into B's near-nullspace directions (`adam_gauge_residual_rel` measures this — grows to 0.34). Whitening (`S_B^{-1/2}`) amplifies those near-nullspace components by up to √cond(S_B) ≈ 13×. So whitening boosts what Adam leaks. Predicts: (a) whitening would help raw-gradient (Muon-style) optimizers more than Adam-preconditioned ones; (b) the whitening gap should widen with training; (c) low cond(S_B) → whitening neutral.

**The diagnostic that would actually answer this is missing.** What's needed: a paired-step probe that runs the optimizer twice at each step (with/without whitening, same gradient + Adam state) and logs `cos(dW_whitened, -G_natural)` vs `cos(dW_unwhitened, -G_natural)` where G_natural is the chain-rule gradient pulled through the LoRA tangent. None of the existing probes give a direct read on whether whitened or unwhitened dA aligns better with descent.

**Caveat that limits any claim:** all eval Δs for the whitening comparison are single-seed at one lr (3e-3). The 0.0016 effect is in the noise band. We can't honestly say whitening "doesn't help" without multi-seed verification. We can also not say it "does hurt." There's a writeup at `docs/notes/polar_product/whitening_ablation.md` that may need to be re-read with this caveat in mind — it was drafted earlier in the session before the user pushed back on the strength of the claims.

### Picard cross-coupling — well-supported "doesn't help" finding

k=1 ≈ k=3 (Δ ≤ 0.0001 at r=16). The diagnostics give a clean mechanism:

- `cross_norm_A_rel` (magnitude of cross-coupling correction relative to u_A): 3% → 9% over training.
- `cross_cos_A` (cosine of cross-coupling with descent direction): −0.10 to −0.14 throughout, consistently.

Small AND consistently in the wrong direction. Additional Picard iters inject perpendicular-with-slight-anti-bias nudge that nets to ~0 effect on eval. This is the most well-supported "negative" finding in the session.

Open question: at r=64 with cross-coupling growing to 10%, does this still hold? r=64 endpoints in flight. So far k=1 0.5221 vs k=3 0.5209 — k=3 is 0.0012 better, ~2σ at proxy. So r=64 may flip slightly, but jobs aren't done.

### Variant 1 (direction-aware ρ) — well-supported "does help" finding

The headline win: −0.0052 vs AdamW at r=16, −0.0030 vs canonical chord-tight at r=16. Mechanism is clean:

- `lambda_dir_gain` = λ_dir / ρ_chord-tight grows 1.21 → 1.41 monotonically over training.
- This is the ratio of variant 1's allowed step magnitude to the worst-case chord-tight bound. Canonical chord-tight leaves 21–41% of step magnitude on the table.

Variant 1's formula: solve `aλ + bλ² = lr` for the direction-aware radius, with `a = σ_max(B·P) + σ_max(Q·A)`, `b = σ_max(Q·P)`, where P = D_A/‖D_A‖₂, Q = D_B/‖D_B‖₂. Provably tighter than chord-tight ρ.

## The "§8 rescale at end" algorithmic thread — UNRESOLVED, defer

Long back-and-forth in this session. User's clarification (the message that finally landed):

> "we just pretend it's saturated, same logic as before. we just solve the problem properly with some τ_A, τ_B, then we pretend τ_A, τ_B is whatever is required to get the rescaled version at the end, and that every internal clip is just polar. does this make sense?"

Interpretation: at every Picard iter, use the polar map for direction. Magnitude is `-τ_A · D_A_n` with τ_A held constant (don't normalize by ‖D_A‖_op each iter). After k iters, apply the §8 chord-tight rescale ONCE: `dA_final = -ρ · D_A_k / ‖D_A_k‖_op`. The cross-coupling at iter n+1 uses `ΔA_n = -τ_A · D_A_n` (the un-rescaled saturating-regime form).

Natural choice for τ_A = ρ. The user's "pretend τ_A is whatever is required at the end" means: it doesn't matter exactly, since the §8 rescale fixes the magnitude post-hoc.

**Current state of `spectral_chord_tight_outer` in `optim.py` (around line 3370): WRONG.** I implemented it with Frobenius rescale inside the loop (the user explicitly said no Frobenius rescale, no dropping polar). The variant slug `adam-polar-product-lora-coupled-spectral-chord-tight-outer` is registered but the math is wrong; do not run sweeps against it.

The user said "i think this might be more subtle than i realized, let's defer for a bit" before context-rotting set in. So this thread is paused, not closed.

**Why this matters:** if the user's intuition is right (rescale at end → Picard cross-coupling has a meaningfully different effect because the in-loop magnitudes aren't pinned), it could explain why Picard "doesn't matter" with the current pinned-every-iter scheme. The "Picard doesn't help" finding might be a feature of pinning, not Picard.
