\title{PoLoRA from a per-factor gradient-covariance metric\\[2pt]
\large (derivation draft --- for discussion, not for \texttt{main.tex})}
\author{}
\date{}

\maketitle

This note derives the PoLoRA update in LoRA factor coordinates $(A,B)$ from a
\emph{linearized trust-region} step on the observed per-example gradients---the
object PoLoRA actually estimates. The chain is
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  &\text{mean per-example linearized loss change}
   +\text{RMS per-example penalty}\\
  &\qquad\to\;\text{empirical Fisher}
   \;\to\;\text{Kronecker fit}
   \;\to\;\text{LoRA pullback}
   \;\to\;\text{spectral polar step}.
  \end{aligned}
\]
Predictive KL enters only as an interpretation (\S\ref{sec:interp},
Appendix~\ref{app:kl}): the second-moment metric here is the empirical Fisher,
which equals the predictive-KL (true Fisher) curvature only for model-sampled
score gradients. The full-inverse (average) counterpart of the step---ordinary
metric preconditioning---is in Appendix~\ref{app:frob}.

\section{Setup: the LoRA tangent split}
\label{sec:setup}

A LoRA layer adds the rank-$r$ adapter
$W=BA\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ to a frozen weight, with
$A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and
$B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. One optimizer step increments the factors by
$\Delta A\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ and
$\Delta B\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r}$. The exact adapter displacement is
$\Delta W_{\mathrm{exact}}=B\,\Delta A+\Delta B\,A+\Delta B\,\Delta A$; the
derivation works in the first-order tangent model
\begin{equation}
  T:=B\,\Delta A+\Delta B\,A,
  \label{eq:tangent}
\end{equation}
discarding the second-order product term $\Delta B\,\Delta A$.
Let $G=\nabla_W\mathcal L\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be the
gradient with respect to $W$. The first-order loss change splits across the two
factors,
\begin{equation}
  \langle G,T\rangle
  =\langle B^\top G,\Delta A\rangle
  +\langle G A^\top,\Delta B\rangle ,
  \qquad
  G_A:=B^\top G\in\R^{r\times d_{\mathrm{in}}},\quad
  G_B:=G A^\top\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times r} .
  \label{eq:split-linear}
\end{equation}
The derivation uses raw gradients; the optimizer replaces $G_A,G_B$ by
bias-corrected momentum buffers before the polar map.

\section{A per-example linearized trust-region step}
\label{sec:metric}

Let $G_i\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be the dense adapter
gradient on example or token $i$, and for a dense update $U$ write the first-order
change in example $i$'s loss as
\begin{equation}
  \delta_i(U):=\langle G_i,U\rangle .
  \label{eq:delta}
\end{equation}
The per-example gradients average to the batch gradient of \S\ref{sec:setup},
$G=\E_i[G_i]$, so the mean first-order change is
$\E_i[\delta_i(U)]=\langle G,U\rangle$. PoLoRA's update solves the variational
problem
\begin{equation}
  \min_U\;\E_i[\delta_i(U)]+\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]
  \label{eq:variational}
\end{equation}
---decrease the average loss, but penalize the RMS size of the per-example
linearized effects (equivalently, minimize $\E_i[\delta_i(U)]$ subject to a fixed
bound on $\E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]$).

The penalty is the second moment of the per-example linearized changes,
\begin{equation}
  \E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]
  =\E_i\!\big[\langle G_i,U\rangle^2\big]
  =\vecop(U)^\top \Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}\,\vecop(U),
  \qquad
  \Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}:=\E_i\!\big[\vecop(G_i)\vecop(G_i)^\top\big],
  \label{eq:ef-penalty}
\end{equation}
so the metric is the empirical Fisher---not a curvature proxy, but exactly the
quadratic form that penalizes the RMS first-order effect of $U$ across examples.
(Newton--Muon instead Taylor-expands the task loss to the Hessian.)

Writing $u=\vecop(U)$, the penalty is a squared whitened length,
$\E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]=\norm{\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}^{1/2}u}_2^2$. A worst case taken
\emph{here}, over parameter-space probes $\xi$, is vacuous---
$\sup_{\norm{\xi}_2\le1}(\xi^\top\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}^{1/2}u)^2
=\norm{\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}^{1/2}u}_2^2$ returns the same length---so a matrix
spectral norm can appear only after the metric is reshaped (\S\ref{sec:worst-input}).

PoLoRA never forms $\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}$; it fits the covariance by a
diagonal--Kronecker matrix
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}\approx Q\otimes P,
  \label{eq:PQ-approx}
\end{equation}
with $P\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}\times d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ the output-side factor and
$Q\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ the input-side factor, both
symmetric positive definite (estimator in \S\ref{sec:est}). Then
$\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}^{1/2}\approx Q^{1/2}\otimes P^{1/2}$, so the whitened vector
reshapes into a whitened matrix,
\begin{equation}
  \E_i[\delta_i(U)^2]
  \approx\norm{(Q^{1/2}\otimes P^{1/2})\,u}_2^2
  =\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_F^2
  =:\norm{Y}_F^2,
  \qquad Y:=P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}.
  \label{eq:ef-frob}
\end{equation}
The half-power factors $P^{1/2},Q^{1/2}$ are the square root of the
gradient-covariance metric.

\section{Pullback to the LoRA factors}
\label{sec:pullback}

For LoRA the dense update is the tangent $T=B\Delta A+\Delta B A$
\eqref{eq:tangent}, so the variational problem \eqref{eq:variational} becomes,
using the linear split \eqref{eq:split-linear},
\begin{equation}
  \min_{\Delta A,\Delta B}\;
  \langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle+\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  +\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\E_i[\delta_i(T)^2].
  \label{eq:variational-lora}
\end{equation}
Under the Kronecker fit the penalty is
$\E_i[\delta_i(T)^2]\approx\norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_F^2$, which
carries a cross term between the two factors. PoLoRA makes a block-diagonal factor
approximation, dropping it:
\[
  \norm{P^{1/2}(B\Delta A+\Delta B A)Q^{1/2}}_F^2
  \approx
  \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F^2
  +\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,A\,Q^{1/2}}_F^2 .
\]
Each block reduces by a trace identity:
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}B\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F^2
  =\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_F^2,
  \qquad C_A:=B^\top P B\in\R^{r\times r},
  \label{eq:A-average-fit}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,A\,Q^{1/2}}_F^2
  =\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_F^2,
  \qquad C_B:=AQA^\top\in\R^{r\times r}
  \label{eq:B-average-fit}
\end{equation}
(e.g.\ $\tr(PB\Delta A\,Q\,\Delta A^\top B^\top)=\tr(C_A\Delta A\,Q\,\Delta
A^\top)$). Here $C_A,C_B$ are the sensitivity metrics induced on the LoRA factor
directions by the per-example gradient metric; equivalently $Q\otimes C_A$ and
$C_B\otimes P$ are the Kronecker fits to the per-factor gradient covariances
$\E_i[\vecop(G_{A,i})\vecop(G_{A,i})^\top]$ and
$\E_i[\vecop(G_{B,i})\vecop(G_{B,i})^\top]$.

\section{Worst-input (spectral) replacement}
\label{sec:worst-input}

The per-factor penalties \eqref{eq:A-average-fit}--\eqref{eq:B-average-fit} are
squared whitened matrices $\norm{Y}_F^2$---with $Y=C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}$ for
the $A$ factor and $Y=P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}$ for the $B$ factor, the dense
case $Y=P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}$ of \eqref{eq:ef-frob} being identical. For any such
whitened matrix the Frobenius norm is the \emph{average} squared response over
unit input probes,
\begin{equation}
  \norm{Y}_F^2=\tr(Y^\top Y)\;\propto\;\E_{\norm{x}_2=1}\norm{Yx}_2^2 ,
  \label{eq:probe}
\end{equation}
the constant ($d_{\mathrm{in}}$) folding into the damping. As a further design
choice, PoLoRA replaces the average probe by the \emph{worst} input probe,
\begin{equation}
  \E_{\norm{x}_2=1}\norm{Yx}_2^2
  \;\leadsto\;
  \sup_{\norm{x}_2\le1}\norm{Yx}_2^2=\norm{Y}_2^2 ,
  \label{eq:RA}
\end{equation}
turning each Frobenius penalty into the spectral
$\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2^2$ and $\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_2^2$.
The block-diagonal split made the two factors independent, so each carries its own
multiplier $\lambda_A,\lambda_B$ (fixed by the radius calibration of
\S\ref{sec:radius}). The two per-factor subproblems are then
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal J_A(\Delta A)
  =\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle
  +\frac{\lambda_A}{2}\norm{C_A^{1/2}\Delta A\,Q^{1/2}}_2^2,
  \label{eq:A-block}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal J_B(\Delta B)
  =\langle G_B,\Delta B\rangle
  +\frac{\lambda_B}{2}\norm{P^{1/2}\Delta B\,C_B^{1/2}}_2^2 .
  \label{eq:B-block}
\end{equation}

\section{Solving by whitening and polar projection}
\label{sec:solve}

\begin{proposition}[Per-factor proximal solutions]\label{prop:channels}
Assume $P,Q,C_A,C_B$ positive definite, and set
\[
  H_A:=C_A^{-1/2}G_AQ^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  H_B:=P^{-1/2}G_BC_B^{-1/2}.
\]
If $H_A\ne0$ and $H_B\ne0$, canonical minimizers of
\eqref{eq:A-block}--\eqref{eq:B-block} are
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A(\lambda_A)
  =-\frac{\norm{H_A}_*}{\lambda_A}\,C_A^{-1/2}\polar(H_A)Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  \Delta B(\lambda_B)
  =-\frac{\norm{H_B}_*}{\lambda_B}\,P^{-1/2}\polar(H_B)C_B^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:A-solution}
\end{equation}
If a whitened gradient is zero, the corresponding zero update is a minimizer.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $Y_A=C_A^{1/2}\Delta A Q^{1/2}$,
$\langle G_A,\Delta A\rangle=\langle H_A,Y_A\rangle$ and the quadratic term is
$\frac{\lambda_A}{2}\norm{Y_A}_2^2$. By the scaled conjugacy between squared
operator norm and squared nuclear norm, the minimum value is
$-\norm{H_A}_*^2/(2\lambda_A)$, attained (since $\polar(H_A)\in\partial
\norm{H_A}_*$) at $Y_A=-(\norm{H_A}_*/\lambda_A)\polar(H_A)$. Undoing the change
of variables gives \eqref{eq:A-solution}; the $\Delta B$ case is identical with
$Y_B=P^{1/2}\Delta B C_B^{1/2}$.
\end{proof}

With the Frobenius norm in place of the spectral norm, the same subproblems yield
the full-inverse steps $-\lambda_A^{-1}C_A^{-1}G_AQ^{-1}$ and
$-\lambda_B^{-1}P^{-1}G_BC_B^{-1}$ instead; the operator norm is the sole source
of the polar map (Appendix~\ref{app:frob}). Define the unscaled update directions
\begin{equation}
  W_A:=C_A^{-1/2}\polar(H_A)Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  W_B:=P^{-1/2}\polar(H_B)C_B^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:WA}
\end{equation}

\section{Damping calibration from the product radius}
\label{sec:radius}

The solutions \eqref{eq:A-solution} have operator norms
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\Delta A(\lambda_A)}_2
  =\frac{\norm{H_A}_*}{\lambda_A}\norm{W_A}_2,
  \qquad
  \norm{\Delta B(\lambda_B)}_2
  =\frac{\norm{H_B}_*}{\lambda_B}\norm{W_B}_2 ,
  \label{eq:block-norms}
\end{equation}
so $\lambda_A,\lambda_B$ control only the scalar lengths along the polar
directions. The product-radius budget alone does not fix the two lengths: if
$\norm{\Delta A}_2=\rho_A$ and $\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho_B$, then
$\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2\le\norm{B}_2\rho_A+\norm{A}_2\rho_B$, and many
$(\rho_A,\rho_B)$ meet a given budget. PoLoRA removes this freedom by the
no-preference symmetry between the two factor coordinates---exchanging the blocks
should not change the radius allocation---which forces the common radius
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\Delta A}_2=\norm{\Delta B}_2=\rho .
  \label{eq:equal-radius}
\end{equation}
The tangent update then obeys
$\norm{B\Delta A+\Delta B A}_2\le\rho(\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2)$; setting this equal
to the budget $\eta$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \rho=\frac{\eta}{\norm{A}_2+\norm{B}_2},
  \qquad
  \lambda_A=\frac{\norm{H_A}_*\norm{W_A}_2}{\rho},
  \qquad
  \lambda_B=\frac{\norm{H_B}_*\norm{W_B}_2}{\rho}.
  \label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
Substituting into \eqref{eq:A-solution} gives the PoLoRA step
\begin{equation}
  \Delta A=-\rho\,\frac{W_A}{\norm{W_A}_2},
  \qquad
  \Delta B=-\rho\,\frac{W_B}{\norm{W_B}_2}
  \label{eq:final-step}
\end{equation}
for nonzero $W_A,W_B$.

\section{Estimating the diagonal metric}
\label{sec:est}

It remains to estimate $P,Q$ in \eqref{eq:PQ-approx}. PoLoRA does so by a coupled
moment closure, with all expectations over the per-example gradient distribution.
The only algebraic consequences needed from \eqref{eq:PQ-approx} are the moment
identities, for compatible $M,N$,
\begin{equation}
  \E[G M G^\top]=\tr(MQ)P,
  \qquad
  \E[G^\top N G]=\tr(NP)Q .
  \label{eq:moment-identities}
\end{equation}
The factor gradients are linear images of $G$, so their second moments carry the
same $r\times r$ matrices $C_A,C_B$ that appear in the update:
$\E[\vecop(G_A)\vecop(G_A)^\top]\approx Q\otimes C_A$ and
$\E[\vecop(G_B)\vecop(G_B)^\top]\approx C_B\otimes P$.

\begin{lemma}[Moment closure under a Kronecker gradient-covariance model]
\label{lem:estimator}
If $\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}\approx Q\otimes P$ with $C_A=B^\top P B$ and
$C_B=AQA^\top$, then
\begin{equation}
  \E\!\left[\tfrac1r G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A\right]=Q,
  \qquad
  \E\!\left[\tfrac1r G_B C_B^{-1}G_B^\top\right]=P .
  \label{eq:unbiased}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the second identity of \eqref{eq:moment-identities} with $N=B C_A^{-1}B^\top$:
$G^\top N G=G_A^\top C_A^{-1}G_A$ and
$\tr(NP)=\tr(C_A^{-1}B^\top P B)=\tr(I_r)=r$, giving the first identity. The
second follows with $M=A^\top C_B^{-1}A$.
\end{proof}

PoLoRA keeps only the diagonals and updates them by EMA:
\begin{equation}
  q_j\leftarrow
  \mathrm{EMA}\!\left[\tfrac1r (G_A)_{:,j}^\top C_A^{-1}(G_A)_{:,j}\right],
  \qquad
  p_i\leftarrow
  \mathrm{EMA}\!\left[\tfrac1r (G_B)_{i,:}C_B^{-1}(G_B)_{i,:}^\top\right].
  \label{eq:ema}
\end{equation}
Because $C_A$ depends on the current $P$ and $C_B$ on the current $Q$, the two
estimates are coupled; the optimizer takes one EMA alternation per step, then
freezes $P,Q$ while computing \eqref{eq:WA}--\eqref{eq:final-step}. In
implementation the inverse square roots are damped, so the formulas are read as
the ideal SPD case.

\section{Interpretation: predictive KL}
\label{sec:interp}

The metric \eqref{eq:ef-penalty} is the empirical Fisher, justified directly as a
gradient-covariance penalty---no curvature claim is needed. It connects to
predictive KL as follows. If $G_i$ were a model-sampled score gradient rather
than an observed-label gradient, then $\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}$ would equal the true
Fisher metric, which is the second-order curvature of predictive KL
(Appendix~\ref{app:kl}). In ordinary supervised training $G_i$ is the
observed-label gradient, so the maintained metric is the empirical Fisher: a PSD
surrogate with the same algebraic form, not the true KL Hessian (Kunstner et
al., 2019).

\paragraph{Summary.}
The empirical gradient covariance \eqref{eq:ef-penalty} is fit by $Q\otimes P$,
pulled back to the LoRA factors as $C_A=B^\top PB$, $C_B=AQA^\top$, and made
worst-input by the spectral norm; the per-factor proximal solutions are the polar
sandwiches \eqref{eq:WA}, and the common product radius \eqref{eq:rho} fixes the
scalar lengths, giving the implemented step \eqref{eq:final-step}.

\section{Predictive-KL interpretation of the metric}
\label{app:kl}

This appendix records the predictive-KL reading of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}$ and
connects it to the KL metric $\Sigma_{\mathrm{KL}}$. Let
$z_i\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$ be the layer input for token $i$ and
$y_i=Wz_i\in\R^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ the output. Let $p_i(\cdot;y)$ be the model's
predictive distribution at output $y$, and for an output perturbation $v_i$,
$D_i(v_i):=\mathrm{KL}(p_i(\cdot;y_i)\,\|\,p_i(\cdot;y_i+v_i))$.

\begin{lemma}[Predictive-KL expansion]\label{lem:local-kl}
Let
\[
  s_i(a):=\nabla_y\log p_i(a;y_i),
  \qquad
  F_i:=\E_{a\sim p_i(\cdot;y_i)}[s_i(a)s_i(a)^\top].
\]
Then $D_i(v_i)=\tfrac12 v_i^\top F_i v_i+o(\norm{v_i}_2^2)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first derivative at $v_i=0$ is
$-\E_{a\sim p_i(\cdot;y_i)}[\nabla_y\log p_i(a;y_i)]=0$ by the score identity; the
second is $-\E_{a\sim p_i(\cdot;y_i)}[\nabla_y^2\log p_i(a;y_i)]=F_i$ by the
Fisher identity. Taylor expansion gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[When Fisher equals Hessian]\label{lem:fisher-hessian}
If $\ell_i(y)=-\log p_i(a;y)$ and the label $a$ is averaged under the current
model $p_i(\cdot;y_i)$, then
$\E_{a\sim p_i(\cdot;y_i)}[\nabla_y^2\ell_i(y_i)]=F_i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\E[\nabla_y^2\ell_i(y_i)]=-\E[\nabla_y^2\log p_i(a;y_i)]
=\E[s_i(a)s_i(a)^\top]$, the same Fisher identity as
Lemma~\ref{lem:local-kl}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Connecting $\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}$ and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{KL}}$.}
Assembling the per-token KL metrics gives
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_{\mathrm{KL}}=\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N (z_iz_i^\top)\otimes F_i .
  \label{eq:param-kl}
\end{equation}
For a likelihood loss the per-example gradient is the score outer product
$G_i=-s_i(a)\,z_i^\top$, so $\vecop(G_i)=-(z_i\otimes s_i(a))$ and
\[
  \E_{a\sim p_i(\cdot;y_i)}\!\big[\vecop(G_i)\vecop(G_i)^\top\big]
  =(z_iz_i^\top)\otimes F_i .
\]
Hence under \emph{model-sampled} labels $\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}=\Sigma_{\mathrm{KL}}$
exactly, and the present derivation is a KL-proximal one. Under \emph{observed}
labels $\Sigma_{\mathrm{EF}}$ is the empirical Fisher, generally
$\neq\Sigma_{\mathrm{KL}}$; the two agree only near a realizable optimum (Kunstner
et al., 2019).

\section{Average response: full inverse versus polar}
\label{app:frob}

This appendix solves the average-response (Frobenius) problem and contrasts it
with the worst-input solution \eqref{eq:A-solution}, isolating the operator norm
as the source of the polar map. Take a single dense variable $U$ with metric
$F:=Q\otimes P$ (the per-factor case is identical with $(P,Q)\mapsto(C_A,Q)$ and
$(P,C_B)$).

\begin{lemma}[Average-response solution]\label{lem:frob}
The minimizer of
$\mathcal J_{\mathrm{avg}}(U)=\langle G,U\rangle
+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_F^2$ is
\begin{equation}
  U_{\mathrm{avg}}=-\frac1\lambda P^{-1}GQ^{-1},
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  \vecop(U_{\mathrm{avg}})=-\frac1\lambda F^{-1}\vecop(G):
  \label{eq:frob}
\end{equation}
the full-inverse metric-preconditioned step (natural gradient under the
Appendix~\ref{app:kl} interpretation).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\norm{P^{1/2}UQ^{1/2}}_F^2=\tr(PUQU^\top)$, so
$\nabla_U\mathcal J_{\mathrm{avg}}(U)=G+\lambda PUQ$; the objective is strictly
convex and the gradient vanishes at $U=-\tfrac1\lambda P^{-1}GQ^{-1}$. The
vectorized form uses
$\vecop(P^{-1}GQ^{-1})=(Q^{-1}\otimes P^{-1})\vecop(G)=F^{-1}\vecop(G)$.
\end{proof}

The worst-input minimizer is instead, with $H=P^{-1/2}GQ^{-1/2}$,
\[
  U_\star=-\frac{\norm{H}_*}{\lambda}\,P^{-1/2}\polar(H)Q^{-1/2}.
\]

\begin{remark}[Where the half-power lives]
Writing both with the same outer factors,
\[
  U_{\mathrm{avg}}\propto P^{-1/2}\,H\,Q^{-1/2},
  \qquad
  U_\star\propto \norm{H}_*\;P^{-1/2}\,\polar(H)\,Q^{-1/2},
\]
the only difference is $H$ versus its polar $\polar(H)$.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Source.} The polar map comes entirely from the operator norm. With
        the Frobenius norm the middle factor is $H$ itself, the inner and outer
        $P^{-1/2},Q^{-1/2}$ recombine into the full inverse $P^{-1},Q^{-1}$, and
        no polar appears.
  \item \emph{Effect.} $\polar(H)$ has unit singular values, so it discards the
        magnitude of the inner whitening: across directions the step is
        reweighted by the half power $P^{-1/2},Q^{-1/2}$ rather than the full
        inverse. The overall step size is unchanged---the global factor
        $\norm{H}_*$, itself a half-power-scale quantity, restores the $F^{-1}$
        aggregate scaling. So the worst-input step is $F^{-1}$ in overall
        magnitude and $F^{-1/2}$ in the \emph{relative} weighting of directions;
        it is never a clean $F^{-1/2}G$.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}
