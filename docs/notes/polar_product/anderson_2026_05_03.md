# Anderson acceleration on the Picard inner loop (2026-05-03)

Follow-up to `closeout_2026_05_02.md` priority #1: Anderson Type-II mixing
inside the polar-product Picard fixed-point iteration. Implementation +
unit tests + r=16/r=64 sweeps.

**Headline:** Anderson works as designed at r=16 (k=4/m=4 recovers k=16
quality), but it's a wrong-target optimization. The actual best unified
default turned out to be **plain Picard k=3** — exploits the damped
oscillation phase to land between iter-2 overshoot and the worse fixed
point. New default: `picard_iters=3` for `adam-polar-product-lora-coupled`.
Anderson stays in the codebase as opt-in for users who want strict
fixed-point convergence at lower wall cost than k=16.

Reference spec: `proposal.md`. Reference oscillation diagnostic
(osc_cos ≈ −0.85 at r=16) motivating Anderson: `closeout_2026_05_02.md`.

All numbers single-seed, 2k-step horizon, OLMo-2-0425-1B + Magicoder-OSS-Instruct-75K,
optimizer `adam-polar-product-lora-coupled`, lr=3e-4, lora_plus_multiplier=1.

## Implementation

Type-II Anderson with a rolling history of size `m+1`, applied AFTER the
polar pipeline + end-RMS-align inside each Picard iteration. Solve

$$\gamma^* = \arg\min_\gamma \|r_k - \Delta R\, \gamma\|^2 + \lambda \|\gamma\|^2$$

where $r_k = g_k - x_k$ is the current Picard residual and $\Delta R$
stacks differences of past residuals. Mixed iterate $g_k - \Delta G\,\gamma^*$
replaces the next $(dA, dB)$.

- New optimizer args: `anderson_m` (history depth; 0 disables), `anderson_reg`
  (Tikhonov; default 1e-10).
- New CLI flags: `--anderson_m`, `--anderson_reg` in `train_lora.py`.
- Bookkeeping: FIFO eviction; $m{=}0$ is bit-identical to plain Picard.
- Code: `lora_playground/optim.py` `AdamPolarProductLoRA.step`.
- Tests: `tests/test_polar_product.py` (5 tests, pass) — `m=0`
  determinism, `picard_iters=1` no-op, ≥2× residual reduction at $k{=}8$
  on a slow-contraction fixture, finite/changes-params smoke,
  end-RMS-align compatibility.

Cells: `picard_iters_override ∈ {2,4,8,16} × anderson_m ∈ {0,4}` (8 cells).

|---|---|

The converged-quality cluster (k≥4 with Anderson, or k≥8 plain) is
within ±0.0002 at every shared step. Anderson at $m{=}4$ closes the
~0.0010 gap between plain $k{=}4$ and $k{=}16$.

### Wall-cost picture

Anderson per-iter overhead (LSQ Gram solve over $m+1$ flattened
$(dA,dB)$ vectors): ~3–7% wall per Picard iter at $m{=}4$. Net:

- Plain $k{=}8$ (0.7583, 3197 tps) is already statistically indistinguishable
  from plain $k{=}16$ (0.7582, 2422 tps) at the canonical horizon — the
  closeout doc's noise floor at r=16 is σ=0.0011.
- Anderson at $k{=}4, m{=}4$ recovers that quality (0.7584) at 3558 tps —
  **~11% faster than plain $k{=}8$** at matched eval.
- Anderson at $k{=}8$ doesn't pay: overhead exceeds gain because plain
  $k{=}8$ is already saturated.
- Anderson at $k{=}2$ doesn't help: history depth too short for the LSQ
  to fit ($m_{\text{use}}=1$ at iter 2; identical to plain Picard within
  rounding).

## Reframing relative to the closeout doc

The closeout (2026-05-02) speculated **3–5× effective iteration reduction**
based on the osc_cos≈−0.85 diagnostic. Empirically: the polar Picard
saturates much faster than the oscillation cosine suggested — by $k{=}8$,
the eval is already at the $k{=}16$ fixed point. So Anderson's regime of
usefulness compresses to one operating-point shift: $k{=}8 \to k{=}4$.

Wall cost of practical shipping:

- Closeout's "picard k=16" recommendation: 2.6× plain $k{=}1$.
- New: **picard $k{=}4$ + Anderson $m{=}4$ ≈ 1.77× plain $k{=}1$** wall,
  matched eval to k=16 within noise.

This validates Anderson as a concrete cost reduction but does not change
the qualitative shipping picture — the r=16 vs r=64 same-rule problem
identified in the closeout is unaffected (Anderson converges to the same
Picard fixed point; cf. closeout §"NOT a r=16 fix").

## The k=3 finding (the actual answer)

The closeout's `picard_iters_sweep_2x2` group already had plain k=3 at
r=16 = **0.7557** — better than every Anderson candidate tested in this
investigation, and 0.0027 below k=4/m=4 (≈2.5σ above noise). The
closeout's interpretation: damped oscillation lets k=3 land between the
k=2 overshoot and the (worse) fixed point.

The unified-default question hinged on whether plain k=3 also converges
at r=64. Sweep `picard_k3_r64` (job 6328100):

| variant | r=16 | r=64 |
|---|---|---|
| **plain k=3** (new default) | **0.7557** | **0.7364** |
| k=4/m=4 | 0.7584 | ~0.7374 (extrapolated from cancelled sweep) |
| plain k=4 | 0.7594 | 0.7374 |
| plain k=16 (closeout shipping) | 0.7582 | 0.7369 |
| AdamW baseline | 0.7579 | — |

Plain k=3 wins or ties at both ranks vs every Picard variant tested,
no Anderson code path needed. **Default flipped: `adam-polar-product-lora-coupled`
factory now uses `picard_iters=3`** (was 2).

## Open questions / next

- **Anderson stays as opt-in.** Useful when a user wants strict
  fixed-point convergence (e.g. for theoretical comparisons) at lower
  wall cost than k=16. The k=4/m=4 result (0.7584 ≈ k=16's 0.7582) is
  a real 1.47× speedup over k=16, just not the right shipping target.
- **k=3 fragility check** (deferred): the lucky-phase mechanism is not
  principled — different lr / model / dataset could shift the
  oscillation phase and put k=3 on the wrong side. Multi-seed at k=3
  (and ideally k=2,3,4 grid) would tell us how robust the k=3 lift is.
- **Heavy-ball averaging** (closeout priority #2) becomes uninteresting
  unless k=3 turns out fragile and we want a more principled fix.
